All right. We are at the end of the **maitreyī**—sorry, the **madhubrāhmaṇa**, 15th **mantra**. And **bhāṣyakāra** has said everything he wants to say. And he is now—we saw the example to say everything is one. The example given was a wheel with, you know, a hub and a rim and spokes in between. And the example is that the spokes have no being apart from the rim and the hub. I mean, they basically are totally one with it. That's the idea being conveyed.

But for somebody who doesn't understand what the **śruti** is saying, because we saw towards the end of the last class, the **śruti** has to present two things. You have to be able to understand the one reality while living in this world where the experience is duality. The experience is always I, and there is what I experience. This is everybody's universal experience. How do I reconcile this experience, which is every moment, with the statement of the **śruti** that there is one non-dual? This is a difficult part of this. And the **śruti** has both kinds of statements. So on one hand it will say, you know, everything is this. On the other hand it will say there is nothing but this. What? How do you say both things? This is a problem, seeming problem.

This is why I said these sentences are what appear like paradoxes to us, because you cannot make peace in your head with two ideas being talked about, and being talked about by the same. It's not that one you made and one—then it's easy to resolve. Two things are being said. Both have—both of them are... Which one is correct? I told you last time, which one is correct? Everything is in me. Everything is not in me. Tell me which one is correct. No, no, both are correct. If both are correct, now I have to somehow make both correct. I am very clear I am different from everything else now. So if **śruti** is saying this, there must be some process by which I will become everything. Therefore **upāsanā** makes so much sense now. By doing **upāsanā**, I will become that. By knowing something, just knowing, I won't become. I will do something after it. What did this **brahman** know by which he became everything? This was 1.4.10 opening. But the feeling is that, no, it's not true. I mean, it's not that you are always that **brahman**. You have to become that.

See, that life of becoming is so engraved in our mind that I want to become **brahman**. I don't want to see I am **brahman**, honestly. I want to become **brahman**. This is a very, very strong sentiment. And this sentiment is when you believe in what you see in your mind, and you also want to have **śraddhā** in **śāstra**. You have **śraddhā** in **śāstra**. How to reconcile the two? When you don't understand **śāstra** as **pramāṇam**, then you try to reconcile the two based on your own **buddhi**. And when you try to reconcile with your **buddhi**, you will come up with some method to reconcile it, some framework. That framework explains all **darśana**s. It's your own framework.

So now the context is what? There is this example of the wheel with the rim and the spokes. And the statement was... This statement was made. So, on all these **upaniṣad**s, ... this vision: what? The total is also real. Individual is also equally real. **samaṣṭi** is also real. **vyaṣṭi** is also real. And when **vyaṣṭi** doesn't know, it is **saṃsārī**. When it knows—will it know? It first knows, then it will do **upāsanā**. And with **upāsanā** it will become **brahman**. Then everything is cleared up. This is thinking.

So he says, why not that way? Now all statements become very real. **karma** is real, **karma** is real. So why don't we go with this approach? It's less hurtful. No part of the **śruti** is given stepmotherly treatment. Everything is good simultaneously. We don't say **karmakāṇḍa** is useless. We say **karmakāṇḍa** is—there is some truth to it, but it is not an independent reality. If you want to know what is absolute independent reality, there, there is no **karma**. There's no **kāṇḍa**. It's all about reality. Rather than go with your approach, which seems to be hurting in some way, let's do one thing. I will explain. See, the whole **śruti** you want to explain, right? I'll explain it, and I'll explain in such a way that both are good. And how are both going to be good? All these are real. You have a new reality. You become something different. That is the right way to look at it. This is his vision. Why? Because he is a person who has studied the **śāstra**, and who has **śraddhā** in the **śāstra**, and wants to understand it without conflict, without really understanding what the **śāstra** is saying. This is the situation.

So **bhāṣyakāra**, without talking specifically of **bhartṛprapañcādi**, has started now a discussion of saying—he is bemoaning the fact that there is **sopādhika**, there is **nirupādhika**, and without understanding these two, people make all kinds of statements. That's the context. Okay, let's go to the **bhāṣyakāra**. Okay, so we saw—yeah, so we saw these last time. Who are the **brahmavit**? What did he say? I was **manu**, **sūryaśca**. I was the sun. I am the sun. I was when it came also. This **sarvātmabhāva** has been explained in detail from 1.4.10 till now. It's been done.
