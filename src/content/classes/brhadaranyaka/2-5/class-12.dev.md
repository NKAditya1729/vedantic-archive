ॐ श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणानाम् आलयं करुणालयम्। नमामि भगवत्पादं शङ्करं लोकशङ्करम्॥ शङ्करं शङ्कराचार्यं केशवं बादरायणम्। सूत्रभाष्यकृतौ वन्दे भगवन्तौ पुनः पुनः॥ ईश्वरो गुरुरात्मेति मूर्तिभेदविभागिने। व्योमवद्व्याप्तदेहाय दक्षिणामूर्तये नमः॥ ॐ सह नाववतु। सह नौ भुनक्तु। सह वीर्यं करवावहै। तेजस्विनावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥ ॐ नमो ब्रह्मादिभ्यो ब्रह्मविद्यासंप्रदायकर्तृभ्यो वंशऋषिभ्यो नमो गुरुभ्यः॥

So we are at the final mantra of the **Madhu Brāhmaṇa**, and this is going with that story of the **Aśvinī** **Kumāras** having gone to **Dadhyaṅ** **Maharṣi**, and what he taught them. So we saw: the first two verses, 16 and 17, were more about the story, and 18 and 19 are giving you the essence of the whole **शास्त्र**. We saw this last time. **इदं वै तन्मधु दध्यङ्ङाथर्वणोऽश्विभ्यामुवाच । तदेतदृषिः पश्यन्नवोचत् ।** That part is the story, referring to the story; no new thing there. The teaching is where: **पुरश्चक्रे द्विपदः पुरश्चक्रे चतुष्पदः । पुरः स पक्षी भूत्वा पुरः पुरुष आविशदिति । स वा अयं पुरुषः सर्वासु पूर्षु पुरिशयो नैनेन किञ्चनानावृतं नैनेन किञ्चनासंवृतम् ॥**

So the whole **जगत्** is really one reality, only one. Yet we find the subject–object difference; we find sentient–insentient **भेद**. How did this come about? When I say "how did this come about", it's like saying, "How did the snake enter the room?" Conclusion is: already there is a snake there; there is no doubt about that. Snake is there. "Now my only question is, how did the snake come in?" If I tell you there is no snake, you will say, "Go away, I don't want to talk to you. I am not asking you, is there a snake? Okay, snake is there. Now you tell me, how did it come?" No. "I don't care, you know, what else you think. Why did you do this to me?" "I didn't do this to you." "Why did you do this to me?" That's the way the **बुद्धि** works.

So then, okay, you want to hear a story? We will tell you one: **पुरश्चक्रे द्विपदः**. See, these are all cities. Each body is a city, a city with several gates. And therefore, see, these old kingdoms would have a fortress, would have a boundary, and they would have a few gates to the city. So how many gates? They would count how many gates are there, and those gates are protected. So in the same way, this is a city, and you interact with the outer world through the gateways. And the gateways are, you know, the eyes, the ears, the nose, etcetera. So what did Brahman do? He wanted to come, you know. So first he created the cities. Then, having created cities, what did he do? What cities did he create? **पुरश्चक्रे द्विपदः**: two-legged, you know, bodies — humans and birds, etcetera. Then what? **पुरश्चक्रे चतुष्पदः**. Then he created four-legged creatures. This is **उपलक्षण** for all creatures.

Then, after that, what did he do? **पुरः** is after that. **पुरः स पक्षी भूत्वा पुरः पुरुष आविशदिति**. Too many **पुरः**, okay? So, **पुरः**: **पुरस्तात्**; **स ईश्वरः**; **पक्षी भूत्वा**: became a bird. What bird? No bird, okay. You have to remember the **Taittirīya** **पक्षी**, because at every level it's called a bird. So this. And bird, because a bird lives in a nest and flies away from a nest; it makes another nest. It's a bird; it needs a perch; it needs a place. So which is this bird? This is a city which has to — which has to perch. Who is the bird? **सूक्ष्मशरीर**. **सूक्ष्मशरीर** is bird. So that is the **पक्षी भूत्वा**.

And what did he do after that? You know: **लिङ्गशरीरं भूत्वा**, **पुरुषः आविशत्**. **पुरः**, **पुरः आविशत्**. **पुरः** again, what? Now, the body. It entered. Right? Therefore, who is present in every body? This fellow who entered. One fellow entered all the bodies. You know, **क्षेत्रज्ञं चापि मां विद्धि सर्वक्षेत्रेषु भारत**. **न तु एकक्षेत्रे**. Know me to be the one who is the knower of this body in every body. So he entered.

So now, therefore, since he entered all the bodies, you know: **सर्वासु पूर्षु पुरिशयः**. He is the one who is present. **शयः**: sleeping; available; doesn't move; he is there always. Where is he? In every body. Therefore we call him what? **पुरुषः**, because he is there in every body. In every city he is there. Who is he? The city dweller. **गिरिधर नागर**. **नगरनिवासी**: **नागरः**. Sophisticated, you know. **Kṛṣṇa** — he doesn't live in a village; he is not a country bumpkin; he is **नागरः**. That's how **Mīrā** calls him: **गिरिधर नागर**. So this is a city. So the indweller in every city is him. So since he is present in each of the cities, he is called **पुरुषः**.

Okay. But then he is restricted? He is restricted to each city then? Each fellow is different? No, it's not true. Even though he may be — one, he is divided? He is not divided. How do you say that? **न एनेन किञ्चन अनावृतम्**, **न एनेन किञ्चन असंवृतम्**. So this is telling us the second part: **पूर्णत्वात् पुरुषः**. So you have to remember both these. **पुरिशयनात् पुरुषः**: **जीवः**. Who is in the **पुरी**, in each body? **जीवः**. And that same **पुरुष** is what? **सर्वं पूरयति**: he fills everything up; he fills everything up. Therefore, how do you say that? **न एनेन किञ्चन अनावृतम्**: nothing is left uncovered by him. Nothing is outside of him; nothing is inside of him; there is only him.

Therefore, what do you get with this now? **पूरयति इति पुरुषः**; **पूर्णत्वात् पुरुषः**. So when you say **पूर्णमदः पूर्णमिदम्**, that **पूर्णः** normally we will take it as **कर्मणि** **त प्रत्यय**. Here we take it as **कर्तरि**, because if he is filled up by something, who is that who fills him up? That will be the question. We don't want that problem. Therefore what? The one who fills up everything; one who pervades everything. Pervades everything — is there a thing? Yeah, as though there is one more thing, because **पुरश्चक्रे**.

You know, even if you look at the **Taittirīya** model — going by the **कोश**s, I am saying — first, **अन्नमय**. Then what? **तेनैष पूर्णः**. That word is **पूर्णः**, right? **अन्योऽन्तर आत्मा प्राणमयः । तेनैष पूर्णः ।** **अन्योऽन्तर आत्मा मनोमयः । तेनैष पूर्णः ।** **अन्योऽन्तर आत्मा विज्ञानमयः । तेनैष पूर्णः ।** Means what? What does it mean, **तेनैष पूर्णः**? **तेन**: by the inner, next level; **एषः**: this outer level; **पूर्णः**. By the **प्राणमय**, the **अन्नमय** is **पूर्णः**. How **पूर्णः**? Every inch of **अन्नमय** is pervaded by **प्राणमय**. "Every inch" is a way of saying, okay: every cell is alive in **अन्नमय** because of the **प्राणमय**. He has entered; he is filled up completely. And then this **प्राणमय** is filled up by whom? **मनोमय**. **मनोमय** is filled up by whom? **विज्ञानमय**. **विज्ञानमय** is filled up by whom? **आनन्दमय**. Who filled **आनन्दमय**? **आनन्दो ब्रह्मेति व्यजानात्**. **आनन्दः**. Correct?

So that same idea here also, but without giving too many levels; just saying, you know, **सर्वासु पूर्षु**. So each one — **अन्नमयशरीरः**, **प्राणमयशरीरः** — therefore each one is a **पुर** in its own way: **सर्वासु पूर्षु**. I am just giving a slightly different way of looking at it, okay: all bodies, and in a body, at every level, he is there. Since you look at him through one body, you call him **जीवः**: **पुरिशयः**, **जीवः**. And now you want to know that that same reality is the **पूर्णः**, one who fills up everything. Therefore, you know: **न एनेन किञ्चनासंवृतम्**. Nothing is left out. So, **अन्तरननुप्रवेशितम्**: not entered. **बाह्यभूतेनान्तर्भूतेन च न अनावृतम्**: no outside, inside. They both are one. **स बाह्याभ्यन्तरो ह्यजः**. He is **बाह्यः**; he is **अभ्यन्तरः**. With both, it's him. It is **आधिभौतिक**, **आधिदैविक**, **आध्यात्मिक**: all three.

See, look at it in any **शास्त्र**: the teaching is not complete till you cover every single thing. Nothing other than this. If there is one more that stands apart — **द्वितीयाद्वै भयं भवति**. There will be two. What is the problem with two? Only problem is we will remain **संसारी**s. That's the only problem; everything else is okay, no problem. So: **एवं स एव नामरूपात्मना अन्तर्बहिर्भावेन कार्यकरणरूपेण व्यवस्थितः ; पुरश्चक्रे इत्यादिमन्त्रः सङ्क्षेपत आत्मैकत्वमाचष्ट इत्यर्थः ॥** So it tells you everything is only one. So you see, this is the **वार्तिक**.

**वार्तिकम्**

**पुरश्चक्रे शरीराणि द्विपदः पक्षिमानुषान् ।।**

Okay? Why? Because **द्विपदः**. Then:

**चतुष्पदोऽथ पशवः पुरश्चक्रे**

How? **स्वमायया**. Right? And then, how did he enter?

**पुरो नवशरीराणि प्राविशज्जलचन्द्रवत् ।।**

Like the reflections enter: the moon enters, in the form of reflections, into the water bodies. Like that he entered. Means there is no entry. The moon is always there. When the water body appears, he appears to have entered. There is no real entry. Okay.

**पुरुषोऽयं भवेदात्मा यतः शेते स पूरुषु ।।**

Why do you call him **पुरुषः**? Because he is sleeping — he is found in the bodies. Therefore he is there in every body. Then what? If you say now:

**परिच्छेदप्रसङ्गश्चेत्**

That means he is limited to the bodies; in between bodies he is not there. No. If you say that:

**नैनेनानावृतं किंचिन्नाव्याप्तं**

Right? So what does it mean? **ओतः तन्तुवत्**. **तन्तुवत्**: like the — when you weave a fabric, the long and the, you know, the width, both you have to weave together. And so everything is totally the thread alone. So it pervades like the thread pervades a cloth. So therefore, what does he mean here?

**पूर्णत्वात्पुरुषः सोऽयं ब्रह्मैकं पुरुषस्ततः ।।**

So that is the idea. All right. So this very quotation, **पुरुषः पुरुषः** — Swamiji used to love saying this, **पुरुषः पुरुषः**, **महावाक्य**. So now I can see where it comes from, very nicely, in one place. Okay.

So then we had seen this last time — the mantra we had looked at, 19. So, **रूपं रूपं प्रतिरूपो बभूव**: he became every single form. For what? **तदस्य रूपं प्रतिचक्षणाय**: to show the glory of Brahman, as though. And then what? How did he become all these? **इन्द्रो मायाभिः पुरुरूप ईयते**. **इन्द्रः**: **परमेश्वरः**. By his **माया**, you know — **माया** means by tricks, kind of; we will look at that in the **भाष्य**. So he became all these: **मायाभिः बहुरूपम् ईयते**. Then, how did he become many? You know, he had all these senses, which are like horses that took him all over the place. In every body there are different senses; therefore there are hundreds and thousands of senses, so to say. Or, you know, I am a **शतावधानी**, I am a **घनपाठी**: all this is **अभिमान**, okay. I am an **अधिकारी** for **कर्म**: **अभिमान**. It's **अभिमान** all the way. Whole life is **अभिमान**.

And that **अभिमान**, because it is **मिथ्याभिमान**, which means it doesn't have **प्रतिष्ठा** — it's not absolutely real. Any **अभिमान** we have, based on anything, can be taken away. Any **अभिमान** can be taken away. Because I think I am a great, you know, musician — my voice can be taken away; I may not have it tomorrow because I had bad food. So the point is, it's all **मिथ्याभिमान**. How do you know it's **मिथ्या**? Because it's subject to going. It wasn't there, and it's subject to going. So, because you are talking about yourself, you know you were without it; you can be without it. Therefore this whole thing is what? **नामरूपभूतकृतमिथ्याभिमानैः**. **नामरूप** includes not only what you objectify with your mind and senses; your mind and senses are also **नामरूप**; **अहङ्कार** is also **नामरूप**. In all these I have **अभिमान**. And therefore, **मायाभिः** it is done.

**प्रज्ञाभिः**, **न तु परमार्थतः** — not in absolute reality. **पुरुरूपः**: **पुरु** means **बहु**; therefore **बहुरूपः**. **ईयते**, **गम्यते**: it becomes, as though.

**एकरूप एव प्रज्ञानघनः सन्** — **Śaṅkarācārya** doesn't leave that opportunity: don't think it all is really happening. Already he said **मिथ्याभिमानैः**; still he says **एकरूप एव**. Yes: **एकः रूपः यस्य**, or **एकः स्वरूपः**. This who is just one, non-dual, **प्रज्ञानघनः**, who is nothing but **चैतन्यम्**, **सच्चिदानन्दः**.

**अविद्याप्रज्ञाभिः** — **प्रज्ञाभिः**, you know: **अविद्याप्रज्ञाभिः**. Now, instead of just **मायाभिः**, here he said **प्रज्ञाभिः**; now he says **अविद्याप्रज्ञाभिः**. They are not real **प्रज्ञा**, okay; it's all **अविद्या**. What is **अविद्या**? **अविद्या** is not knowing the truth of myself. Therefore what I know is wrong. And that fellow who doesn't know himself is the knower; he is the **प्रमाता**. I am a knower: **अविद्या**. Therefore everything you know will be what? If the base fellow is a pretender, everything he will know will be what? Pretension only, okay. Whole thing is this. Therefore, **अविद्याप्रज्ञाभिः**: with that alone he knows everything.

"Swamiji, then even **ब्रह्मविद्या** is known by **अविद्याप्रज्ञा**; therefore that will also go away?" "Swamiji, if all things are **मिथ्या**, how from **मिथ्या** can you know **सत्यम्**?" From **मिथ्या** you don't come to know **सत्यम्** that way. When one **मिथ्या** knocks out another **मिथ्या**, **सत्यम्** remains. **ज्ञानम्** is also **मिथ्याभूतम्**; **अज्ञानम्** is also **मिथ्याभूतम्**. This **ज्ञान** knocks out **अज्ञान**, and what remains? Remains what always was, without this projection of **अज्ञान** and **ज्ञान**: that reality I am. Why? How do I come to know? Because it is **स्वतःसिद्ध**; it is self-evident. Okay. So he says, **अविद्याप्रज्ञाभिः**.

**कस्मात्पुनः कारणात् ?** Now, **कस्मात्पुनः कारणात्** — so you have to connect to the next word now: **युक्ता ह्यस्य हरयः**. He has to connect it. So now, **कस्मात्पुनः कारणात्**: for what reason, again? So he is saying:

**युक्ताः रथ इव वाजिनः, स्वविषयप्रकाशनाय, हि यस्मात् , अस्य हरयः** — **युक्ताः**: they are yoked. **युक्ताः**: like what? **रथे इव वाजिनः**. Watch the **रथे**, okay? Don't make it **रथः इव**. **रथे**: to the chariot. What is yoked? **वाजिनः**: horses. So, **वाजिनः**: **स्वविषयप्रकाशनाय**: to show all the forms, **विषय**s. **स्वविषयप्रकाशनाय**. **यस्मात्**, **अस्य हरयः**. So now, **अस्य**: who is this? **इन्द्रः**. See, **इन्द्रः पुरुरूपः अभवत्**. How he became? For what reason he became? Because he has got all these horses yoked to him. Whose horses? **अस्य हरयः**: this **परमेश्वर**'s horses.

**हरयः**. What is this **हरयः**? What are the horses here? **हरयः** — horse, I gave the translation in English, okay. But what is **हरयः**? Now let me make it proper. **हरयः** means what? Those which carry you away, take you away. Because you need to go places. If you need to go places, what do you need? You need something which will take you places. What will take you places? **हरणात् हरयः**. Just see: I could have said **गमयति**; that's not the word. **हरयति**. **हरण** means what? Takes you away — away. **हरति**, **हरण**. So what takes you away from yourself? **इन्द्रियाणि**. Your senses — mind and senses — take us away from ourselves.

If I want to — if I want to know myself, I should not be trying to know anything, in a way, okay. I am saying it so you understand there is an importance to being without trying to do — having studied the **शास्त्र**, not before. Without **शास्त्र** it makes no sense; it's just empty quietness. With **शास्त्र**, it's not silence — not just silence. See, the point is this. Suppose I have no thoughts: is that freedom? Is that **मोक्ष**? No thoughts. Let's say you have no thoughts at all: is that **मोक्ष**? That no-thought — can it be permanent? How much ever you try, can it be permanent? A thought will arise. Therefore that no-thought is a **कारणावस्था**. The no-thought zone, like in sleep, is a **कारणावस्था**, **बीजावस्था**. That is what is called **कारणशरीर**. Don't think you have to sleep — no, **कारणशरीर**. What is **कारण**? **कारण** for **कार्य**. What is **कार्य**? All your **वृत्ति**s are **कार्य**s. The whole world is **कार्य**, known by **वृत्ति**s, whether it's dream or waking.

Therefore, not having a **वृत्ति**, not having a thought, not having anything going out: are you free? You are free — temporarily. But if you have a thought in the background, "I should not look out", that's already **संसार**, even in that moment. If you want to get rid of **संसार**, if you want to avoid **संसार**, you are a **संसारी** already. Naturally you are free from **संसार**; that is a different matter. It's stress, you know, because the next thought is — a thought is a nightmare now, because thought will take you away. **हरयति**, **हरति**: **हरयः** will come. "You don't want; I don't want." That "don't want" itself is a thought, you know. "I don't want a thought" is a thought. So how are you going to deal with this problem? Except by seeing the **मिथ्यात्व** of the thought.

You can sit for all that you want in a quiet place and sit quietly. For ten hours you will sit; ten hours and one minute, you will get up. **व्युत्थानं भवत्येव**. Unless, in those ten hours, it helps you solidify your understanding of the **मिथ्यात्वम्** of what comes and goes — then it's good. But if it's just a matter of being away from all distraction for a while, you will love to be that way, and you would always want to be in a quiet place. That is fine. That will happen if your **प्रारब्ध** allows it. Otherwise you will be in the middle of **Deepāvali**, in a place where they are bursting crackers all around. That's what will end up happening, okay. **प्रारब्ध**: you don't have a say over whether you will be in a quiet place or in a very noisy place. No way. So freedom should not depend on that situation.

And I am saying to you, therefore: see, when you look at a pot and clay, there is **कारण**, there is **कार्य**, okay: there is **कार्यकारणम्**; there are only two levels, okay — there is **स्थूल**, there is **सूक्ष्म**. But when it comes to the human being's state, there is a separate **बीजावस्था**, because you experience this every day, wherein you are free from **संसार**. Every day we experience it in sleep, minimally. And actually, every time you smile you experience it, but it doesn't last; it's immediately replaced by a thought. Therefore a thought-free state is not **मोक्ष**. A thought takes you away. Therefore what's the immediate solution? "Arrest all the thoughts. Kill them. Destroy the mind." They will say this: **मनोनाश**, **वासनानाश**. How are you going to destroy the mind? With what are you going to destroy the mind?

Therefore we have to understand that these **वृत्तयः**, **हरयः**, are also not separate from the **वस्तु**. They, as though, take you away. **ध्यायतीव लेलायतीव**: **Bṛhadāraṇyaka** talks about it elsewhere. So when you say, you know, "I meditated very well; my mind really stayed, Swamiji. Very nice; meditation was very nice." What is that? **ध्यायतीव**. You said "I meditated"; you didn't meditate. Mind was quiet, okay; you are the same. Another day: "Swamiji, today, nothing; meditation was useless; mind was going all over the place." **लेलायतीव**. **आत्मा ध्यायतीव**, **आत्मा लेलायतीव**: **आत्मा** as though gets resolved in **ध्यानम्**; as though is distracted in **ध्यानम्**. Really what? **आत्मा न तु ध्यायति**, **न तु लेलायति**. It doesn't focus; it doesn't do **ध्यानम्**; it doesn't get distracted. And what is **आत्मा**? It is the truth of both, and free from both.

What disturbs? Thoughts disturb? Thoughts can't disturb. Thoughts don't disturb unless I let them disturb. Why do I let them disturb? Because of **अज्ञानम्**: because I take the thought as also something other than me. Therefore thought becomes my nightmare, because I have bought the idea that thoughts are the problem. **पराञ्चि खानि व्यतृणत्स्वयम्भूः**: these outward-turned senses, they take me away. Therefore, **तस्मात्पराङ् पश्यति नान्तरात्मन्**: therefore you don't see yourself; you see only the outside world. **कश्चिद्धीरः**: one **विवेकी**. **प्रत्यगात्मानमैक्षत्**. How? **आवृत्तचक्षुः**: he turned his eyes around. **अमृतत्वमिच्छन्**: **मुमुक्षु**. Therefore this thinking can be there. Now what is **श्रुति** saying? **श्रुति** is saying, look: all these senses are also not separate from Brahman, okay? That's the idea.

So he says here, **इन्द्रः परमेश्वरः मायाभिः प्रज्ञाभिः** — oh, we finished that; sorry. **हरयः**. **अस्य हरयः हरणात् इन्द्रियाणि**. How many of them? How many **इन्द्रिय**s are there?

**शता शतानि, दश च** — hundreds, and ten. And ten means what? It's like saying, you know, **अत्यतिष्ठद्दशाङ्गुलम्**. In the **पुरुषसूक्त**, you know, he stands taller by ten fingers. A **कारण** stands taller than the **कार्य** by ten fingers means what? A **कारण** pervades the **कार्य**, and has more space for more **कार्य**s, because there is no **कार्य** really other than the **कारण**. So here also he is saying what? **शता दश च**. Okay? Which means what? **अनन्त**. Really, hundred, ten, etcetera: **अनन्तवाच्य**. It's not — it's like, you know, **सहस्रशीर्षा पुरुषः**. Means what? **भगवान्** has one thousand faces? One thousand and one **नहीं चलेगा**? Not true. One thousand doesn't mean one thousand; one thousand means infinite. Okay? So here also, that's the way it is. So why are you saying so many, **दश च**?

**प्राणिभेदबाहुल्यात् शतानि दश च भवन्ति** — **प्राणिभेदबाहुल्यात्**. What? I and you smell; we smell some things, but we are not able to find the fellow who broke into our house. We need one special creature which can smell better than me. Dog squad will come. Then that fellow will smell and will take you where — where. Airport: you go, one dog is waiting to smell your suitcase, to see if you are carrying something which human beings can't figure out. It can figure, inside your box, there is heroin inside. How? So a dog's capacity to smell is different. A dolphin's capacity to hear is different. A bat's capacity to perceive sound, ultrasound, is different. Right? A capacity of a tiger or lion, nocturnal animal, to see in darkness, infrared vision, is different. So the world — you imagine this world view of a snake. You imagine the world view of a cockroach. You imagine the world view of an ant. Is it your world view? It's impossible. Imagine the world view of a rat, the world view of an eagle. Each world view is different, okay? How? Because their **इन्द्रिय**s are different. Therefore he is saying that, because of **प्राणिभेद**, you know, **शतानि दश च भवन्ति**.

**तस्मात् इन्द्रियविषयबाहुल्यात् तत्प्रकाशनायैव च युक्तानि तानि** — because of the variety of objects in the world, and each object requires a different sense perception. So they say, if an earthquake is about to happen, certain animals can sense it, which means they have some **इन्द्रिय** through which they can sense whatever is the **सूक्ष्म** appearance of the earthquake; its precursors are seen by it. So how did it know? Because that's the capacity it has. So, **इन्द्रियविषयबाहुल्यात्**: because there is a huge variety of things to be known, not only by human beings, but by whole — whatever is in the **जगत्**. **बाहुल्यात्**, **तत्प्रकाशनायैव**: to bring to light all these different things in the world, because each is a glory of **भगवान्**. **तत्प्रकाशनायैव च युक्तानि तानि**.

**न आत्मप्रकाशनाय** — so all the senses are illumining those, not you. No sense, no mind can illumine who you are, okay? And therefore what he says — he quotes:

**‘पराञ्चि खानि व्यतृणत्स्वयम्भूः’(क. उ. २ । १ । १)** **Kaṭhopaniṣad**. **पराञ्चि** means turned outwards. These **गोलक**s, **इन्द्रिय**s, are all turned outwards. They are not turned inside; they are turned outwards. That's why God destroyed us, by putting all our senses turned outward, so you are always looking out like this. Ears listen to sound outside. Suppose your ears would hear sounds inside, what will happen? You will hear some gastric sounds, or what? That doesn't make sense, okay. It's just a way of saying that, since your mind is engaged and captured by the world outside, there is no attention to yourself. That's all it means. So you have to be — that's why **शमदम**, wherein you are able to pull your senses where you want, use them where you want. It should only be a **करण**. Now the **करण** has become, as though, the **कर्ता**; it is driven by the **इन्द्रिय**s. That is when it becomes really **हरयः**: it takes you away from yourself. So, **इति हि काठके ।**

**तस्मात् तैरेव विषयस्वरूपैरीयते, न प्रज्ञानघनैकरसेन स्वरूपेण** — so: **युक्ता ह्यस्य हरयः शता दश**. One second. **इन्द्रो मायाभिः बहुरूपम् ईयते**: that's the sentence, okay? **बहुरूपम्**. Where is that **ईयते**? Here's the **ईयते**. See the **ईयते** here? That **ईयते** is bringing back here: **तस्मात् तैरेव विषयस्वरूपैरीयते**. So when you say **परमेश्वर** — what does **इन्द्रो मायाभिः बहुरूपम् ईयते** mean? He takes on many forms, **बहुरूपम्**; he becomes many forms. So now **भाष्यकार** is saying: he doesn't become many forms as the **चैतन्यम्**, as the **सत्तामात्रम्**. **सच्चिदानन्द** did not undergo any change to become these many forms, this manifold world. Then why do you say it became manifold? From the standpoint of the **विषय**s and the **इन्द्रिय**s; not from the standpoint of **चैतन्यम्**, the consciousness which is you.

Therefore, **तस्मात् तैरेव**. What is **तैरेव**? These **इन्द्रियैः**, **हरयः**: on account of them. **तैरेव विषयस्वरूपैरीयते**. That Brahman alone has become both the **विषय** and the **विषयी**, really. Because of those alone we say he became **बहुरूपः**; not really. **न प्रज्ञानघनैकरसेन स्वरूपेण**: not from the context of the **प्रज्ञानघन**. What is **घनम्**? There is no other thing; there is only **चैतन्य**. **स्वरूपेण**.

**एवं तर्हि अन्यः परमेश्वरः अन्ये हरय** — See the thing now here. He says what? **युक्ता ह्यस्य हरयः**. What is **अस्य**? **परमेश्वरस्य**: **परमेश्वरस्य हरयः**. Suppose I say you are **गोमान्**. Means you are a person with cows, you are a person owning a lot of cows. Now, those cows and you: is there a relationship or not? Are you the cow? Suppose you are a person with horses: **हरयः**, horses. Suppose you say, you know, "I have five horses: eyes, nose, all these are my horses; I have five horses." Am I the horse? No. "Therefore the **इन्द्रिय**s are separate; I am separate." Why? Because the language is **अस्य हरयः**.

Any language you say, you will use some word, and that word will indicate **द्वैतम्** only. That's the nature of the word, because the language is born of **अविद्या**. **प्रमाता** is born of **अविद्या**. Knower means **अविद्या**; I told you. If you say "I am a knower": **अविद्या**. **प्रमाता** is **अविद्या**, **प्रमाणम्** is **अविद्या**, **प्रमेय** is **अविद्या**. What is not **अविद्या** then? All this — only when you know you can call it **अविद्या**; until then it's **विद्या**. Therefore, in that context a **पूर्वपक्ष** is being raised: if you are saying **युक्ताः अस्य हरयः**, then **एवं तर्हि** — if that is so — then **अन्यः परमेश्वरः**: **इन्द्र**, **परमेश्वर**, is different; **अन्ये हरयः**: and the senses are different. **इत्येवं प्राप्ते उच्यते**.

Now what do we want to show? Though the sentence is **युक्ताः अस्य हरयः**, that **अस्य** is not a real **अस्य**. It is what? The **षष्ठी** is not **सम्बन्ध**; it's **राहोः शिरः इव**. What is **राहोः शिरः इव**? What is **राहु**? What is **केतु**? See, there was a demon. It was one demon which was head and tail. Now, what did **Viṣṇu** do? He cut that head off, so it doesn't get **अमृत**. Okay, now one fellow is split into two: one is head, one is tail. Now you cannot say "**Rāhu**'s head", because **Rāhu** is the head. But how do you refer to him? How do you refer to the head? You say "**Rāhu**'s head". That is called **राहोः शिरः इव**, which means what? It is purely words. Doesn't mean any **सम्बन्ध**. There is no real **सम्बन्ध**, because there is only one thing. Same way here also, what? It is not **सम्बन्ध**. Therefore, how does the **श्रुति** say that?

**अयं वै हरयोऽयं वै दश च सहस्राणि बहूनि चानन्तानि च** — so **अयम्** is what? **अयम्**: **परमेश्वरः**. **अयं वै हरयः**: this **परमेश्वर** alone is in the form of mind and senses. **अयं वै दश च सहस्राणि बहूनि चानन्तानि च**: the infinite number of forms of senses. Compound eyes, simple eyes. You know, a plant has a tendril. You look at a creeper: it will throw out one tendril. What will it do? It will look for where I can find a support to wrap around. How does it know? **हरयति**: takes. Therefore in this form — there are infinite forms of senses, depending on which **जीवराशि** you are looking at. All of them are what? **अयं वै**: this **परमेश्वर** alone is in the form of all the **इन्द्रिय**s.

**प्राणिभेदस्य आनन्त्यात्** — because of the manyness of the variety of the **प्राणि**s, of the living creatures.
