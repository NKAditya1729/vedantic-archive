ॐ श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणानाम् आलयं करुणालयम्। नमामि भगवत्पादं शङ्करं लोकशङ्करम्॥ शङ्करं शङ्कराचार्यं केशवं बादरायणम्। सूत्रभाष्यकृतौ वन्दे भगवन्तौ पुनः पुनः॥ ईश्वरो गुरुरात्मेति मूर्तिभेदविभागिने। व्योमवद्व्याप्तदेहाय दक्षिणामूर्तये नमः॥ ॐ सह नाववतु। सह नौ भुनक्तु। सह वीर्यं करवावहै। तेजस्विनावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥ ॐ नमो ब्रह्मादिभ्यो ब्रह्मविद्यासंप्रदायकर्तृभ्यो वंशऋषिभ्यो नमो गुरुभ्यः॥

So, we were looking at the last mantra in the **Madhu Brāhmaṇa**. We'll go back to the text. So, **रूपं रूपं प्रतिरूपो बभूव तदस्य रूपं प्रतिचक्षणाय**: to reveal its glory, its potential. So, to reveal its glory, etcetera — you know, to whom? The question was, you know, to whom will it reveal its glory? That's the way it is. I mean, it's all the way of presenting it.

But the fact is that we have a life, and we see a lot of things happening; we experience so many things. And if one is awakened to it, one is surprised as to how everything is, you know. So a Zen monk was apparently asked, you know, that he sees some miracles. "So what miracles have you seen?" He says, "You know, I eat food: it's a miracle." So you look at it: you know, I walk, I talk, the sun rises, the sun sets every day, the seasons go around. It's a miracle. It is a miracle, in a way, because what puts all these in motion? And there is only one, and it appears as a seer, as a seen, as a thinker, thought. What more miracle do you want? But that fellow said, "That's not — I am expecting a miracle. This is not a miracle." Hey, it is a miracle.

So if you look at it that way — I am saying, if you have that — because we take so many things for granted, you know. As you grow older, you realise what you took for granted when you were younger, but it is too late to go back in time. You wasted so much of your time and energy doing things which were unproductive. You fought causes which were not worth fighting for, and you didn't fight causes which are worth fighting for. I mean, whatever. All I am saying is: you only in hindsight, you know, what perhaps could have been done differently. And that is also not necessarily right, that you should have done it differently, because if you did it differently, you wouldn't arrive at where you are today. So everything in life has, you know, some purpose which we don't understand. We don't understand why, because there is only one thing that could be understood. That's the way. So I am just saying, understand: **प्रतिचक्षणाय** is to be understood that way. **इन्द्रो मायाभिः पुरुरूप ईयते**.

**इन्द्रः परमेश्वरः मायाभिः प्रज्ञाभिः नामरूपभूतकृतमिथ्याभिमानैर्वा न तु परमार्थतः** — not. It's not really there are many. It is not really **महिमा**, or **महिमा** — a separate fellow who doesn't have **महिमा**, etcetera. All of this is just an appearance. So how does he go about doing this?

So he says: suppose, you know, you imagine you have a lot to show. Like, modern day, I would say, you know, you have a city or a national park, or you have something which wants to showcase itself. How would it showcase itself? There are different aspects to it. So you may have a situation where you have a mountain and ocean both together; in California it is like that. So then you have to showcase both. So you might need to do different things. One of the hiking trips will be there; then, you know, a deep-diving trip perhaps. I mean, whatever it takes, okay? You have whale watching, dolphin watching; there are so many things going on. Now each of them requires a different way of going about it, because, same way, you can't experience both. So this world is so manifold.

It says, you know, it's like the — there are horses yoked to chariots. And what is the purpose of these horses and chariots? They are meant to take the person in the chariot all over the place, wherever he wants to go. It's a good tourism, you know: **देखो देखो**. See all the things you want to see; you watch them all. So how do you do that? So there must be **करण**s to do it. And therefore, **हरयः**: **युक्ताः हरयः**.

**युक्ताः रथ इव वाजिनः** — **रथे इव वाजिनः**. Not **रथः इव**: **रथे इव वाजिनः**. Like to a chariot you have yoked horses. And what are they meant for? So, **विषयप्रकाशने**. And they will take the person who is, you know, sitting. So even nowadays still there is a fancy for a horse carriage. You just don't go in a car; you sit in a horse carriage and you see the city. So the horses take you around; they take you all around. So what is the chariot here? **शरीरमेव रथः**. **इन्द्रियाणि हयाः**. The **इन्द्रिय**s are the **हय**s. For whom are they meant? For the **रथिन्**, the person who is sitting in the chariot: the **रथस्वामी** — not even **रथिन्**, **रथस्वामी**. And you may have a charioteer, or a driver also, who knows the place well, how to lead you there. Nowadays you have what? You have a driverless car. A driver is there; all the AI is sitting in there. Some driver only is driving the driverless car.

So — but the fact is, all this is meant for whom? For the rider. Who is that rider? So you enjoy your ride. Then who made you enjoy the ride? That is **इन्द्रो मायाभिः पुरुरूपम् ईयते**. You imagine, if you lost one sense organ, what would your world view be? No eyes: same world continues; what is your world view? Lost your smell; lost your taste. Each one will strip off one **महिमा** of life, one glory. So that — all that. And why do we have so many names and forms? Because there are so many sights to be seen, and for each of them you need different instruments. Therefore, in each living being the **इन्द्रिय**s are of a different kind, and there are so many different kinds of **इन्द्रिय**s, you know, as many as the living beings themselves. And they all have their own **विषय**s, you know. On account of this you have, you know, so many. Therefore he says, **अस्य हरयः शता**, **दश च**: 100 plus 10 plus 1000, etcetera. All these are meant for what? **अनन्तानि**.

"Oh, so they are all different from me?" No. **अयं वै हरयः**: these horses are indeed the same reality alone. So there is no real difference.

**प्राणिभेदस्य आनन्त्यात्** — **प्राणिभेदानन्त्यात्**: this should have been a compound. **प्राणिभेदानन्त्यात्**. Okay.

**किं बहुना ?** What more can we say? **तदेतद्ब्रह्म** — this **ब्रह्म** is what? **य आत्मा**: it is me. What is it? **अपूर्वम्**. **अपूर्वम्**.

**नास्य कारणं पूर्वं विद्यत इति अपूर्वम्** — **पूर्वम्** is what? **कारणम्**. So sometimes **भाष्यकार** will put the word before. He did not say **पूर्वं कारणम्**; he said **कारणं पूर्वम्**. Both are possible. So for this Brahman there is no predecessor. Okay: this came from that, that came from that; the Brahman came from what? **ऐसा नहीं है**. Brahman didn't come from anything: **अपूर्वम्**. "Oh, Brahman did not come from anything; from that everything came. From Brahman everything came — correct or not? **जगत्कारणं ब्रह्म**: **जगत्** came from Brahman or not?" Look: whether Brahman is born, or something is born of Brahman — if something is really born of Brahman, if there is real birth, then Brahman is changing. If Brahman itself is born, it is in time; it is changing. So, either way, there is no **जाति**. **अजातिः एव सत्यम्**. There is no change; there is no birth. It's all, you know — it is not a real **परिणामि उपादानम्** at all. What is Brahman? **विवर्त उपादानम्**. Without changing, without undergoing any change, it becomes, as though, many. That's why the example is a rope-snake: without being touched, the rope becomes the snake. So these words here are telling us whatever we said earlier, **जगत्कारणं ब्रह्म**, etcetera. Now you understand what it is all about. **अपूर्वम्**: it doesn't have a cause. Then what is next after that? **अनपरम्**. **अपूर्वम्**, **अनपरम्**.

**नास्यापरं कार्यं विद्यत इति अनपरम्** — this should have been in blues. Okay. So, **नास्यापरं कार्यं विद्यते**, which means what? There is no effect. There is no cause for Brahman; there is no effect for Brahman. Which means what? Brahman is Brahman all the time; it never became anything.

**नास्य जात्यन्तरमन्तराले विद्यत इति अनन्तरम्** — one more word. So there is nothing in between also: **अन्तराले मध्ये अपि न विद्यते**. **अनन्तरम्**.

**तथा बहिरस्य न विद्यत इति अबाह्यम्** — **अबाह्यम्**: there is nothing outside it. When you say nothing outside it: nothing which is different from it, which is outside. Nothing — that is, nothing which is independently different. "Oh, I see so many things outside." Yes, they are there. But whatever you see, starting with your body onwards, your mind onwards, your **अहङ्कार** onwards — **अहङ्कारादिदेहान्तम्** — whatever you see, all of them, and the **विषय**s, they all do not have a second being. Then what is being? That being — that being is I, the I. Or take it as Brahman. See, basically the point: I will say "I", but the fact is, we should arrive at it. So I am telling you: Brahman is all these. It is not a **कार्य**; it's not a **कारण**; it's not outside; it's not inside. Then you have to arrive at what — what is the solution to this jigsaw puzzle. They are giving you all the pieces. If I tell you the solution, it is not a solution; the solution, I have to arrive at. And in that arrival there is, you know — he says what? **अबाह्यम्**: it is not outside.

**किं पुनस्तत् निरन्तरं ब्रह्म ?** It is **निरन्तरम्**. Okay. It is **निरन्तरम्**, because it has nothing in between also. There is no gap. It's **घनः**. See, when you say **नास्य जात्यन्तरमन्तराले विद्यते**, there is nothing else. So there is Brahman, then there is some space, then there is some more — so it's split Brahman? No, there is nothing else. Therefore **Śaṅkarācārya** says, **किं पुनस्तत् निरन्तरम्**: there is no interval, there is no gap; there is only one. **किं पुनः**? What is that?

**अयमात्मा** — now **श्रुति** says, that is **अयमात्मा**. Every human being's perception is — every perception is one experience. Every experience is distinct. Life is a series of experiences, a series of knowledge taking place, because each experience is really knowledge. There is no — there is no experience without knowledge. Experience is knowledge of experience. This word "experience" sometimes is a problem, because you say, "You have to experience sugar." Sugar is not knowledge? "Do you know sugar? I will explain to you: white crystals." That is knowledge. Do you understand what sugar is? You don't. So you need to experience sugar. The only way to know sugar, because you are talking of taste, is to taste it. If you are talking of smell — oh, you know, you see a rose. Do you know what a rose means just because it's white? You don't. You have to experience it. Means what? You have to know it. You have to know it in terms of the smell, that's all. You have to know it.

Now, any experience: how do you experience anything? How do you experience anything? Who experiences? You have to say "I experience", because you are the fellow, the experiencer. Who is that you, without whom there is no experience? That you is **अयमात्मा**.

**कोऽसौ ?** Who is this **आत्मा**? **भाष्यकार** is being very kind. **कोऽसौ ?** Who is this **आत्मा**?

**यः प्रत्यगात्मा** — **प्रत्यक्** means what? **आन्तरः**. How **आन्तरः**? **न बाह्यः**. See, **अबाह्यम्**. **अबाह्यम्** means that which is never seen as outside; means that which never becomes **दृष्टिगोचर**; which means that which never comes to be known through a process of knowing; that which never comes to being through a process of becoming. That is **आत्मा**. Everything you experience comes to be experienced, and that experience goes. No experience is forever. Even when you feel it's forever — you are sitting on a plane, and that plane's constant drone is there till you land — but still it is not forever. The fact that you could recognise it as a drone means you have had life without that drone. The fact that I can understand "I want to be different" means I have been different. **संसार** is not possible unless there is already an understanding of freedom. Except that the freedom came in moments, and I associated that freedom with something else.

And now what is being said is: it is **निरन्तरम्**; there is no interval in this. And that **निरन्तरम्** is who? **प्रत्यक्**. **प्रत्यक्** — the translation typically is "inner" or "innermost". That "innermost" takes us to a spatial frame, generally. So you want to now see inside; you go inside, deeper in the heart. Expressions are okay; any expression is all right. But that expression should not mislead, you know; it should not become something spatial. See, that is why there are people who will say **आत्मा** is **अणु**: it's tinier, very tiny, so tiny you will go and find it somewhere inside. Not true. "Inner" means that which never becomes outer: can never be externalised, can never be objectified. That is why it is **सर्वान्तरः**. **निरन्तरः**: it's everywhere. **सर्वान्तरः** — you know, **निरन्तरः**: no gap; **सर्वान्तरः**: it's everywhere. No place where it is not.

So, **अयमात्मा**, **प्रत्यगात्मा**. Who is he? **द्रष्टा**: you are seeing all these places, **देखो देखो**; you are seeing everything; that fellow is you. **श्रोता**: you are hearing these words; that fellow is you. **मन्ता**: the fellow thinking is you. **बोद्धा**: one who knows, one who has woken up, is you. **विज्ञाता**. All of these are you. "Then I am **संसारी**?" No. You are all of these, yet you are free from all of them. That's your **महिमा**. So, **सर्वानुभूः**. **सर्वानुभूः**. What do you mean by **सर्वानुभूः**?

**सर्वात्मना सर्वमनुभवति** — one who — **भाष्यकार**: **सर्वात्मना सर्वमनुभवति**. Being the very being of everything: **सर्वात्मना**. Experiences everything. Being everything: **सत्**. Experiencing everything: **चित्**. There is no **सत्** different from **चित्**: **सदेव चित्**, **चिदेव सत्**. Therefore what are you? **सर्वानुभूः**. You are the experiencer; you are the experienced. You are the thinker; you are the thought; you are the thinking. Seeing: I call myself a seer. Hearing: you just look at it, right? I have been hearing; I am a hearer. Hearing goes: am I gone? I am seeing; sight stops: am I gone? I am tasting something very delicious, ice cream. I am tasting. Ice cream — how long does it stay? That's why ice cream: it melts away. Then what are you now? I was a taster. That's with reference to taste: I was a taster. Without reference to taste, without reference to hearing, without reference to sight, without reference to any movement, any action — but every action you are. Therefore, **सर्वानुभूः**.

You are the one who is the truth of the experience; you are the truth of the experiencer. You are the content of the experiencer — if I say that, you think experience is outside. So again you have to correct that. It is the truth of experiencer and experienced. But experience outside — that's until you locate yourself as the experiencer. You cannot separate an experience from the experiencer or the experienced. There is no hearing unless there is a hearer and a heard; you cannot have hearing without them. One phenomenon alone is divided — divided. And that's how **व्यवहार** is possible: one thing at a time. That's how we are; the bodies, minds are designed that way. And why did he do it that way? So that you can experience the glory of the truth. That's how it's presented.

So, **विज्ञाता सर्वानुभूः**: **सर्वात्मना सर्वमनुभवति**: **सर्वानुभूः**. **और क्या**, what else should I say? **इत्येतदनुशासनम्**. I don't know why, in this particular verse, this time we have so many of things — all these are missing. The **प्रतीक**s are all a little messed up here. Okay. So just keep in mind: I could have used it, but I wasn't using it; I have my document. So it is, you know: **अयमात्मा** — **इत्येतदनुशासनम्**.

**इत्येतदनुशासनम् सर्ववेदान्तोपदेशः** — **इत्येतदनुशासनम्** means what? **सर्ववेदान्तोपदेशः**. It's not — **भाष्यकार** here is saying: it's not just "I am concluding the **Madhu Brāhmaṇa**"; it is not that "I am concluding the second chapter"; it is not "I am concluding the **Madhu Kāṇḍa**", which is the first and second chapters of **Bṛhadāraṇyaka**. It is **सर्ववेदान्तोपदेशः**: all **Vedānta**, only this much they have to say. You go one more step, you can say **सर्ववेदोपदेशः**: nothing more.

**एष सर्ववेदान्तानामुपसंहृतोऽर्थः** — why did you say that, **सर्ववेदान्तोपदेशः**? Because **एष सर्ववेदान्तानामुपसंहृतोऽर्थः**. This is the conclusion; this is what you arrive at through every **वेदान्तवाक्य**. Every **उपनिषद्** has only this one, you know: **तत्तु समन्वयात्**, the fourth **सूत्र**. All of them have **समन्वय** only in this one truth. What is this?

**एतदमृतमभयम्** — **परिसमाप्तश्च शास्त्रार्थः**. This is the one which is timeless; this is the one which frees you from insecurity of any kind, this knowledge. **शास्त्र** is over: **परिसमाप्तः**, **परितः समाप्तः**. There is nothing more to be said; it is done. That's how the **Madhu Kāṇḍa** concludes. Very, very nice. So you can see, finally, **इत्येतदनुशासनम्**. **इत्येतदनुशासनम्** means the idea is, there are no more things to be said, because it is **सर्वान्तरः**, **सर्वानुभूः**, **निरन्तरः**. What else can you talk about?

So — unless I do not understand Brahman — there is no second thing. If I don't understand Brahman, there are many; definitely there are many. It's not one; subject–object will always be there. But if the subject–object oneness is understood, then how do you count a second thing? And there is nothing to be counted, and you understand all that is here is me, without a process of knowing, without a process of becoming. Then what else is to be done? To say, **हाँ**, okay, now what? Means I haven't understood. Correct? All there is: I understand, but there is something which makes me come back to my old ways of thinking again and again. I may need to get to that clarity, because that is why this **संशय** has to go. Doubt has to go. Then the contradictory feeling has to go — has to go, from the perspective of the one who is struggling with it. But for one who has understood, what is there?

"No, Swamiji, but when in the class you tell me, I correct myself; but again, when I go to the world, I get lost. What is this going on? What is all this happening?" You know, that kind of a question can arise. It's not something that can be denied, that a question like that arises. So, you know, somebody had a question. Question is what? You know, it's like: why listening and understanding doesn't persist? You know: "If I listen to the class, then I feel that, you know, I understood. When I go away, again it goes away." It's true; it's how it feels. But examine what went and what came. You are — see, you are that person who says "experience went away" and "experience came", which means you are looking at freedom as something to be experienced for some time. It's an experience; it's still in time. For that to change, I have to see: the experiencer is me. I am not an experiencer. That "I" has to arrive — really arrive — at the one who always is. It's not that I go; it's not that I come; it's not that I sleep; it's not that I wake up. I do all this, but I am unchanging.

So the fact is, anything — any one moment, any one change — I can talk about only because I am there. **अन्वय**: **अन्वय** is **अहम् अस्मि**. **व्यतिरेक** is everything that can go. Everything else goes: **अवस्था**s go, **शरीर**s go. **अहम्** never goes, because to report one thing in the universe I need to be there. So if I am able to appreciate that presence as me, without having to go through a process of thinking — see, if I ask myself "Who am I?", or if I ask you "Who are you?", do you think to say "I am Devadatta"? How long does it take to say "I am Devadatta", **अस्मि**? And how well entrenched is it for you to say it, even if you are woken up in the middle of the night? You will say it. I don't think anybody will say, "Oh, let me think who I am." I don't think it happens. Only in the **Vedānta** class they think, try to think who we are. Otherwise, who I am is very clear to me: Devadatta. With that identity I want to become free. Devadatta can never become free; Devadatta's freedom will only come and go. That Devadatta has to know that: **देवदत्तनामा अहम् अस्मि**. That's just a name given to this expression. But what is only that existence, by which alone everything is coming to light?

I count many because I identify with the mind and body. So the question is, you know: if I don't have this clarity, then what should I do? Then we will say, **निदिध्यासनम्**: **श्रवणं मननं निदिध्यासनम्**. But the fact is, when is **ज्ञानम्** really there? One can say one has **ज्ञानम्** and one is trying to gain **निष्ठा**: **ज्ञाननिष्ठा**. But when there is only one thing, where is **निष्ठा**? But based on the human experience we have all these words, because the teacher also understands, the student also understands, the difficulty. And both have to say: finally there is no **द्वैतम्**, but still we feel that way. So it's a very funny world, you know; it's a very funny business. But if I don't have to become anything different, then **एतावदनुशासनम्**. Because what is — I am okay with the body, with this given mind, with the world, with all its headaches. Nothing needs to change, because I see that all these are my glories. All the movements are my glory. Nothing is really happening. That understanding is what — that is what is called **निश्चयज्ञानम्**.

So that knowledge is such that it is not a knowledge in the normal parlance that we use. It's not a knowledge that can be forgotten. Because suppose you say, "I forgot something" — **मत्तः स्मृतिर्ज्ञानमपोहनं च** — the one who says "I forgot" is you, not what you forgot. The one who says "I have an experience of forgetting" — he is **सर्वानुभूः**. So the experience of forgetting is you; you are the fellow who experienced forgetting. "I forgot, Swamiji; the **Vedānta** words I forgot." You are there. How can you forget? "I remembered." You are there. How can you remember? You are always there. It's not something you can remember or forget. What can be remembered is what can be forgotten. So, until I am thinking there is something I have to remember, it means it is a concept. It is a concept. Any concept I may forget, because anything which is gathered is subject to going. Therefore, thinking that I can forget just shows that I haven't understood, finally. What is subject to being experienced is not me. **सर्वानुभूः**: what is available in every experience. **प्रतिबोधविदितम्**. There is no experience where it is not. Recognising that alone is understanding.

And if we really understand that clearly, then — **किमिच्छन्कस्य कामाय शरीरमनुसंज्वरेत्** — for what reason would we do anything? Everything would be okay; whatever happens will be okay. You still continue to do; you don't need to go into some, you know, state of non-doing. **प्रारब्ध** takes each person's life differently. Somebody may be sitting in **समाधि** for his whole life, and somebody force-feeds him; that's his **प्रारब्ध**. It doesn't necessarily mean that he is more, you know, knowledgeable; it doesn't mean that. So — "Swamiji, there are seven levels." You want to go for seven levels? Go after them; that's your choice; nobody says no. But to think that I should be like a stone is not necessary. That's again a want. "I should go to the **सप्तम**", you know — that seventh level — is **संसार**, is it not? "No, no: only then I won't come back." Who knows whether you will come back, or what will come back? Right now you are not saying you are going, coming. What will come back? If you are not born, where is the question of rebirth? But that level of conviction in my understanding, which is not mistaken, which cannot be improved upon — that is **एतावदनुशासनम्**. There is nothing more to be said.

Now what remains is to remove what prevents me from appreciating this. And what prevents me from appreciating it is my own, you know — of course, **अविद्या**; that's all the problem really is. But what prevents removing **अविद्या** when it's told? I am holding on to some things; I want to hold on; and I hold a certain identity. You know, I think that certain things are fair and unfair, and I am very vocal about it; I can't but do something about it. It's fine; nothing wrong; everything is fine in its own place. But understand that, until "I think this is not right, that is right, this is wrong" softens, we have to continue to struggle. Because when you cannot accept something — you cannot hide in a forest forever, okay? And even if you hide, if you come out of **समाधि**, you will remember all the unfairness of the world. It is not going to go away. This is why it is very important to appreciate that what is, is in order in its own way. I am not saying it is right or it is wrong; please understand that.

Right and wrong really is here, because when everything is Brahman, what is wrong? With respect to **धर्म** we will say — because what will help you more towards this truth is to be kept. So it doesn't mean — you know, we will say, for a person who is a **ज्ञानी**, even if he kills, no **पाप**. What does it mean? He will not kill; that's a fact.

So then, what prevents my owning this is that I still have my agenda for the world. I have an agenda for this body; I have an agenda for the mind. Which means I am still identified with the mind, 100%. It's a quieter mind, perhaps; it's a well-informed mind, correct. But I need to know that I don't call the shots. And even the mind which is quiet for days — one fine day it just goes into riot, and you know that you were okay because you were identified as a quiet mind. Then only you realise that it's a quiet mind which you took as "I am free". Now you are not free; it just took the right kind of disturbance to get you out. So I am saying, I don't have to really do anything, but I have to do something. Why do I have to do something? I do something so that I have dealt with all these difficulties. And disturbances come in life, because you don't know when they will come; they can come any moment. Just because I went to heaven, let us say, and spent, you know, 10 million years there — when I come back, I will again have a problem, right?

So many a time people feel that way, you know: you are living in a very nice, settled place; everything looks good; now you feel "I am totally okay". But some event can shake me up. So, if that event shouldn't shake me up, I should never have that sense of well-being which is dependent upon my mind being calm, or the body being okay. I have to examine what makes me comfortable. Generally, I am saying, even as we grow, generally what makes us comfortable is: the mind is calm; it's not demanding that anything should be different. As soon as a thought comes, "This is not okay; this has to go; this has to change" — then there is no — unless that "it should change, it should go" is optional. So you say, you know, "There should be trees around. There are no trees; everybody is cutting trees." All right, I'll go and plant five trees. But if somebody cuts, they cut; that's how it is. I can only plant. The freedom to plant remains mine, but the freedom to keep every tree I planted is not mine. I plant; but that the plant dies is also not in my hand.

So basically I am saying, all action has to be born of that freedom. If the action is for the sake of making the world a better place, or making myself a better person, then that action is still within the realm of growing, becoming. Nothing wrong with the effort. So then, what is it? The only thing that frees me is the understanding. That is why we say **ज्ञानादेव कैवल्यम्**: no other way. But for that **ज्ञान**, for that **ज्ञाननिष्ठा**, whatever needs to be done to remove the **प्रतिबन्धक** may require some action, because growing into any attitude requires an action. So the more compelled I feel to act, the stronger are my compulsions to act, or compulsions to avoid. That is **रागद्वेष**. I can justify it: "No, no, I cannot but do it." That justification doesn't do me any good, finally. It may be in a good way: "This society is not being fair." Maybe. But if you feel very strongly, it means the society is real, its actions are real, your mind is real, everything is real. In that real world, **संसार** will be real, 100%. So it doesn't mean — if you accept all the inequities in the world — did you create the world? Do you — are you in charge? Try, try taking charge; you will be destroyed one day, too soon. You can't take charge. "But I can make it —" Yeah, you can make it a better place, 100%. You can help a million people. Good, wonderful, do it. But after helping a million people, you will still see 10 million who need to be helped. That won't work.

I am not against social, you know, causes, working for them. Please understand. Anything I say is misinterpreted; that's unfortunate at times, you know. Some things have to be said in a public forum; some things cannot be said. That needs to be appreciated. But from the person's perspective, anybody can feel upset about anything. Understandable; that's how their mind works. But that doesn't make it right. And "what I think alone is right, and all other things are wrong" is wrong. One has to have that openness as to what is being said, in what context, to whom, where and when, and why. Suppose somebody says, "I want to do something", and you say "Do it" when it's not the best thing for the person — why should they do it? One has to think about it at times. You know, there is a lot of thinking in terms of saying, "Among **साधन**s, this alone will do." But it's not necessarily true. One medicine is not for everybody, and many medicines can achieve the same effect. Homeopathy can give a result; allopathy can give a result; Ayurveda can give a result. Disease goes or not? It goes. Why do you insist, "Allopathy worked for me; that alone should be taken by everybody"? It's not necessary; it's not necessary.

So I am saying, in terms of **साधन**s, each person is unique, and each person's circumstances are unique. But **एतावद्धि अनुशासनम्**: there is nothing more to be said. If this is understood, this is all. "See, Swamiji, if this is not understood?" Then start again. Start again what? Read the **Maitreyī** **Brāhmaṇa** again. Read **Ajātaśatru** again. Read the **Madhu Brāhmaṇa**, till it is understood. **पवनपुण्येन श्रवणं कुर्यात्**. Gain **अधिकारित्वम्**. "So **अधिकारित्वम्**, Swamiji, is real? It is other than **अद्वैतम्**?" No, it's not. **अधिकारित्वम्** is also part of the **द्वैत**. But through **द्वैत** only you walk up to **अद्वैत**, because that's human life. Right. So, **एतावद्धि अनुशासनम्**. So then — **किम्** — now, what has to be done? Then what? **षष्ठं ब्राह्मणम्**. **अथ वंशः**. **अथ वंशः**. **वंशः** means what?

**घृतकौशिकाद्घृतकौशिकः पाराशर्यायणात्पाराशर्यायणः पाराशर्यात्पाराशर्यो जातूकर्ण्याज्जातूकर्ण्य आसुरायणाच्च यास्काच्चासुरायणस्त्रैवणेस्त्रैवणिरौपजन्धनेरौपजन्धनिरासुरेरासुरिर्भारद्वाजाद्भारद्वाज आत्रेयादात्रेयो माण्टेर्माण्टिर्गौतमाद्गौतमो गौतमाद्गौतमो वात्स्याद्वात्स्यः शाण्डिल्याच्छाण्डिल्यः कैशोर्यात्काप्यात्कैशोर्यः काप्यः कुमारहारितात्कुमारहारितो गालवाद्गालवो विदर्भीकौण्डिन्याद्विदर्भीकौण्डिन्यो वत्सनपातो बाभ्रवाद्वत्सनपाद्बाभ्रवः पथः सौभरात्पन्थाः सौभरोऽयास्यादाङ्गिरसादयास्य आङ्गिरस आभूतेस्त्वाष्ट्रादाभूतिस्त्वाष्ट्रो विश्वरूपात्त्वाष्ट्राद्विश्वरूपस्त्वाष्ट्रोऽश्विभ्यामश्विनौ दधीच आथर्वणाद्दध्यङ्ङाथर्वणोऽथर्वणो दैवादथर्वा दैवो मृत्योः प्राध्वंसनान्मृत्युः प्राध्वंसनः प्रध्वंसनात्प्रध्वंसन एकर्षेरेकर्षिर्विप्रचित्तेर्विप्रचित्तिर्व्यष्टेर्व्यष्टिः सनारोः सनारुः सनातनात्सनातनः सनगात्सनगः परमेष्ठिनः परमेष्ठी ब्रह्मणो ब्रह्म स्वयम्भु ब्रह्मणे नमः ॥**

A lineage, okay? A lineage. A lineage of what? A lineage of teachers. And what did they do? Each one communicated the uncommunicable to the next person, without any loss. That's what is communicated. It is not the ways of doing which are always communicated; that's part of it. See, **प्रक्रिया**s are ways of doing things. But what really needs to get communicated is that vision: that there is only one reality, despite all the apparent differences. And that vision really is what has to come in the **वंश**. Not just saying, you know, "My father did this; you know, they celebrated Pongal, so I should also celebrate Pongal." That's okay, because the form and the spirit go hand in hand. Without any forms, the spirit will die; without spirit, the form will die. People don't appreciate this. This is something that **Pūjya** Swami used to highlight at times, you know: that the form and the spirit, both are needed. So, "Now you understood the spirit; therefore you don't need the forms; you drop all the forms" — the next generation will not catch the spirit also. You had the benefit of the form; you came to the spirit. Or you had the benefit of the spirit; therefore you appreciate the form. Both are needed.

So here, what? **पौतिमाष्यो गौपवनाद्गौपवनः पौतिमाष्यात्पौतिमाष्यो गौपवनात्**. So what is the repetition? No — it's the same teachers' names that are repeated, okay? Probably the same name. That's all that's happening. So it's a whole teaching **परम्परा**. Okay. But what is at the end, you see? This is starting last-in-first, you know: who is the last teacher? Being mentioned first. **अस्मदाचार्य** is being mentioned first. Who is in the first? **परमेष्ठी**. **ब्रह्मणः**. Who is that **ब्रह्म**? **ब्रह्म स्वयम्भु**: **स्वयं भवति**. Not caused: **अपूर्वम् अनपरम्**. If it is **अपूर्वम् अनपरम्**, where is **जगत्**? Where is **परम्परा**? Still it is there. Uncaused, unchanging, no cause, no effect means there is only one. That one — in that you have all this. So, **भाष्यकार**:

**अथेदानीं ब्रह्मविद्यार्थस्य मधुकाण्डस्य वंशः स्तुत्यर्थो ब्रह्मविद्यायाः ।** Why is there **स्तुत्यर्थः**? To praise this **ब्रह्मविद्या**: with so much effort, through teacher after teacher, it has come. From whom? From **Sadāśiva**, **ब्रह्म**.

Same way you will find, you know, in **Taittirīya**, in the **Śikṣāvallī**, several mantras are meant for that. It will say, "You chant this; it will remove your obstacles to your **ज्ञान**." How will it remove? This is where it is **अदृष्टम्**. Why that mantra alone? Why not **ॐ नमः शिवाय**? Can't say. Does it mean without that mantra nobody will get freedom? That's also not. Is there only one way of worshipping? Not true. A person, a **Kabīr** — he worshipped differently. Does it mean he won't get any freedom? Not true. See, because freedom is already a fact. How each person arrives there, there can be difference. But the fact — what is the fact remains the fact.

**तत्र वंश इव वंशः** — and now he says, **तत्र वंश इव वंशः**. See, the word **वंश** also means the ridges in the bamboo, you know. There is one bamboo, but you have these ridges. So, **वंश इव वंशः**.

**यथा वेणुः वंशः पर्वणः पर्वणो हि भिद्यते** — between each section of the bamboo you have these differences. It's like a ladder also, okay. **अध्यायचतुष्टयस्य आचार्यपरम्पराक्रमो वंश इत्युच्यते** — so the **आचार्यक्रम** in all the **अध्याय**s is being said here.

**तत्र प्रथमान्तः शिष्यः** — **भाष्यकार** makes a very simple commentary. **तत्र प्रथमान्तः शिष्यः**. Hello, who is in the **प्रथमा विभक्ति**? All the words: **शिष्य**. What is the other word?

**पञ्चम्यन्त आचार्यः** — so, from whom did you learn? **अपादान**, you know. So you go to the **आचार्य**. Therefore **पञ्चम्यन्त आचार्यः**.

**परमेष्ठी विराट् ; ब्रह्मणो हिरण्यगर्भात्** — so, you know, **विराट्** comes right in the beginning, because the first person to come. So where does your **परम्परा** begin? **ईश्वर**. You start with **ईश्वर** with no **गुण**s. Then you start with — the first one with **गुण** is what? **हिरण्यगर्भ**, **विराट्**. That's what you will say: **ब्रह्मणो हिरण्यगर्भात्**.

**ततः परम् आचार्यपरम्परा नास्ति ।** So from **ब्रह्मा**, **हिरण्यगर्भ**, the whole **परम्परा** begins: **सदाशिवसमारम्भाम्**; or **नारायणं पद्मभुवं वसिष्ठम्**, you know; or you would state the **Śiva** **परम्परा** or the **Nārāyaṇa** — it does make no difference, okay; this name difference is not relevant to us. **ततः परम् आचार्यपरम्परा नास्ति**.
