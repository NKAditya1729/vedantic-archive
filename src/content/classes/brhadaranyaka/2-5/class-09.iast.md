oṃ śrutismṛtipurāṇānām ālayaṃ karuṇālayam| namāmi bhagavatpādaṃ śaṅkaraṃ lokaśaṅkaram|| śaṅkaraṃ śaṅkarācāryaṃ keśavaṃ bādarāyaṇam| sūtrabhāṣyakṛtau vande bhagavantau punaḥ punaḥ|| īśvaro gururātmeti mūrtibhedavibhāgine| vyomavadvāptadehāya dakṣiṇāmūrtaye namaḥ|| oṃ saha nāvavatu| saha nau bhunaktu| saha vīryaṃ karavāvahai| tejasvināvad hītamastu mā vidviṣāvahai|| oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ|| oṃ namo brahmādibhyo brahmavidyāsaṃpradāyakṛtbhyo vaṃśaṛṣibhyo namo gurubhyaḥ||

We are looking at the end of the Maitreyī Brāhmaṇa — sorry, the **madhubrāhmaṇa**, in which the Upaniṣad, the **śruti**, has done a job, and now the **upaniṣad** itself is bringing in an **ākhyāyikā**. An **ākhyāyikā** is a story.

I think the Upaniṣad recognises one thing: human beings relate well with stories. If you teach anything in a very formulaic way, like a physics class, it may go in, but a story sticks. It seems to be using a story here, and it has its own way of putting a story across. We saw last time that the praise is in showing what kind of a risk a person takes to get this **vidyā**, and what kind of a risk a person takes to offer this **vidyā**. Both. That’s the story. The first two verses are more about the story, and the last two verses are about the **vidyā**. So this is the story we have to look at.

And I will say to you: in the whole **madhubrāhmaṇa** so far, what should you remember? What is **madhu** till now? **madhu** is what is mutually dependent, what is not independent. If any two things are depending on each other — if one thing depends on another thing, and that other thing depends on the first — then neither of them can be the truth. In fact, anything that depends on another is not an independent truth. This much we have to get from the **madhubrāhmaṇa**. If we don’t get anything else, it’s okay, but we have to get this much in our head: if anything depends on something else, the mutual dependency is very clear.

The **madhubrāhmaṇa** took a particular way to explain it. We saw thirteen or fourteen of those repeated things — what is the **madhu** — and it covered the **ādhyātmika**, the **ādhibhautika**, and it covered the **devatā**. This is one way of looking at it. But if you look at it in a general way now, the whole world depends on its being cognised by me, perceived by me. My eyes perceive light, therefore there is light. Correct? Because my eyes perceive it. Suppose there were no light: my eyes won’t perceive anything. And suppose there are eyes but there is no light: again my eyes won’t perceive anything. Equally true. Suppose there is taste, and there is nothing to taste: there is no taste for it. So all five senses are what? Sense and sense objects are **parasparaṃ** **madhu**. Therefore, what does it show? What does it reveal? Both are **mithyā**.

**jaganmithyātvam** is established by just this **parasparaṃ** **madhu**. Subject–object, at one more level, you see: the subject proves the object, the object proves the subject. **madhu**. **tripuṭī**, you see — **jñātā** **jñānaṃ** **jñeyam**, knower, knowledge, known. Can you talk of one without the other? What does it show? These three are three viewpoints into one reality. Everything we know is a viewpoint. What is the view? That alone is **satyam**. And all knowledge implies a viewpoint — viewpoint in the sense, here I am saying, there is a **jñātā**, there is a **jñeyam**, there is a **jñānam**, in everything. **jñātā**, **jñānaṃ**, **jñeyam** — are they independent things? Is there a **jñātā** without a **jñeyam**? Is there a knower without a known? Can you say he is a knower? Knower of what? Anything — but then that anything becomes an object. Without an object there is no knower.

So what is **brahmajñānam**? What are you going to know? If you know something, you become a knower, and the knower is not the truth. The Upaniṣad struggles with this, right? It tries to tell us this — the Kena Upaniṣad: **yasyāmataṃ** **tasya** **mataṃ** **mataṃ** **yasya** **na** **veda** **saḥ**. One who knows doesn’t know; one who doesn’t know, knows. What is this? One who doesn’t know knows? No — one who doesn’t know also doesn’t know. It’s all a struggle to try to convey something which is not conveyable by a word. And we always want to understand categorically: “No, tell me in this word what exactly it is.” We cannot. All of us have this, because everything we have known in the world we have known categorically, and therefore there is an attempt to somehow box this into that category. That cannot happen. If I become smug, saying “I understood,” then **na** **veda** **saḥ**. That way the Kena is a very beautiful Upaniṣad. Short, but it hits you on the face. If you think you know, you don’t know. Then if you think you don’t know, do you know? You don’t really know even then.

So then what is knowing? **pratibodhaviditam**. In every cognition it is known — not as an object, not as the content of a thought, but as the very being, the very **adhiṣṭhānam** of every thought. That **adhiṣṭhānam** you are.

In anything else, I may know some things. The examples are all what? Suppose you see something on the road. Is it a snake? I’m not sure. Could it be a pipe? It could be. Now, any number of times you knock off a mistake, it still doesn’t mean you know, because you may still be seeing something else. You can go through ten iterations and still not know what is right. But in the matter of what we are talking about, the truth, the truth is not something you have to know as an object. There is no **abhāva** for the truth. **abhāva** is not accepted; total **abhāva** is not acceptable. There has to be an **adhiṣṭhānam** for the **abhāva**. Therefore, in this regard, if you just say “this is not me,” the job is done in a way. Since **abhāva** is not accepted, whatever is the **adhiṣṭhāna** of all the **bhāva** and **abhāva** talked about in **vyavahāra** — that is not something that I have to know. That is.

So we have to understand the **vilakṣaṇatvam** of this whole teaching. It’s unlike anything else — very unlike everything else. And therefore, when you ask a question, I try to answer, but any question I try to answer categorically, I am going to be wrong. Please understand the limitation. Secondly, I may have my own kind of way of looking at things, and that will show. It’s not necessarily right for you; it may be right for me. Whatever helps us in a given way, we have total **śraddhā** in that. Everybody doesn’t come to this from the same background. That background, I think, accounts for the variety of ways that people arrive here, and each one therefore starts their own **paramparā**. But that which is cannot be contained in a method — one particular method, only one way.

But I’ll tell you what is only one way: that you are free. There is no doubt about that. If there is a **mokṣa** — I am saying “if” deliberately — if there is such a thing called freedom, which is totally independent of everything else, it cannot depend on anything else, it cannot be affected by anything else. Say it differently: it can be the only significant thing, the only **satyam**. This has to be understood. How you get there ultimately is not so important. So I am not a stickler to say this is the only way to get it, because everybody doesn’t speak the same language. I cannot say that a Ramana or a Nisargadatta doesn’t know what he is talking about. Cannot say it. My tongue will get cut if I say that. How do I know what he knows? But he does not speak the same language exactly as the Upaniṣad speaks. Is he wrong? You can’t say he is wrong. Is he right? Right and wrong are again subjective — unless you say what is right is that which is **abādhitaṃ** **satyam**. Then we are back to the same definition: what cannot be denied.

Your knower-status can be denied. Every day it is denied; in sleep it is denied every day. Therefore “you are a knower” is out of the question. Then what is this **vidyāvān**, **brahmavit**? All words. So sometimes it is very difficult what to say, what not to say. Should you put your hat on one method, or should you not put your hat on one method, on the name of a method? So much fight — not worthwhile. We should know one thing: we are looking for a truth which doesn’t depend on anything else. Therefore anything that depends on something else is **mithyā**. **madhu**. That is **madhu**. If we understand that **madhu**, then whichever Upaniṣad you are looking at, you are looking at what is **madhu** — because you eliminate the **madhu**, and then what remains is **satyam**.

Now, in the part we are going to see here, **madhu** means **brahmavidyā**. **madhuvidyā** is **brahmavidyā**. Now be ready for that shift; don’t confuse the two. The same word is used elsewhere: there **madhu** means karma, **karmaphala**, because **karmaphala** is sweet; you want to experience it, therefore it is **madhu**. So what is **madhu** depends on the context.

Then let me say one more thing here before I go on. This is a beautiful new help developed by Harsha ji — very useful — so I thought I would demonstrate it by using it a little. I’ll share the link with all of you. So, before concluding the topic I started in the Taittirīya Upaniṣad, I’ll just extend the **madhu** we just talked about a little.

**annaṃ** **bahu** **kurvīta** | **annaṃ** **na** **paricakṣīta** | Then here it is — look at this: **annaṃ** **na** **nindyāt** | **tadvratam** | Leave that out. **prāṇo** **vā** **annam** | **śarīramannādam** | **prāṇo** **vā** **annam** — **prāṇa** is **annam**. And what is **śarīra**? **annādam**, the one who eats. The **śarīra** eats the **prāṇa**. **prāṇe** **śarīraṃ** **pratiṣṭhitam** — where is the body really there? Only in a live being. Therefore **prāṇe** **śarīraṃ** **pratiṣṭhitam**. But where is **prāṇa**? **śarīre** **prāṇaḥ** **pratiṣṭhitaḥ**. See: A is in B, B is in A. **madhu**. **tadetadannamanne** **pratiṣṭhitam** — now he has removed the words **anna** and **prāṇa** also: **annamanne** **pratiṣṭhitam**. **sa** **ya** **etadannamanne** **pratiṣṭhitaṃ** **veda** **pratitiṣṭhati** — one who knows this, **pratitiṣṭhati**. That much. The rest of it is **upāsanā**; leave it out.

What I’m saying is, look at the beauty. **prāṇo** **vā** **annam** — why? **śarīrāntarbhāvāt** **prāṇasya**. Because where do you see **prāṇa**, life? In a body. Therefore **prāṇo** **vā** **annam** — **annam** means body here. **yadyasyāntaḥ** **pratiṣṭhitaṃ** **bhavati** **tattasyānnaṃ** **bhavati** — what is **annam**? The **bhāṣyakāra** is giving the definition of **annam**: whatever is established inside something, that is its **annam**. The whole body is **annamaya**, and **prāṇa** is established in it; therefore that is **anna**. But without life, no body survives. Therefore **śarīre** **ca** **prāṇaḥ** **pratiṣṭhitaḥ**, **tasmāt** **prāṇo'nnam**. **prāṇa** is established in the **śarīra**, therefore **prāṇa** is **annam**. **tathā** **śarīramapi** **annam** — the body also is **annam**, even though we said **śarīra** is the **annāda** and **prāṇa** the **annam**. In one way, see: I assimilate food, therefore what is assimilated by me is food, and I am the eater. So this is how it is. Why? **kasmāt**? **prāṇe** **śarīraṃ** **pratiṣṭhitam** — how do you say that? **tannimittatvāt** **śarīrasthiteḥ**: without **prāṇa** the **śarīra** doesn’t survive.

**tasmāt** **tadetadubhayaṃ** **śarīraṃ** **prāṇaśca** **annamannādaśca** — both are **anna**, both are **annāda**. Which is **anna**, which is **annāda**? It depends on how you want to look at it. Which is **kārya**, which is **kāraṇa**? It depends on how you want to look at it. Is the rain the cause of the ocean? Is the ocean the cause of the rain? **madhu**. **nāmarūpa** is all that. **yenānyonyasmin** **pratiṣṭhitaṃ** **tenānnam** — one depends on the other, therefore it is **annam**. **yenānyonyasya** **pratiṣṭhā** **tenānnādaḥ** — and one is the **pratiṣṭhā** for the other, therefore that is also **annādaḥ**. Both are standpoints. **tasmāt** **prāṇaḥ** **śarīraṃ** **cobhayamannamannādaṃ** **ca**. **sa** **ya** **evametadannamanne** **pratiṣṭhitaṃ** **veda** — one who knows this in this way (here it is meditation) **pratitiṣṭhati**; that fellow **pratitiṣṭhati**.
