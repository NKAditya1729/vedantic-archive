So the whole **jagat** is really one reality, only one. Yet we find the subject–object difference; we find sentient–insentient **bheda**. How did this come about? When I say "how did this come about", it's like saying, "How did the snake enter the room?" Conclusion is: already there is a snake there; there is no doubt about that. Snake is there. That's the way the **buddhi** works.

So then, okay, you want to hear a story? We will tell you one: **puraścakre dvipadaḥ**. See, these are all cities. Each body is a city, a city with several gates. And therefore, see, these old kingdoms would have a fortress, would have a boundary, and they would have a few gates to the city. So how many gates? And the gateways are, you know, the eyes, the ears, the nose, etcetera. So what did Brahman do? He wanted to come, you know. So first he created the cities. Then, having created cities, what did he do? What cities did he create? **puraścakre dvipadaḥ**: two-legged, you know, bodies — humans and birds, etcetera. Then what? **puraścakre catuṣpadaḥ**. Then he created four-legged creatures. This is **upalakṣaṇa** for all creatures.

Then, after that, what did he do? **puraḥ** is after that. **puraḥ sa pakṣī bhūtvā puraḥ puruṣa āviśaditi**. Too many **puraḥ**, okay? So, **puraḥ**: **purastāt**; **sa īśvaraḥ**; **pakṣī bhūtvā**: became a bird. What bird? No bird, okay. You have to remember the **Taittirīya** **pakṣī**, because at every level it's called a bird. So now, therefore, since he entered all the bodies, you know: **sarvāsu pūrṣu puriśayaḥ**. He is the one who is present. **śayaḥ**: sleeping; available; doesn't move; he is there always. Where is he? In every body. Therefore we call him what? **puruṣaḥ**, because he is there in every body. In every city he is there. Who is he? The city dweller. **giridhara nāgara**. **nagaranivāsī**: **nāgaraḥ**. How do you say that? **na enena kiñcana anāvṛtam**, **na enena kiñcana asaṃvṛtam**. So this is telling us the second part: **pūrṇatvāt puruṣaḥ**. And that same **puruṣa** is what? **sarvaṃ pūrayati**: he fills everything up; he fills everything up. Therefore, how do you say that? **na enena kiñcana anāvṛtam**: nothing is left uncovered by him. Nothing is outside of him; nothing is inside of him; there is only him.

Therefore, what do you get with this now? **pūrayati iti puruṣaḥ**; **pūrṇatvāt puruṣaḥ**.
