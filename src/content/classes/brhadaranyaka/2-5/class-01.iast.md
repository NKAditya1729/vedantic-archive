Okay. We looked at **maitreyīvidyā**. **maitreyīvidyā** is the **vidyā** taught to **maitreyī**. Before that, we saw what? **ajātaśatruvidyā**, taught by **ajātaśatru** to **dṛptabālāki**. So with names, you cannot be sure why they were called what they are called. **maitreyīvidyā** was because it was taught to **maitreyī**, so this must be taught to **madhu**. No, it is not like that. Neither is **madhu** the teacher here, nor is the first word of the **brāhmaṇa** **madhu**. There is just a convention; some way a name gets coined for a thing. But what is starting now is the fifth **brāhmaṇa** of the **dvitīya** **adhyāya**, and this **brāhmaṇa** generally is called **madhubrāhmaṇa**, and the teaching is sometimes referred to as **madhuvidyā**.

So what does **madhu** mean? We will see. **madhu** has, depending on situations, different meanings at times. If you hear the word **madhu**, **rūḍhyā**, what meaning do you get in your head? **madhu** is honey. **madhu** means honey. Sweet. What is sweet usually for us? Honey is sweet, of course. What is sweet? **karmaphalam**. So sometimes the word **madhu** is used for **karmaphalam**. Here the word **madhu** will be used in a way of meaning it is a **kārya**, an effect.

So what we have seen in both **ajātaśatrubrāhmaṇa** and especially in **maitreyībrāhmaṇa**, it was predominantly a **tvampadavicāra**. **tatpada** we hardly saw. In **ajātaśatrubrāhmaṇa** we did see the **sṛṣṭi** in a way, because we had so many **upāsanāḥ** being given first by **bālāki**. And after that **ajātaśatru** came and used the **avasthātraya** to some extent, and finally came to **neti** **neti** and showed us what **brahman** really is, that it has to be **prāṇasya** **prāṇaḥ**.

So we came from the first chapter, where it said **ātmetyevopāsīta**, 1.4.7. Then we saw the **avidyāsūtra**, 1.4.10. Then we also saw, in the same **mantra** before that, we had seen what? **ahaṃ** **brahmāsmi**. Then after that came 1.5. It was called **saptānnabrāhmaṇa**. A lot of **sṛṣṭi**-related things came there. And then it was said, all things we said in **saṃsāra** has been said in the first **adhyāya**. 1.6 was a summary of that. And in 1.6 what did we see? We just saw there are just three things. The whole **jagat** can be put as three things: **nāma**, **rūpa**, **kriyā**. This much is the truth that we know. But that is not the whole truth. Its **satyam** is **bhagavān**, **prāṇaḥ**. **prāṇā** **vai** **satyam**. Then **prāṇasya** **satyam** **kim**? That brought us to the second chapter.

Then in the second chapter we had **bālāki** coming and saying, “I will teach you. **brahma** **te** **bravāṇi**.” And **ajātaśatru** said, “Oh, just for saying that, here are the thousand cows.” And then **bālāki** went on and on, and at every point **ajātaśatru** said, “That I already know. Therefore he said, “I will not take away that respect. We will keep the **maryādā**, but I will make you understand what is **brahma**.” That is the job.

So we saw 2.1, 2.2. 2.2 was **śiśubrāhmaṇa**. So the first one, 2.1, was called **ajātaśatrubrāhmaṇa**. Second is called **śiśubrāhmaṇa**, where we saw some similarities between a cow—a calf, really—and **prāṇa**, etc. That was supposed to be an explanation of **prāṇā** **vai** **satyam**. Then **teṣāmeṣa** **satyam**. So with that we entered into the third **brāhmaṇa** of the second chapter, which is called **mūrtāmūrtabrāhmaṇa**. And though it is called **mūrtāmūrtabrāhmaṇa**, what was shown? **vastu** is neither **mūrta** nor **amūrta**. **vastu** is only known by **neti** **neti**. That **neti** **neti** was the main thing, the last **mantra** in 2.3. So with that **ajātaśatruvidyā** ended. **ajātaśatruvidyā** was **ajātaśatrubrāhmaṇa**, **śiśubrāhmaṇa** and **mūrtāmūrtabrāhmaṇa**. Three **brāhmaṇāḥ** was one **vidyā**, one teacher.

Then we entered **maitreyībrāhmaṇa**. **maitreyībrāhmaṇa** was the teaching of **yājñavalkya** to his wife **maitreyī**. And the highlight was, he said, “I want to take off. I want to go away somewhere, far away.” And therefore that **saṃnyāsavidhi** was highlighted by **bhāṣyakāra**. He said, “This **vidyā** is **sasaṃnyāsam**.” That was highlighted.

All right. So now the next **brāhmaṇa** is beginning, and customary to **bhāṣyakāra** is to show what is the connection of this **brāhmaṇa** to what has gone by, what this **brāhmaṇa** is going to do. So you can call it **sambandha**, you can call it **avatārikā**; it does not matter what we call it. Basically it is a connection, it is a link. At a verse level, we typically call them **avatārikā**. What is **avatāra**? Coming down. What brings it down is called **avatārikā**. So bringing it down means what? You are introducing the **mantra** which is about to come.

What is **sambandha**? Relationship. Relationship between **karmakāṇḍa** and **jñānakāṇḍa**. That is called the basic **sambandhabhāṣyam**. In any **upaniṣad** you will have a **sambandhabhāṣyam**. What will it be? Because where is **upaniṣad**? It is at the end of **karmakāṇḍa**; you have **jñānakāṇḍa**. Therefore, what is **jñānakāṇḍa**? What is its relevance? Why **karmakāṇḍa** came before? How is **karmakāṇḍa** different from **jñānakāṇḍa**? These matters are typically taken up in the **sambandhabhāṣya**. And once that **sambandhabhāṣya** is over, the **upaniṣad** begins.

So we normally say **sambandhabhāṣya** is only one, but sometimes I will use that word to mean the connection between one **brāhmaṇa** and the next also. I say **sambandha**. You could call it **avatārikā**, you could call it **sambandha**, you can just say **bhāṣya**. It does not matter what you call it. But every time you begin, there is a connection with what went by and what it connects to the future. These both things can be talked about. All right. So this is what the **madhubrāhmaṇa** is to now begin. To see, in spite of seeing many, in spite of experiencing many, **neha** **nānāsti** **kiñcana**. There is no manyness really. In fact, there is no manyness, because you have to know what to count. What should be counted is what is, independently is. So you do not count all derivatives. You count the real thing. If you want to count derivatives, count. That is a different set. But we are interested in counting what really is, what is **satyam**. So to count **satyam**, if you are counting many, we have to just show that the many that you count do not have an independent being.

All right? So here, this you will see at least in the beginning. In **madhubrāhmaṇa**, in the beginning, it is just stating this. It is not like an inquiry directly. It is a different way. So this is the general background for **madhubrāhmaṇa**. Because why are you going away? You are going away to firm up your **amṛtatva**. Because **udyāsyan**—**udyāsyan** **vā** **aham** **asmi**. For what? For gaining **jñānaniṣṭhā**. That is why you are going away. That is what I want.

So what is **maitreyībrāhmaṇa** for? What is the purpose of **maitreyībrāhmaṇa**? **amṛtatvasādhanam**. It is **amṛtatvasādhanam**. See how briefly he is saying it. **amṛtatvasādhanaṃ** **vaktavyam**. And that **amṛtatvasādhanam**, to gain that abidance in **amṛtatvasādhanam**, what is **yājñavalkya** doing? He is not getting married to a third wife. He is walking away. Why is he walking away? Because this **jñānam** is **karmanirapekṣam**. **amṛtatvam** is **karmanirapekṣam**, which means what? **karma** has nothing to do with **mokṣa**, once the **adhikāritvam** is there. **kevalam**, **karmanirapekṣam**. It has no dependency on **karma**. **amṛtatvasādhanam**, **tadvaktavyam**. That has to be said. Because though **ajātaśatrubrāhmaṇa** told you **neti** **neti**, it did not tell you it is **karmanirapekṣam** explicitly. **bhāṣyakāra** looks at **maitreyībrāhmaṇa** as a seal of approval for **saṃnyāsam**. **ārabdham**. **tacca** **ātmajñānaṃ** **sarvasannyāsāṅgaviśiṣṭam**. Making it very explicit. So what is **amṛtatvasādhanam**? **brahmajñānaṃ** **vā** **ātmajñānaṃ** **vā**, one and the same. If I am not acceptable, any amount of doing will only give me temporary windows of self-acceptance. When the world is acceptable, when the mind is acceptable, I accept. Mind is acceptable, why? Because the world only comes to me through my mind. And whatever the mind brings in, whether I accept or I say, “I cannot accept it,” is what happens here. And if you want to accept the world, if you want to accept yourself despite what the mind does inside, mind is not the problem there. “The mind is real” is the problem. “The world is real” is the problem there. “I think I am different from these” is the problem. What is reality? The reality is **brahmaivedaṃ** **sarvam**. All that is there is only **brahman**. That is you. If that is understood, **kimicchan** **kasya** **kāmāya** **śarīramanusañjvaret**? So what we know is, we grow and we understand that things outside really do not matter. They matter because I want them to be different. So you understand that if you do not mind, it does not matter. “Money, **svāmījī**, is a problem.” Not true. “Money is most wonderful.” Not true. Both are not true. One is **śobhanādhyāsa**, one is **aśobhanādhyāsa**. So you have to see money as what? You should see money as money. You should see relationship as relationship. Everything should be seen as it is. How do you see everything as it is? Unless you know the whole. Because you have to see everything, how it relates to everything else, because we are related. We have a horizontal relationship.
