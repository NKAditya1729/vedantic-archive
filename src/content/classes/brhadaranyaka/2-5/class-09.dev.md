    <p class="source-ref-line">Covered: बृहद् २.५.१६</p>
    <p class="src-bhashyam">परिसमाप्ता ब्रह्मविद्या अमृतत्व-साधनभूता, यां मैत्रेयी पृष्टवती भर्तारम् <a href="https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/display/bhashya/Brha/devanagari?page=2&amp;id=BR_C02_S04_V03&amp;hl=%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%B5%20%E0%A4%AD%E0%A4%97%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%83%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A7%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%82%20%E0%A4%B5%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%A6%20%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%B5%20%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%87%20%E0%A4%AC%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%82%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%BF"><span class="src-note-blue">‘यदेव भगवानमृतत्वसाधनं वेद तदेव मे ब्रूहि’ (बृ. उ. २ । ४ । ३)</span></a> इति । एतस्या ब्रह्मविद्यायाः स्तुत्यर्था इयमाख्यायिका आनीता । तस्या आख्यायिकायाः सङ्क्षेपतोऽर्थप्रकाशनार्थावेतौ मन्त्रौ भवतः ; एवं हि मन्त्रब्राह्मणाभ्यां स्तुतत्वात् अमृतत्वसर्वप्राप्तिसाधनत्वं ब्रह्मविद्यायाः प्रकटीकृतं राजमार्गमुपनीतं भवति — यथा आदित्य उद्यन् शार्वरं तमोऽपनयतीति — तद्वत् ।</p>
    <p class="src-bhashyam">अपि च एवं स्तुता ब्रह्मविद्या — या इन्द्ररक्षिता सा दुष्प्रापा देवैरपि ; यस्मात् अश्विभ्यामपि देवभिषग्भ्याम् इन्द्ररक्षिता विद्या महता आयासेन प्राप्ता ; ब्राह्मणस्य शिरश्छित्त्वा अश्व्यं शिरः प्रतिसन्धाय, तस्मिन्निन्द्रेण च्छिन्ने पुनः स्वशिर एव प्रतिसन्धाय, तेन ब्राह्मणस्य स्वशिरसैव उक्ता अशेषा ब्रह्मविद्या श्रुता ; यस्मात् ततः परतरं किञ्चित्पुरुषार्थसाधनं न भूतं न भावि वा, कुत एव वर्तमानम् — इति नातः परा स्तुतिरस्ति ।</p>
    <p class="src-bhashyam">अपि चैवं स्तूयते ब्रह्मविद्या — सर्वपुरुषार्थानां कर्म हि साधनमिति लोके प्रसिद्धम् ; तच्च कर्म वित्तसाध्यम् , तेन आशापि नास्त्यमृतत्वस्य ; तदिदममृतत्वं केवलया आत्मविद्यया कर्मनिरपेक्षया प्राप्यते ; यस्मात् कर्मप्रकरणे वक्तुं प्राप्तापि सती प्रवर्ग्य-प्रकरणे, कर्मप्रकरणादुत्तीर्य कर्मणा विरुद्धत्वात् केवल-सन्न्यास-सहिता अभिहिता अमृतत्व-साधनाय — तस्मात् नातः परं पुरुषार्थ-साधनमस्ति ।</p>
    <p class="src-bhashyam">अपि च एवं स्तुता ब्रह्मविद्या — सर्वो हि लोको द्वन्द्वारामः, <a href="https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/display/bhashya/Brha/devanagari?page=1&amp;id=BR_C01_S04_V03&amp;hl=%E0%A4%B8%20%E0%A4%B5%E0%A5%88%20%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%88%E0%A4%B5%20%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%87%20%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%80%20%E0%A4%A8%20%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%87"><span class="src-note-blue">‘स वै नैव रेमे तस्मादेकाकी न रमते’ (बृ. उ. १ । ४ । ३)</span></a> इति श्रुतेः ; याज्ञवल्क्यो लोकसाधारणोऽपि सन् आत्मज्ञानबलात् भार्या-पुत्र-वित्तादि-संसार-रतिं परित्यज्य प्रज्ञान-तृप्त आत्म-रति-र्बभूव । अपि च एवं स्तुता ब्रह्मविद्या — यस्मात् याज्ञवल्क्येन संसारमार्गात् व्युत्तिष्ठतापि प्रियायै भार्यायै प्रीत्यर्थमेव अभिहिता, <a href="https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/display/bhashya/Brha/devanagari?page=2&amp;id=BR_C02_S04_V04&amp;hl=%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%82%20%E0%A4%AD%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B7%E0%A4%B8%20%E0%A4%8F%E0%A4%B9%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B5"><span class="src-note-blue">‘प्रियं भाषस एह्यास्स्व’ (बृ. उ. २ । ४ । ४)</span></a> इति लिङ्गात् ॥</p>
    <p class="src-mulam">इदं वै तन्मधु दध्यङ्ङाथर्वणोऽश्विभ्यामुवाच । तदेतदृषिः पश्यन्नवोचत् । तद्वां नरा सनये दंस उग्रमाविष्कृणोमि तन्यतुर्न वृष्टिम् । दध्यङ् ह यन्मध्वाथर्वणो वामश्वस्य शीर्ष्णा प्र यदीमुवाचेति ॥ 2-5-16 ॥</p>
    <p class="src-bhashyam">तत्र इयं स्तुत्यर्था आख्यायिकेत्यवोचाम ; का पुनः सा आख्यायिकेति उच्यते — <span class="src-pratika">इदमि</span>त्यनन्तरनिर्दिष्टं व्यपदिशति, बुद्धौ सन्निहितत्वात् ; <span class="src-pratika">वै</span> - शब्दः स्मरणार्थः ; तदित्याख्यायिकानिर्वृत्तं प्रकरणान्तराभिहितं परोक्षं वै - शब्देन स्मारयन् इह व्यपदिशति ; यत् प्रवर्ग्यप्रकरणे सूचितम् , न आविष्कृतं मधु, <span class="src-pratika">तदिदं मधु</span> इह अनन्तरं निर्दिष्टम् — <a href="https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/display/bhashya/Brha/devanagari?page=2&amp;id=BR_C02_S05_V11&amp;hl=%E0%A4%87%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%82%20%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%83%E0%A4%A5%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%B5%E0%A5%80"><span class="src-note-blue">‘इयं पृथिवी’ (बृ. उ. २ । ५ । ११)</span></a> इत्यादिना ; कथं तत्र प्रकरणान्तरे सूचितम् — ‘दध्यङ् ह वा आभ्यामाथर्वणो मधु नाम ब्राह्मणमुवाच ; तदेनयोः प्रियं धाम तदेवैनयोरेतेनोपगच्छति ; स होवाचेन्द्रेण वा उक्तोऽस्म्येतच्चेदन्यस्मा अनुब्रूयास्तत एव ते शिरश्छिन्द्यामिति ; तस्माद्वै बिभेमि यद्वै मे स शिरो न च्छिन्द्यात्तद्वामुपनेष्य इति ; तौ होचतुरावां त्वा तस्मात्त्रास्यावहे इति ; कथं मा त्रास्येथे इति ; यदा नावुपनेष्यसे ; अथ ते शिरश्छित्त्वान्यत्राहृत्योपनिधास्यावः ; अथाश्वस्य शिर आहृत्य तत्ते प्रतिधास्यावः ; तेन नावनुवक्ष्यसि ; स यदा नावनुवक्ष्यसि ; अथ ते तदिन्द्रः शिरश्छेत्स्यति ; अथ ते स्वं शिर आहृत्य तत्ते प्रतिधास्याव इति ; तथेति तौ होपनिन्ये ; तौ यदोपनिन्ये ; अथास्य शिरश्छित्त्वा अन्यत्रोपनिदधतुः ; अथाश्वस्य शिर आहृत्य तद्धास्य प्रतिदधतुः ; तेन हाभ्यामनूवाच ; स यदाभ्यामनूवाच अथास्य तदिन्द्रः शिरश्चिच्छेद ; अथास्य स्वं शिर आहृत्य तद्धास्य प्रतिदधतुरिति । यावत्तु प्रवर्ग्यकर्माङ्गभूतं मधु, तावदेव तत्राभिहितम् ; न तु कक्ष्यमात्मज्ञानाख्यम् ; तत्र या आख्यायिका अभिहिता, सेह स्तुत्यर्था प्रदर्श्यते ; <span class="src-pratika">इदं वै तन्मधु दध्यङ्ङाथर्वणः</span> अनेन प्रपञ्चेन <span class="src-pratika">अश्विभ्यामुवाच</span> । <span class="src-pratika">तदेतदृषिः</span> — <span class="src-pratika">तदेतद्</span> कर्म, <span class="src-pratika">ऋषिः</span> मन्त्रः, <span class="src-pratika">पश्यन्</span> उपलभमानः, <span class="src-pratika">अवोचत्</span> उक्तवान् ;</p>

ॐ श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणानाम् आलयं करुणालयम्। नमामि भगवत्पादं शङ्करं लोकशङ्करम्॥ शङ्करं शङ्कराचार्यं केशवं बादरायणम्। सूत्रभाष्यकृतौ वन्दे भगवन्तौ पुनः पुनः॥ ईश्वरो गुरुरात्मेति मूर्तिभेदविभागिने। व्योमवद्व्याप्तदेहाय दक्षिणामूर्तये नमः॥ ॐ सह नाववतु। सह नौ भुनक्तु। सह वीर्यं करवावहै। तेजस्विनावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥ ॐ नमो ब्रह्मादिभ्यो ब्रह्मविद्यासंप्रदायकर्तृभ्यो वंशऋषिभ्यो नमो गुरुभ्यः॥

We are looking at the end of the Maitreyī Brāhmaṇa — sorry, the **मधुब्राह्मण**, in which the Upaniṣad, the **श्रुति**, has done a job, and now the **उपनिषद्** itself is bringing in an **आख्यायिका**. An **आख्यायिका** is a story.

I think the Upaniṣad recognises one thing: human beings relate well with stories. If you teach anything in a very formulaic way, like a physics class, it may go in, but a story sticks. It seems to be using a story here, and it has its own way of putting a story across. We saw last time that the praise is in showing what kind of a risk a person takes to get this **विद्या**, and what kind of a risk a person takes to offer this **विद्या**. Both. That’s the story. The first two verses are more about the story, and the last two verses are about the **विद्या**. So this is the story we have to look at.

And I will say to you: in the whole **मधुब्राह्मण** so far, what should you remember? What is **मधु** till now? **मधु** is what is mutually dependent, what is not independent. If any two things are depending on each other — if one thing depends on another thing, and that other thing depends on the first — then neither of them can be the truth. In fact, anything that depends on another is not an independent truth. This much we have to get from the **मधुब्राह्मण**. If we don’t get anything else, it’s okay, but we have to get this much in our head: if anything depends on something else, the mutual dependency is very clear.

The **मधुब्राह्मण** took a particular way to explain it. We saw thirteen or fourteen of those repeated things — what is the **मधु** — and it covered the **आध्यात्मिक**, the **आधिभौतिक**, and it covered the **देवता**. This is one way of looking at it. But if you look at it in a general way now, the whole world depends on its being cognised by me, perceived by me. My eyes perceive light, therefore there is light. Correct? Because my eyes perceive it. Suppose there were no light: my eyes won’t perceive anything. And suppose there are eyes but there is no light: again my eyes won’t perceive anything. Equally true. Suppose there is taste, and there is nothing to taste: there is no taste for it. So all five senses are what? Sense and sense objects are **परस्परं** **मधु**. Therefore, what does it show? What does it reveal? Both are **मिथ्या**.

**जगन्मिथ्यात्वम्** is established by just this **परस्परं** **मधु**. Subject–object, at one more level, you see: the subject proves the object, the object proves the subject. **मधु**. **त्रिपुटी**, you see — **ज्ञाता** **ज्ञानं** **ज्ञेयम्**, knower, knowledge, known. Can you talk of one without the other? What does it show? These three are three viewpoints into one reality. Everything we know is a viewpoint. What is the view? That alone is **सत्यम्**. And all knowledge implies a viewpoint — viewpoint in the sense, here I am saying, there is a **ज्ञाता**, there is a **ज्ञेयम्**, there is a **ज्ञानम्**, in everything. **ज्ञाता**, **ज्ञानं**, **ज्ञेयम्** — are they independent things? Is there a **ज्ञाता** without a **ज्ञेयम्**? Is there a knower without a known? Can you say he is a knower? Knower of what? Anything — but then that anything becomes an object. Without an object there is no knower.

So what is **ब्रह्मज्ञानम्**? What are you going to know? If you know something, you become a knower, and the knower is not the truth. The Upaniṣad struggles with this, right? It tries to tell us this — the Kena Upaniṣad: **यस्यामतं** **तस्य** **मतं** **मतं** **यस्य** **न** **वेद** **सः**. One who knows doesn’t know; one who doesn’t know, knows. What is this? One who doesn’t know knows? No — one who doesn’t know also doesn’t know. It’s all a struggle to try to convey something which is not conveyable by a word. And we always want to understand categorically: “No, tell me in this word what exactly it is.” We cannot. All of us have this, because everything we have known in the world we have known categorically, and therefore there is an attempt to somehow box this into that category. That cannot happen. If I become smug, saying “I understood,” then **न** **वेद** **सः**. That way the Kena is a very beautiful Upaniṣad. Short, but it hits you on the face. If you think you know, you don’t know. Then if you think you don’t know, do you know? You don’t really know even then.

So then what is knowing? **प्रतिबोधविदितम्**. In every cognition it is known — not as an object, not as the content of a thought, but as the very being, the very **अधिष्ठानम्** of every thought. That **अधिष्ठानम्** you are.

In anything else, I may know some things. The examples are all what? Suppose you see something on the road. Is it a snake? I’m not sure. Could it be a pipe? It could be. Now, any number of times you knock off a mistake, it still doesn’t mean you know, because you may still be seeing something else. You can go through ten iterations and still not know what is right. But in the matter of what we are talking about, the truth, the truth is not something you have to know as an object. There is no **अभाव** for the truth. **अभाव** is not accepted; total **अभाव** is not acceptable. There has to be an **अधिष्ठानम्** for the **अभाव**. Therefore, in this regard, if you just say “this is not me,” the job is done in a way. Since **अभाव** is not accepted, whatever is the **अधिष्ठान** of all the **भाव** and **अभाव** talked about in **व्यवहार** — that is not something that I have to know. That is.

So we have to understand the **विलक्षणत्वम्** of this whole teaching. It’s unlike anything else — very unlike everything else. And therefore, when you ask a question, I try to answer, but any question I try to answer categorically, I am going to be wrong. Please understand the limitation. Secondly, I may have my own kind of way of looking at things, and that will show. It’s not necessarily right for you; it may be right for me. Whatever helps us in a given way, we have total **श्रद्धा** in that. Everybody doesn’t come to this from the same background. That background, I think, accounts for the variety of ways that people arrive here, and each one therefore starts their own **परम्परा**. But that which is cannot be contained in a method — one particular method, only one way.

But I’ll tell you what is only one way: that you are free. There is no doubt about that. With that head now — **तेन** **ब्राह्मणस्य** **स्वशिरसैव** **उक्ता** **अशेषा** **ब्रह्मविद्या** **श्रुता** — with that human head, the whole **ब्रह्मविद्या** was heard. **यस्मात्** **ततः** **परतरं** **किञ्चित्पुरुषार्थसाधनं** **न** **भूतं** **न** **भावि** **वा** — because this is so.

So he says: look at this. There is a person who is willing to take off the head of the most respected person in his life, his teacher, with great risk, and put on a horse’s head, and wait for it to get cut off. And with the horse’s head also he has to start teaching **ब्रह्मविद्या** — he has to start somewhere; only then will Indra come and cut it off. So the horse also should talk like a human being! And after that, when the horse’s head is cut off, you have to be able to bring back the old head and stick it on, and it should be a successful surgery. Only then can the fellow complete **ब्रह्मविद्या**. This is the student’s risk. And the teacher’s risk is that he doesn’t know whether he’ll come back — **यद्गतं** **तद्गतम्**. He doesn’t mind, because he is free. The student’s risk is bigger, because only this person knows it; if he is gone, the **ब्रह्मविद्या** is gone.

So in this way it is. We saw this: **यस्मात्** **ततः** **परतरं** **किञ्चित्पुरुषार्थसाधनं** **न** **भूतं** **न** **भावि** **वा** — therefore he says there is no other **पुरुषार्थसाधन**. It was not there before, **न** **भूतम्**; it will not be there, **न** **भावि**. **कुत** **एव** **वर्तमानम्** — and where is it now, other than this? **इति** **नातः** **परा** **स्तुतिरस्ति** — therefore this is the best praise for it.

Whether you agree it’s praise or not doesn’t matter. This is how **श्रुति** is presenting it; this is how the **भाष्यकार** presents this as **स्तुति** of Brahman. Otherwise, you would have to explain why this sentence is sitting in the Veda, a deliberate **प्रमाण**. Once you accept the Veda as **प्रमाणम्**, you have the onus of showing the meaningfulness of every word in the Veda. A Ramana or a Nisargadatta does not have this onus. They are saying, "**हमारा** **काम** **हो** **गया**" — we don’t care what else is there or not there. That’s a big difference.

Because suppose I have no clue, and I am quite happy. The only thing I want is one cake instead of bread to eat today. That’s all I want. “I am tired of eating bread every day. I want cake — fruit cake. What is **ब्रह्मविद्या** going to give me, you tell me? Who cares for your **ब्रह्मविद्या**? there’s nothing, no life in it. Give me fruit cake; that’s what I want.” Then you had better go for karma. **श्रुति** will say: “You want that? You do some karma.” Correct. Ramana: “You go to Ramana.” “I want a fruit cake.” “Who wants a fruit cake?” “I want.” “Who is the ‘you’ who wants a fruit cake? Think about it.” “Poyā — I will go to the restaurant and eat it. Over. Outside the gate is a restaurant. No Ramana Ashram; I don’t have to be there. I will eat outside. Take care.” That’s what the fellow will say.

**श्रुति** says, “I am a supermarket. Nobody goes away from me without what they want.” It’s Kāmadhenu. Whoever wants something, they will get it. “You want fruit cake? **चलो**, I will give you fruit cake. How to get it, I will tell you. Pray, you will get it; or do this, you will get it.” Somebody else says, “No, no, fruit cake I have eaten. Now I know I can make better fruit cake and make money out of it.” “All right, you start a business. That also I will support.” “I am tired of making money.” “All right, what do you want?” “I want to go places.” “All right, you can move to **स्वर्ग**, where nobody has been. Go. That also I support.” “Done — enough travel. I am tired of just travelling, and I am tired of never being able to relax.” “Okay, you want to relax? Just be. Come, I will teach you **ब्रह्मविद्या**. That also I will teach.” This is called **श्रुति**.

It does not deny anybody. It is **सहस्रमातृवत्** **हितैषिणी** — like a mother who cares for her child. She doesn’t mind what the child wants. The child wants to go away from her; she says okay. The child wants to stay with her; she says okay. The child wants to eat something, or doesn’t want to eat; okay. Everything is mother’s love only. That is how **श्रुति** is. You cannot compare that love with a father’s love, which says, “No, you must work more; you must become successful. Start a good business; do something. If you want to do something, become famous in your own field.” Why not? That is a father’s love. A mother’s love is different — maybe the same, maybe different.

But what I’m saying is, **श्रुति** addresses everybody, whereas an individual teacher, an individual person who teaches in a given way **ब्रह्मविद्या**, may not teach in a way which is understandable by everybody, because he has a background too, as I said to you earlier. You see which verses of the Gītā Ramana Maharshi picks for students to learn — school students: no **कर्मयोग**. All the way, he wants to teach only **ज्ञानम्**, at that stage itself. That’s what he looks at. He doesn’t think there is anybody who is not an **अधिकारी**. But if I am interested in fruit cake, Ramana is not going to help me there. Won’t work. That we should understand. So this is the **परा** **स्तुति**.

Now, **अपि** **चैवं** **स्तूयते** **ब्रह्मविद्या** — another way of **स्तुति**. What is that? **सर्वपुरुषार्थानां** **कर्म** **हि** **साधनमिति** **लोके** **प्रसिद्धम्**. In the world it is well known to everybody: for anything you want, you have to do something. Without doing, you don’t get anything. **कर्म** **हि** **साधनम्** — **सर्वपुरुषार्थानाम्**, for any **पुरुषार्थ**, for anything a human being wants, you must work; effort is the means. **तच्च** **कर्म** **वित्तसाध्यम्** — and how do you manage to do **कर्म**? “I want to live comfortably.” Yes, you need a big house, a good house — not one room in paying-guest accommodation. So then what do you want? “I want a big house.” Good. You need money for it. **तच्च** **कर्म** **वित्तसाध्यम्**. But **तेन** **आशापि** **नास्त्यमृतत्वस्य** — from that **वित्त** you would not get immortality. This was told clearly by Yājñavalkya in the Maitreyī Brāhmaṇa, if you remember the quote. **तदिदममृतत्वं** **केवलया** **आत्मविद्यया** **कर्मनिरपेक्षया** **प्राप्यते** — this total freedom, from being limited by time or space or anything else, is gained only **केवलया** **आत्मविद्यया**, only by **आत्मविद्या**. What kind of **आत्मविद्या**? **कर्मनिरपेक्षया**, which does not depend on karma. **प्राप्यते**. That we saw.

And **यस्मात्** **कर्मप्रकरणे** **वक्तुं** **प्राप्तापि** **सती** **प्रवर्ग्यप्रकरणे** — even though there was an occasion to talk about **अमृतत्वम्** in the **प्रवर्ग्यप्रकरण** — a section called **प्रवर्ग्यप्रकरण**, which is not part of the **उपनिषद्**, in the earlier part of the **ब्राह्मण** — **कर्मप्रकरणादुत्तीर्य** **कर्मणा** **विरुद्धत्वात्** **केवलसन्न्यासहिता** **अभिहिता** **अमृतत्वसाधनाय**. It did not talk of it there, because it cannot be mixed with karma. Therefore **कर्मप्रकरणादुत्तीर्य**, transcending the **कर्मप्रकरण**. Why? **कर्मणा** **विरुद्धत्वात्** — because this **ज्ञानम्** we are talking about is opposed to **कर्म**, because we are going to tell you that you are **अकर्ता**. Where is the question of karma? You are **अभोक्ता**. I want karma for **भोग**; you are **अभोक्ता** — where is karma?

“Swamiji, then I will never do any karma.” You will be a lazy **भक्त**! Unless you are ready — when is it “no karma”? When you don’t need anything to be different; you understand that making it different won’t make a difference to you. Something will come and go; that’s all. You are very, very clear, and you are at ease with that. Being clear is one matter; being at ease with that is another matter. Intellectually we are all very clear about it. But I am not at ease if you take away my nice Mercedes car: “I only like to drive a Mercedes, not any other car.” Then you had better understand that **केवलज्ञान** will not do anything for you; you had better go for karma also. You need it.

Therefore **अमृतत्वम्** is **कर्मणा** **विरुद्धम्** — **विरुद्धम्** until you understand karma is **मिथ्या**. Once it is understood, nothing is **विरुद्ध**. Nothing is **विरुद्ध** to **ज्ञान**, finally. **साध्यावस्थायाम्** it is **विरुद्ध**; **सिद्धावस्थायां** **किञ्चिदपि** **न** **विरुद्धं** **भवति**. Since this is pulled out and taught — **तस्मात्** **नातः** **परं** **पुरुषार्थसाधनमस्ति** — therefore there is no **साधन** more important than **ब्रह्मविद्या**.

Another way of **स्तुति**: **अपि** **च** **एवं** **स्तुता** **ब्रह्मविद्या**. The **भाष्यकार** is in a mode of showing **अर्थवाद**, how all along **ब्रह्मविद्या** is praised. **सर्वो** **हि** **लोको** **द्वन्द्वारामः** — all people generally are happy in relationships, **द्वन्द्वारामः**: wife for husband, husband for wife, partners — nowadays more and more live-in partners — friends, whichever way you use it. It doesn’t matter what word you use. But “I want you, you want me, we are both happy with each other”: this is the idea. **द्वन्द्वारामः**.

Where does the Upaniṣad say this? He quotes: ‘**स** **वै** **नैव** **रेमे** **तस्मादेकाकी** **न** **रमते**’. You know, Brahmājī came; he is doing **सृष्टि**, and he is alone. He is the fellow who is to do **सृष्टि**, and there is nobody around. **स** **वै** **नैव** **रेमे** — he was unhappy. His genes are coming to us — Brahmājī’s genes. Therefore **तस्मादेकाकी** **न** **रमते**: nobody is happy being alone. You want that special person in your life with whom you can gel, and you are looking for that person all your life — that’s a different problem.

“You know, Swami, I am not happy. I went to the Himalayas; it was so beautiful, but I was missing sharing it with my beloved.” See the problem: while you are enjoying the beautiful thing, you are missing something. That is called **एकाकी** **न** **रमते**. You went to a beautiful ashram, you enjoyed the teaching, but suddenly: “My friend is not with me. He should also have been with me.” Very nice. He also wants the same thing. That’s the idea. **एकाकी** **न** **रमते** **इति** **श्रुतेः**.

And what about Yājñavalkya? **याज्ञवल्क्यो** **लोकसाधारणोऽपि** **सन्** — he is also like you and me, another person. He doesn’t have horns on his head. **लोकसाधारणोऽपि** **सन्**, **आत्मज्ञानबलात्** — on account of this **आत्मज्ञान** — **भार्यापुत्रवित्तादिसंसाररतिं** **परित्यज्य**: completely giving up — **परितः** **त्यक्त्वा** — his value, his false values, for wife, children, wealth, family, house, business, whatever; giving up everything, all **संसाररति**, the delight in this world of travelling. **परित्यज्य** **प्रज्ञानतृप्तः** — pleased just by himself — **आत्मरतिर्बभूव**: he became what? **यस्त्वात्मरतिरेव** **स्यादात्मतृप्तश्च** **मानवः** । **आत्मन्येव** **च** **सन्तुष्टस्तस्य** **कार्यं** **न** **विद्यते** ॥ — the Gītā. He became a person who is happy being himself, nothing else. Not happy with his mind — happy being himself. To be happy with the mind is very difficult; the mind is very difficult to be happy with sometimes.

**अपि** **च** **एवं** **स्तुता** **ब्रह्मविद्या** — in this way also it is praised. How? **यस्मात्** **याज्ञवल्क्येन** **संसारमार्गात्** **व्युत्तिष्ठतापि** — Yājñavalkya, who is walking out of this **संसार**. **व्युत्तिष्ठता**: **उत्थानम्** is going away. **वि** — **विशेषेण** **उत्थानम्**, getting up from, means going away, **इत्यर्थः**. **संसारसन्न्यास** is called **व्युत्थानम्**. **व्युत्तिष्ठतापि** **प्रियायै** **भार्यायै** **प्रीत्यर्थमेव** **अभिहिता** — for his wife, who is very dear to him, for her pleasure, for her happiness, it was taught. On what basis? ‘**प्रियं** **भाषस** **एह्यास्स्व**’ **इति** **लिङ्गात्**. She said, “I am not interested in anything but **अमृतत्वम्**; by what you will get **अमृतत्वम्**, teach me that alone.” She showed what she wants, and therefore, though he was walking away, he stayed back just to give her this, because it is so valuable. In this way also **ब्रह्मविद्या** is **स्तुता**.
