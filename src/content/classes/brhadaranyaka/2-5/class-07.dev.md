ॐ श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणानाम् आलयं करुणालयम्। नमामि भगवत्पादं शङ्करं लोकशङ्करम्॥ शङ्करं शङ्कराचार्यं केशवं बादरायणम्। सूत्रभाष्यकृतौ वन्दे भगवन्तौ पुनः पुनः॥ ईश्वरो गुरुरात्मेति मूर्तिभेदविभागिने। व्योमवद्व्याप्तदेहाय दक्षिणामूर्तये नमः॥ ॐ सह नाववतु। सह नौ भुनक्तु। सह वीर्यं करवावहै। तेजस्विनावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥ ॐ नमो ब्रह्मादिभ्यो ब्रह्मविद्यासंप्रदायकर्तृभ्यो वंशऋषिभ्यो नमो गुरुभ्यः॥

All right. We are at the end of the **मैत्रेयी**—sorry, the **मधुब्राह्मण**, 15th **मन्त्र**. And **भाष्यकार** has said everything he wants to say. And he is now—we saw the example to say everything is one. The example given was a wheel with, you know, a hub and a rim and spokes in between. And the example is that the spokes have no being apart from the rim and the hub. I mean, they basically are totally one with it. That's the idea being conveyed.

But for somebody who doesn't understand what the **श्रुति** is saying, because we saw towards the end of the last class, the **श्रुति** has to present two things. You have to be able to understand the one reality while living in this world where the experience is duality. The experience is always I, and there is what I experience. This is everybody's universal experience. How do I reconcile this experience, which is every moment, with the statement of the **श्रुति** that there is one non-dual? This is a difficult part of this. And the **श्रुति** has both kinds of statements. So on one hand it will say, you know, everything is this. On the other hand it will say there is nothing but this. What? How do you say both things? This is a problem, seeming problem.

This is why I said these sentences are what appear like paradoxes to us, because you cannot make peace in your head with two ideas being talked about, and being talked about by the same.
