ātmetyevopāsīta; tadeva etasmin sarvasmin padanīyam ātmatattvam, yasmāt preyaḥ putrādeḥ — ityupanyastasya vākyasya vyākhyānaviṣaye sambandhaprayojane abhihite — 'tadātmānamevāvedahaṁ brahmāsmīti tasmāttatsarvamabhavat' (bṛ. u. 1।4।10) iti; evaṁ pratyagātmā brahmavidyāyā viṣaya ityetat upanyastam।

**ātmetyevopāsīta** — where is this 1.4.7? We spent a lot of time there. Because this **ātmetyevopāsīta** is considered like the sentence which is being explained starting from there till the end of **bṛhadāraṇyaka**। So this word **ātma iti eva upāsīta** — what is **ātmā** there? **ātmā** is not a body. **ātmā** is what? **yadāpnoti yadādatte yat ca atti viṣayān** — that which pervades — basically, it's talking of what? **sṛṣṭi-sthiti-laya-kāraṇam** — so it is that from which it arises, that by which it is sustained, that into which it resolves — that's the definition of **ātmā** coming from **liṅgapurāṇa**। But that is **ātmā** here. What is this **iti** for **ātma iti eva upāsīta**? That **iti** is presented as trying to show that it is different from what is **vyākṛta** and **avyākṛta**। What is different from **kārya** and **kāraṇa**? What is different from **vidita, avidita**? That **iti** is doing all that work for us. Because otherwise, see — the word **ātma iti** means it looks like a typical **upāsanavākya**। So, **śālagrāmaṁ viṣṇuḥ iti upāsīta** — **śālagrāma** is not **viṣṇu**। A stone is not **viṣṇu**, a stone is not **śiva**। You will say what? In that you bring **śiva-buddhi**। You bring **viṣṇu-buddhi**। Therefore this sentence is very easily misunderstood as an **upāsana-vākya**। But there the **iti** is not to say — it is not — you look upon it — that is not the meaning of **iti** here. Meaning has to be brought out. **sampradāya** does that. **eva** — **eva** is what? There is no other way. You know — it is not anything else. It is not **vācya**। It is not **abhidhāna**। It is not **abhidheya**। It is not a word. It is not the meaning of the word. Okay. So, we have to understand the word very clearly — what is being talked about is that which cannot be objectified. And **upāsīta** — **upa** is **sāmīpyam** — near — **ās** is to sit. What is nearer than **kārya** and **kāraṇa**? What is nearer than anything in the world? What cannot become nearer? That is **upa**। Same **upa** is in **upaniṣad। upa** - **samīpe**। Means — this **upāsīta** means you do **vicāra**। It does not mean **upāsana** — the sentence is very — if you look at it per se, it doesn't say anything. Without a commentary you would probably never understand it. It looks like it's stuck in a large **mantra**. 1.4.7 is a big **mantra**. It's called **vidyāsūtra। ātma iti eva upāsīta** is called **vidyāsūtra**। So this was said already — **ātma iti eva upāsīta** — so do enquire into this **ātmā** yourself. What is the reality of yourself? With the help of **āgama-vākya**। Why should you look into it? We don't know the business. We can do a lot of things. **agnihotra** is there — we have done.

**tadeva etasmin sarvasmin padanīyam ātmatattvam। tadeva** — That alone — **padanīyam**। What is **padanīyam**? **pada** is **padyate iti padam** — whatever you go after, **puruṣārtha** — what is sought after is called **pada। tadeva ātmatattvaṁ padanīyam**। Amongst all things, anything that one can go after — what is the thing that really is worth going after? **anīyar pratyaya** has that meaning of extra force — it should be gone to, it's worth going after. **tad arhati** — **tadeva etasmin sarvasmin padanīyam** — amongst all this, this is the one, it should be gone to. What is it? **ātmatattvam**। Why should we go only after **ātmatattvam**? Why not something else? **yasmāt preyaḥ putrādeḥ** — **preyaḥ** — what is **preyaḥ**? Dearer. It is dearer. It is **priyataraḥ। preyaḥ** — **priyataraḥ। preṣṭhaḥ** — **priyatamaḥ**। Should remember — this is **īyasunpratyaya। preyas**। Like **jyāyas। jyāyasī cetkarmaṇaste matā buddhirjanārdana** — what is **jyāyasī cet**? — if you think **karma** is better — **śreyaśca preyaśca manuṣyametaḥ** in **kaṭhopaniṣad। śreyas** — so that **īyasunpratyaya** — **īyas**। Here also it is there — **preyas। preyas** means dearer. So, sometimes it appears it is better to use that relative degree word than the superlative. See — suppose you say "This is the dearest" — it's something absolute. But another way of saying it is — **preyaḥ** — anything you say, it is dearer than that. Anything you say, it's dearer than that. Which means what? You cannot reach it in any categorical way. Because anything you look upon, it is dearer than that. So in that way **śruti** sometimes plays with the words. You will see a lot of these relative words. You will also see the absolute sometimes also. **preṣṭhaḥ** also you will see, but here **preyaḥ** — compared to what? **putrādeḥ**। What is dear to one — house is dear to one, car is dear to one, job is dear to one, wealth is dear to one — I don't mind spending all my wealth because my children — I will take care of them. My children are so important, they are my life. **putraḥ** — **putrādeḥ api preyaḥ** — **ādipadāt** — bring everything else. Children are dear to me and I am willing to do so much. I am willing to sell my house for the sake of the child. I am willing to, you know, spend all my money for a child's well-being. But then this body becomes more important than even that. For this body's sake I will give up something else too. I will even give up myself — if I can't take care of myself, I will take care of myself first. What is dearer than the body? What is dearer than life? What is dearest is myself. Whatever makes me comfortable, whatever pleases me. So, for some people, you know, dying for the cause of religion or dying for the cause of nation is more important than preserving this body. But even for them, what pleases them is most important. So, if you take it anywhere — what makes me more acceptable to myself is the dearest. Therefore, what is — see, whatever pleases me is dear to me. Whatever pleases me is dear to me. So, what is really what should be dear to me? What pleases me is dear to me secondarily — because it pleases me. Therefore, what am I really after? I am not after what pleases me. I am after the pleased I. Pleased me is what I am interested in. Happy me, a comfortable me, an acceptable me is what my real value is for. So therefore **preyaḥ putrādeḥ** — it is that which is dearer than anything. Therefore **ātmā** is dearest. **ātmā** — self — self-acceptable — is the nature of **ātmā**। It is by itself the **svarūpa** of **ātmā**। It is **preyaḥ। putrāt preyaḥ, vittāt preyaḥ**। We add the word "pleased" because to us it looks like self can be pleased or displeased. Self can be happy or unhappy. Wanting or fulfilled. So I am not interested in wanting self. I am interested in fulfilled self. But finally what we understand — self is fulfilled. The problem is you take yourself to wanting, which is not inherent to self. Therefore, self alone is what we are looking for in and through every effort and pursuit in life.

Therefore, **ātmatattvaṁ padanīyaṁ yasmāt preyaḥ putrādeḥ — ityupanyastasya vākyasya vyākhyānaviṣaye sambandhaprayojane abhihite** — this **upanyastasya** — it is mentioned, it is said as **vidyāsūtra**। Of this sentence, **vyākhyāna-viṣaye sambandhaprayojane abhihite** — **abhihite** was said. What was said? **sambandha** and **prayojana**। What is **prayojana**? Purpose. What is **sambandha**? Connection between **vidyā** and **prayojana**। And what are these? These are things to be explained. **vyākhyāna-viṣaye। vyākhyānaviṣaye sambandhaprayojane abhihite** — Where was it said? **tad ātmānam eva aved** — he knew himself — as what? **ahaṁ brahmāsmīti**। I am **brahman। iti** he knew. And when he knew, what happened? **tasmāt tat sarvamabhavat** — when he knew **brahman**, he became everything. How he became everything? He didn't become — he recognised: I am **brahman** and **brahman** is **jagatkāraṇam**, therefore I am all that is here. He knew. So, this is what now — what is the **sambandha**? By knowing **ātmā** alone you become — you are everything. There is nothing. There is no **dvaitam**। You become free. That is the **sambandha**। What is the **prayojana**? **sarvamabhavat**। You see you are all-pervading. There is nothing but you. There is no reason for fear because **dvitīyād vai bhayaṁ bhavati** — there is no second thing. There is no second reality. There is only one reality and everything is just a manifestation of that. **iti** — **evaṁ pratyagātmā brahmavidyāyā viṣayaḥ**। In this way — **pratyagātmā** — to know **brahman**, what should you finally know? You cannot know some unknown **brahman** just like that. **brahman** is not one more thing to be known. Therefore, **brahman** can only be known — not as any one thing — but as all that is here. How would you know that? — only by knowing yourself as non-separate from that **brahman**। Therefore, **pratyagātmā**, I who is not objectifiable ever, never externalizable, that **ātmā** alone is **brahmavidyāyāḥ viṣayaḥ**। That is the subject matter for **brahmavidyā। brahmavidyā** is **ātmavidyā**। Because it began with what? **ātmetyevopāsīta** — **na tu brahma iti eva upāsīta**। I am the subject matter of **brahmavidyā** — why? Because **brahma** is **ātmā**। Therefore the knowledge is — I am **brahman** — **ahaṁ brahmāsmi**।

And therefore, the knowledge is — I am **brahman। ahaṁ brahmāsmi**। And by knowing that, you become everything — means what? There is nothing else to be gained. **brahmavidāpnoti param**। Why? Because if there is one more thing, then there is something else remaining. But if — by knowing **brahman** — **tasmāt tat sarvamabhavat** means **brahma eva idaṁ sarvam**, **sarvaṁ khalvidaṁ brahma** — this understanding is gained. **iti evaṁ pratyagātmā brahmavidyāyā viṣaya ityetat upanyastam। bhāṣyakāra** is saying this all has been said already. It's been talked about. Further — you told what is really **padanīyam**, what is really to be gone after, the most important thing. But what about other things?

avidyāyāśca viṣayaḥ — the subject matter of avidyā। What is avidyā? avidyā is not really knowledge — it can be improved upon. Because what is avidyā? That which will go in the wake of vidyā। Okay. So, because there is no such thing, you can never objectify avidyā। Whatever you objectify at that time is vidyā। Only when you understand it — as wrong — then vidyā will displace avidyā। So, avidyāyāḥ viṣayaḥ। anyo'sāvanyo'hamasmīti na sa veda — so this is 1.4.10. Starting here, it's called avidyāsūtra। vidyāsūtra is ātmetyevopāsīta। Now avidyāsūtra is what? anyo'sau anyo'hamasmi iti — that is different, I am different — which means I have managed to contain myself, limit myself to this body-mind-sense complex. Otherwise I cannot say anyaḥ asau, anyaḥ aham। That aham has to be — you have to have boundaries. That binding — antaḥ — you know — anantaḥ is unbound, antaḥ is bound. So anyo'sāvanyo'hamasmīti na sa veda — iti ārabhya — having begun in this manner — cāturvarṇya-pravibhāgādi-nimitta pāṅktakarma-sādhya-sādhana-lakṣaṇaḥ bījāṅkuravat vyākṛtāvyākṛtasvabhāvaḥ nāma-rūpa-karmātmakaḥ saṁsāraḥ 'trayaṁ vā idaṁ nāma rūpaṁ karma' (bṛ. u. 1।6।1) ityupasaṁhṛtaḥ — it was concluded.
