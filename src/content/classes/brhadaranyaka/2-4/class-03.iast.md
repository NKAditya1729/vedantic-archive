This is **pūrvamīmāṁsā**।

So for such a person, his whole thing is **karma** alone. So that **karmasaṁnyāsa** is, like, "No, I can't accept this **karmasaṁnyāsa**. You are messing up the whole order. You are destroying people." If you remember the story of **śaṅkara** meeting **maṇḍanamiśra** — you know, **maṇḍanamiśra** was a great **karmaṭha**, and on **śrāddha** day — that too, **śaṅkara** goes, and goes by **yogavidyā**, he goes over the boundary, you know, gate, and enters the place, creates a lot of commotion. So then, "This **karma** you can’t give up.”

When he is being told in the **upaniṣad** itself — in this **brāhmaṇa** it is going to be said — that if you say... suppose you are a **brāhmaṇa**, **kṣatriya**, **vaiśya**, **śūdra**, suppose you are a **kṣatriya**, and if you say "I am not a **kṣatriya**" — suppose you say "I am not a **kṣatriya**" — what will **kṣatriya** community do to you? They will disown you. They will disown you. Understand now what does **upaniṣad** want to say?

See, in **vaidika** life, your **varṇāśramābhimāna** is protected, in a way, and enhanced by the **veda**, introduced and enhanced by the **veda**। Whole **lokavyavahāra** is about **varṇāśrama**। What is your **varṇa**, and which state of life are you in? What is your station in life? Are you an administrator, a teacher, an agricultural worker, a menial worker, helping others — what do you do? You must have some role in society. And what is your age? Are you in the age of a student? Are you in the age of being a married person working with family? Are you a person who has given up and gone to a forest? What is your state? Between this matrix, 4x4 matrix, is your whole life.

So suppose I told you that "You don't belong to a **varṇa**. You are not a **brāhmaṇa**, you are not a **kṣatriya**, you are not a **śūdra**, you are not a **vaiśya**" — then what **vaidikakarma** can you do? You will be lost, because you have no place in the matrix now. You are called **vedabāhya** — you are outside the scope of the **veda**। Then what **karma** can you do?

But what does **upaniṣad** say? It says **brahmatam parādāt**— the **brāhmaṇa**s will give up a person who says "I am not a **brāhmaṇa**." **kṣatriya**s will give up a person who says "I am not a **kṣatriya**." And what does **brahman** want to tell? na iti na iti - What does it want to tell? It says — "If you think you are **brāhmaṇa**, **neti**। I am a **kṣatriya**, **neti**। I am a **vaiśya**, **neti**। I am a **śūdra**, **neti**। I am a **brahmacārī**, **na**। I am not a **brahmacārī**, **brahmacārī** I am. I am not a **brahmacārī**, I am a **gṛhastha। na**, **gṛhastha** I am, but I am not a **gṛhastha**। I am a **saṁnyāsī**, **na। saṁnyāsī** I am, but I am not a **saṁnyāsī**।" You get the point? Beggar I am in the play, but I am not a beggar. I am an actor. I am only an actor. All roles are as though.

This **śāstra**, **upaniṣad**, cannot tell, if **karma** is required. Because if **karma** is what — it means for **mokṣa**, it must reinforce your identity with a particular **varṇa**। It cannot say "You are not **brāhmaṇa**, you are not **kṣatriya**, you are not **vaiśya**, you are not **śūdra**। You better belong to one of them."

So first it said wealth **nindā**, now it is doing what? **varṇāśramavyavasthānindā**। Not **vyavasthānindā**, it is just saying "You are not — you don't belong to **varṇa**." What does it show? Again, commitment is not in this section. Commitment is not to **karma**. So if you are not a **brāhmaṇa**, what should you do? Give up **brāhmaṇakarma**। You are not a **kṣatriya**, what should you do? Give up **kṣatriyakarma**। You are not **vaiśya**, what should you do? Give up **vaiśyakarma**। What **karma** will you have remaining?

"Swamiji, what about women? They don't have any **karma**." Question is raised by **vārtikasāra**। "Hey, woman also thinks 'I am a doer' and does things in life, right? That she can give up. She has to also give up what? The idea that I am a doer." Don't give up your **karma**. **upaniṣad** is never telling you give up **karma** directly. You have to give up **karma**। How is **saṁnyāsa** really? By giving up **kartṛtvam**, not by giving up **karma**। Giving up **karma** is **karmatyāga। saṁnyāsa** really is **kartṛtvatyāga**। Doership, the agency that I am a doer, is given up. But in **vividiṣāsaṁnyāsa**, you give up **karma**, doership is still there. Otherwise you would be a **vidvatsaṁnyāsī**। So it's a bridge there.

But that idea is being said here by saying — which we will look at next time — **brahmatam parādāt** etc. That section also shows that **karma** is — **karma-saṁnyāsa** is the subject matter here. Why? Because **bhāṣyakāra** wants to show **maitreyībrāhmaṇa** is about giving up **karma**।
