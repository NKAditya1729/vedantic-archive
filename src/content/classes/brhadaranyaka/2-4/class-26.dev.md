ॐ श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणानाम् आलयं करुणालयम्। नमामि भगवत्पादं शङ्करं लोकशङ्करम्॥ शङ्करं शङ्कराचार्यं केशवं बादरायणम्। सूत्रभाष्यकृतौ वन्दे भगवन्तौ पुनः पुनः॥ ईश्वरो गुरुरात्मेति मूर्तिभेदविभागिने। व्योमवद्व्याप्तदेहाय दक्षिणामूर्तये नमः॥ ॐ सह नाववतु। सह नौ भुनक्तु। सह वीर्यं करवावहै। तेजस्विनावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥ ॐ नमो ब्रह्मादिभ्यो ब्रह्मविद्यासंप्रदायकर्तृभ्यो वंशऋषिभ्यो नमो गुरुभ्यः॥

So we are examining — **श्रुति** is examining, or telling us how **आत्मा** should be recognized, how it should be known. **आत्मा** **वा** **अरे** **द्रष्टव्यः**. How? **श्रोतव्यो** **मन्तव्यो** **निदिध्यासितव्यः**.

**श्रोतव्यः** was told through the example of, or through the verse which talked of, you know, **ब्रह्म** **तं** **परादात्**. That's where **श्रोतव्यः** was said. Not in **ब्रह्म** **तं** **परादात्** — in the end of that mantra, where it says everything is one. **इदं** **क्षत्रम्**, **इदं** **ब्रह्म** — all that is one. That statement that everything is only one is the **श्रवणम्** part. But I don't understand it. Logically, it doesn't make sense. For the **मननम्** comes the next few verses.

And what are they consisting of? **मननम्** is logically showing thing in three ways. What something — what a thing is, the truth of a thing, is that of which, from which it appeared to come, that which sustains it, and that into which it goes. Which means what? At no time the thing is anything other than itself.

Because the thing that — see, if a thing changes really from one to another, if A can become B, neither A is true nor B is true. If A can really become B, if A and B are entirely different, then neither A can be called truth nor B can be called truth, because **श्रुति** knows that there is a thing which is the same all the way.

You and I can say, you know, that yeah, everything is the way it is and everything changes. Change means what? Change means that you recognize a given thing in another form. There is no other thing called change. Correct? So if I were to say, this is a pen. Without this one piece, also it's a pen. Then I would have an idea of what a pen means. What is right is a pen? Is the refill the pen? Why do you call it refill? So what is exactly a pen? If you try to define it, you'll be in trouble.

**त्रिकालेऽपि** **तिष्ठति** — which is there in all three periods of time. So **त्रिकाल** can be looked at as what? Normally past, present, future. You can look another way too. What is past before it came into what it is now? Something is in a given form. Before it appeared in that form, what was it? That also is the same what is now also. And after this form, when it's not in that form, what is it? That's also the same. So — **अव्यक्तादीनि** **भूतानि** **व्यक्तमध्यानि** **भारत** — in a form it's not manifest, becomes manifest in a given form, and again becomes unmanifest. What are all these? One thing alone.

Therefore, to show **मन्तव्यम्**, to show logically that there is only one, what does **श्रुति** try to say? Look, what it was — **सृष्टि**, from that which in it came; **स्थिति**, that which sustains it when it appears as the world; and that into which the world, so-called world, resolves — all of that is one. So three examples for that.

So first one we saw is **स्थिति**. What is **स्थिति**? It showed that just like the sounds of the — the unique sounds, special sounds of a drum, of a conch, of a **वीणा** — cannot be cognized unless the general sound of the drum or the conch or the **वीणा** are cognized. Which means you look at the fact that anything is known only if it's **सामान्यम्**. **सामान्यम्** can also be taken as **स्वरूप**. Because without **स्वरूप** — that's what he's trying to say — without **स्वरूप**, you cannot know anything. But instead of — see, the way we look at the world is, oh, this is a drum and this is a special sound of a drum, etc. We look at it that way. So he's using that language, and says, without that you cannot know it.

That **सामान्यम्** — so you go from that **सामान्यम्** to **शब्दसामान्यम्**. First three sources of sound with **सामान्य** **विशेष**. Then **शब्द**, sound itself is **सामान्यम्**. Then sound itself is characteristic of mind, **बुद्धि**. Then **मनस्** to **बुद्धि**. **बुद्धि** to consciousness. And that you end there. Therefore, you can see the one in everything, **इति**. First was shown.

Second part we are looking at is now in a time of **सृष्टि**. Already presented. Like the sparks, the smoke, etc. are all nothing but fire. Being born of fire, so also this entire **जगत्** is nothing but that one reality. But how is he presenting it? He said, all this, you know, the mantras — and he said, **ब्राह्मण**, eight ways of looking at, all of them are coming from that **भगवान्** alone. **निःश्वसितम्**. This is what we were looking at. So this example is being shown to say everything is born of that. That much you understand. We will go back to the text. I will explain it further.
