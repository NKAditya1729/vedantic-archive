ॐ श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणानाम् आलयं करुणालयम्। नमामि भगवत्पादं शङ्करं लोकशङ्करम्॥ शङ्करं शङ्कराचार्यं केशवं बादरायणम्। सूत्रभाष्यकृतौ वन्दे भगवन्तौ पुनः पुनः॥ ईश्वरो गुरुरात्मेति मूर्तिभेदविभागिने। व्योमवद्व्याप्तदेहाय दक्षिणामूर्तये नमः॥ ॐ सह नाववतु। सह नौ भुनक्तु। सह वीर्यं करवावहै। तेजस्विनावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥ ॐ नमो ब्रह्मादिभ्यो ब्रह्मविद्यासंप्रदायकर्तृभ्यो वंशऋषिभ्यो नमो गुरुभ्यः॥

So here we looked at the **भाष्यम्** for the mantra 2.4.5, and we started looking at the **वार्तिक** — same ideas, but with a lot more elaboration. The section we were looking at last time is, why did **याज्ञवल्क्य** begin with, you know, a wife is not dear for the wife's sake. Why this statement? And the statements are meant to generate objectivity — generate **वैराग्यम्**. **वैराग्यम्** is objectivity. Because we are not trying to look at anything in a way which it is not. So we don't — see, we are very reasonable. We don't say anything is useless. But we say, we look at everything for what it really has. Anything — what it has, you see it as it has it, then it is objectivity. If you see something more or something less, that is your extra value addition. And wherever you love something, that love is extra value addition — because there is nothing in that object, that attribute called love or dearness. **प्रियत्वम्** is not in any object. This is hard to understand or accept. Because the only things we love are things other than ourselves, we think. And therefore, if I love all these things, and these things indeed are lovable, then we better love them. But if they are not lovable, we better know that. That's the whole idea here. Whatever we are going after, does it really have what I am going after? Am I really going after things for the sake of things? Or is my interest elsewhere? What do I want, really, in and through all my pursuits? And what's being said through these sentences is that what you really love is the pleased self. Pleased self — that "pleased," that extra, I'm adding, only because we think that it is the pleased self. **उपनिषद्** does not do that. It just says, **आत्मनस्तु** **कामाय** — **न** **तु** **प्रिय** **आत्मनस्तु** **कामाय**; Just **आत्मनस्तु** **कामाय**. So **आत्मा** is dear. Everything else really does not have dearness in it.

If you love to look at the mirror, what do you really love? The mirror? Suppose the mirror shows you a face which is not very pleasant — you won't like to look at it too much, or you will try to do something to it. But even when you look at your face in the mirror, you suddenly find that, oh, I forgot my **तिलकम्**. Where do you put the **तिलकम्**? On the mirror? Where do you put it? You put it here, and then it shows up there. Why? Why? Because you know that what you are looking at is your face. You are not looking at the mirror for the sake of the mirror. The mirror does not have anything beautiful about it. The mirror may be beautiful in its own way. But you are not looking at the mirror to look at the mirror. You want to see your beautiful face. And how many ever times you see it in a day, it's not enough. It always is nice to look at it. So when you look at it, you don't beautify that face — the face in the mirror. The mirror is not a necessity for your face to be there. The mirror cannot show you anything other than the face. But it can show you something other than the face — when it is distorted. When the mirror is not properly made. When the mirror is greasy or dirty. And let's say the mirror has got some curves on it — convexity, concavity — it is not going to show you your face as it is. And now, how much ever you try to change your face, still it looks different from what you want it to be. So you feel your face is too wide — then what should you do? Compress it? So the only way is to understand: this face has no problem. The problem is with this dumb mirror. And unless I recognize the problem belongs to the mirror, I'm always trying to go and find some other way to do it — to change something here.

So what you really love is yourself, and everything else is derived love. Because you think that you become okay when things around are okay. This idea has to be dismantled. The idea — the wrong idea — that everything else makes me happy, that everything else has what it takes to make me happy, to make me pleased, has to be dismantled. And dismantled at every level. Starting with the ones you love most — your family. Not that — please don't misunderstand this to say you should stop loving your family, okay? Don't have a fight at home, "I told you, you only love yourself, you don't love me." That's not the idea, okay? The idea is only to see clearly what is what. Alright. Nothing has to change on ground. It all has to change here. Okay. So with that background, let us enter back into the **वार्तिक**. So we saw a few of these verses last time. I will continue further now.

स्वत एवाप्रियोऽनात्मा ह्यात्मप्रीत्यर्थसाधनात्। जायादिः स्यात्प्रियो भक्त्या बन्धक्याः कामुको यथा॥ ८२ ॥

न कस्यांचिदवस्थायामात्मा लोकेऽप्रियो मतः। प्रियोऽप्रियश्च जायादिर्यथाऽनात्मा मुहुर्मुहुः॥ ८१ ॥

Correct. That's where I want to start. **न** **कस्यांचिदवस्थायाम्** **अपि।** **आत्मा** **लोके** **अप्रियो** **मतः।** **आत्मा** means me, I. See, sometimes we say **आत्मा** is dear — you may think **आत्मा** is something else. No, we are talking about you. **कस्यांचिदवस्थायाम्।** In any situation whatsoever **आत्मा** **न** **अप्रियो** **लोके।** You are never a source of displeasure to yourself, ever. Why this statement? Because you think, I am the source of my own problems. I wasted my time for the last five years. I didn't do anything useful. I spent all my youth uselessly. Whatever you think, okay? A lot of guilt we have. So this is — even if you think so, and you say, okay, you know what, I'm going to jump off a cliff, because life is not worth it. Even if you do that — so that people think it's self-hate. That is not self-hate. That is self-love. Because the conclusions you have made, based on seeing the mind, is that with this mind, with this situation, with this body, with this life, I cannot be happy anymore. I want to be. See, I love myself — the pleased me. And if the pleased me is not there, I would rather end my life and get a fresh chance if possible — come back, you know, with another body. So I want to end my life. Why? Because in this life I can't make it. I can't make it. "I can make it. I had made it. I can't make it." All this is based on what? The understanding that you know what it means to be okay. Therefore he is saying: **कस्यांचिदवस्थायाम्** in any situation — even the worst situation, when you feel suicidal — even there, you think that this **दुःख** which is there in the **आत्मा**, you want to put an end to. You want to put an end to the sorrow centered on yourself. How do you do that? "I don't see another way. Put an end to this body." Even that is an indication of love for your pleased you.

He says, therefore, **लोके** **अप्रियः** **आत्मा** is never true. It's always dear. **प्रियोऽप्रियश्च** **जायादिर्यथाऽनात्मा** **मुहुर्मुहुः।** **अनात्मा** — whatever is other than I — is what? **मुहुर्मुहुः** — again and again. What? **प्रियः**, **अप्रियः**, **प्रियः**, **अप्रियः।** Today — so nice, you are the best guy. Tomorrow — horrible fellow, why did you come to my life? Both are going on, okay? **मुहुर्मुहुः** — repeatedly. One day smile, one day frown. So — not only about people, with respect to things also. You know — "I love you." "Why do you love me?" What should be the right answer? What will be the answer? Typically somebody asks, "Why do you love me? Tell me, why do you love me?" "Oh, you are so kind to me." Okay. Five days later, "I don't like you at all. I hate you." "Why? Why?" "You are never kind to me." Which is true? Which is true? Both of them are untrue. It is true at that time, for you. Okay? When the person gives in to your demands, she is very kind, or he is very kind. When they don't give in to your demand, they will be unkind. Which is true? Your demand is the problem, okay? The person is also doing what they can. But based on your demand, they respond differently. So what's the true answer? "Why do you love me?" "Because you please me." "Why do you not love me?" "Because you don't please me." That's the answer. Finally, that's the answer. But do you really please me, or displease me? No — you are also changing. Therefore pleasure and displeasure are changing. That's part of the story.

Suppose I am unchanged. You know — so now you have a situation. You have your house, or you bought a car, and the car you really love. Two days later, you don't love it. Did the car change? What changed? Your demands of the car changed. Are your demands ever fixed? They are ever-varying. Flavor of the day, flavor of the moment — they change. Is that you? Or do you know the change in demands? Therefore **शास्त्र** is being very objective. What do you really love? You really love a demand-free you. An agenda-free you. Is agenda a part of you? If it were a part of you, you cannot be free from it. What you can be free from is what is not inherent to you. That's the truth. Therefore, all the demands you have are extra baggage. And extra baggage you recognize as extra baggage — that's all. If you carry extra baggage, you have to pay for it. This is the rule, okay? For every kilo, you will pay. **भगवान्** allows a free allowance — zero free allowance, here. He says, you travel without any checked baggage. This is your hand baggage, okay? This body is your hand baggage — body, mind. You have to travel without any checked baggage. Any checked baggage, you have to pay. You decide — you decide whether you want to. "No, I need the baggage." You're going to go to a beautiful, you know, five-star hospitality, where everything is given to you. Why do you need to carry extra baggage? "No, no, I need extra baggage." Then you carry, and pay for it. So that idea has to be conveyed.

So what he is saying — he is making it clear that, you know, in no time are you displeased with yourself, or not dear to yourself. Whereas everything in the world is constantly a yo-yo, a seesaw. Like what? **जायादिर्यथा।** So his example is what? This is dear, this is not dear — again, again. Like what? Like your own spouse. Now he is dear, now he is not dear. Now she is dear, now she is not dear. That's the truth.

**स्वत** **एवाप्रियोऽनात्मा** **ह्यात्मप्रीत्यर्थसाधनात्।** **जायादिः** **स्यात्प्रियो** **भक्त्या** **बन्धक्याः** **कामुको** **यथा।** So now he says— look, if **अनात्मा** is always **अप्रिय**, generally **अप्रिय**, **आत्मा** alone is **प्रिय**, then **अनात्मा** cannot make **आत्मा** **प्रिय**. You know, if it is **अप्रिय**, it is **अप्रिय** totally. So the answer is being given. **स्वत** **एव** **अप्रियः** **अनात्मा।** **अनात्मा** by itself is always **अप्रिय**. Always **अप्रिय**, but when I like it, it is **प्रिय**? No — it is always **अप्रिय**. Then what is happening now? **आत्मप्रीत्यर्थसाधनात्।** You yourself said what? The wife is dear for the pleased self, right? The husband is dear for the pleased self. Therefore it is a means to make me happy. The person, or the relation, or the thing, is a means to make me happy. Therefore it has some **प्रीति** in it, or not? It must have something in it. Otherwise, why does it make me happy? He says, it is **भक्त्या**. **भक्ति** here is not devotion, okay? **भक्ति** means **गौणी**. **गौणम्** means secondary. It's not primary. Like you say, "Oh, this boy is like a lion." The lion-ness does not belong to the boy, really. You clearly see that there is bravery in the boy, and therefore you say **सिंहो** **ऽयं** **माणवकः**. It doesn't mean anything except that you are giving value based on something else — an attribute of the boy. But he is not a lion. So when you say **अनात्मा** is dear, it is purely figurative. Even when we say **अनात्मा** — wife is dear for the sake of the pleased self — wife is not dear even there. That is also purely **गौणम्** figurative. It's not real. That's what he is saying. So he gives an example, which is — you know, suppose there is a woman who lives by attracting men. Are those people really dear to her? Or is she really dear to them? It is all figurative.

निर्हैतुकी स्वतः प्रीतिरात्मन्येव यतस्ततः। भाक्तं प्रियं परित्यज्य मुख्यं प्रियमुपाश्रयेत्॥ ८३ ॥

**हेतु** is a cause. **निर्हेतु** is what? **निर्गताः** **हेतवः** **यस्मात्** — all causes have gone from which — that is **निर्हेतु**. **निर्हेतुक** is **निर्हेतु** only; **निर्हैतुकी** is feminine. So **स्वतः** **प्रीतिः** **आत्मन्येव** — the love you have for yourself is without reason. Why do you love yourself? Why are you pleased? "Oh, because my son got admission to a college." Why are you pleased? "Because my daughter is getting married." Forget it. Reason — there is no reason. If you think you have a reason to be happy, you will have the same reason to make you unhappy. You know — today, one year ago, this was such a wonderful day; today it all changed. One year has changed so much. Why? It all changed, I don't know — but if you are happy, why it all changed, it's good; if you are unhappy, it's a huge problem. Right? So he says — **निर्हैतुकी**. It's a very important thing. Why do you love a wife? Because she invokes the pleased self. Why do you love yourself? For no reason. Why are you happy? "Because everything is going well." Wrong answer. Incorrect means not analyzed. I am happy because happiness I am. There is no reason why I should be unhappy.

See — Swamiji would say, suppose you see a person who is laughing, or is smiling. Generally you don't go about asking, "Why are you happy? Why are you happy?" You generally don't go about asking — sometimes you do. But suppose that person has a long face, you can see that he or she has cried — do you ask, generally, or not? "What happened? Why are you sad? Why are you crying? What happened?" You don't ask this, generally — anybody you know well, if they are in tears, if they seem to have a long face, you ask, "What happened? What happened?" But suppose somebody is smiling and happily moving around — you don't ask, "Hey, why are you happy today? Why are you happy today?" You don't ask. Why? Why do you not ask? Because you know that happiness doesn't require reason. I mean — extra exaltation requires reason. But just being cheerful does not require reason. So the **वार्तिककार** is making it clear: **निर्हैतुकी** **स्वतः** **प्रीतिः**. There is no reason why you love yourself. Because you are lovable. Love is your truth. Love means acceptance — self-acceptance. **आत्मन्येव** **यतः** — since this love for yourself is natural, without any cause, uncaused — therefore, what should you do? **भाक्तं** **प्रियं** **परित्यज्य** **मुख्यं** **प्रियमुपाश्रयेत्।** Give up this object through which you are trying to derive that happiness. Okay — happiness is you. Now you are trying to derive it from something — from a dog, from a cat, from a tree, from a person. Don't do that. This is like a **विधि**, you know. **भाक्तं** **प्रियम्** — this figurative happiness which has — you give that up, and establish yourself in **मुख्यं** **प्रियम्**, what is really dear to you. Why should I do so?

क्षयान्ता निचयाः सर्वे पतनान्ताः समुच्छ्रयाः। संयोगा विप्रयोगान्ता मरणान्तं च जीवितम्॥ ८४ ॥

Very, very well-known quotation — especially the second part:
