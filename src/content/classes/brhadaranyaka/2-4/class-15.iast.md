oṃ śrutismṛtipurāṇānām ālayaṃ karuṇālayam| namāmi bhagavatpādaṃ śaṅkaraṃ lokaśaṅkaram|| śaṅkaraṃ śaṅkarācāryaṃ keśavaṃ bādarāyaṇam| sūtrabhāṣyakṛtau vande bhagavantau punaḥ punaḥ|| īśvaro gururātmeti mūrtibhedavibhāgine| vyomavadvāptadehāya dakṣiṇāmūrtaye namaḥ|| oṃ saha nāvavatu| saha nau bhunaktu| saha vīryaṃ karavāvahai| tejasvināvad hītamastu mā vidviṣāvahai|| oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ|| oṃ namo brahmādibhyo brahmavidyāsaṃpradāyakṛtbhyo vaṃśaṛṣibhyo namo gurubhyaḥ||

We are in the midst of the **maitreyī** **brāhmaṇam**, where the question has been asked by **maitreyi** — teach me that which gives **amṛtatvam**. And **yājñavalkya** began by saying things which are intended to make a person objective. Recognise that what you really love is only the pleased self. Everything that one does in life is because you want something that is dear to you. And anything is dear to you not for its own sake, but only for your own sake. And that "you" happens to be already pleased — you better know that. That's the idea. You better know that.

Therefore you should — this, the real you, the you who is free from a need to become different — needs to be recognised. **draṣṭavyaḥ**. There's nothing more to be done. But to recognise, you have to do things. To know, though knowing appears to be doing — knowing is what? **vastutantra** — it is dependent on the object. Knowing depends on what you want to know. Knowing does not depend on your wish. Whatever we know is determined by what it is that we are trying to know. If I am knowing the sun, I will know it as a sun. If I am knowing a star, I will know it as a star. I cannot see a star and know it as a sun. So knowledge is true to what is to be known. And what about **karma**? Karma is **kartṛtantra** — it is true to what you want to do. It depends on you. What you want to accomplish depends on you. Something unaccomplished, to be accomplished, depends on you. But what is already a fact, which is to be known, depends on what is.

So the statement is — **ātmā** **vā** **are** **draṣṭavyaḥ**. And for that, what should you do? **śrotavyaḥ**, **mantavyaḥ**, **nididhyāsitavya**: What will happen if I do this much? You will know. Then what will happen? Then everything will become **ātmā** for you — there will be nothing but **ātmā**. That's the way **ātmā** has to be known — not as an individual standing apart. This much has been said. Now what we are doing, since a couple of classes, we are going into more detail as to how the **vārtikakāra** presents it.

So last time we saw how **vairāgyam** is important. That was presented — saying, whatever begins will end. Whenever somebody climbs up, there will be a downfall. So you look at what happens: any change is subject to further change. Therefore understand that anything you do, accomplish, etc. — it doesn't matter what you accomplish, that accomplishment will wither away in time. Whatever is accomplished in time will disintegrate in time. Therefore understand that any attempt to change, any attempt to bring something, to accomplish something new — it's good, it's fine, but that can't be the last word. Because what is subject to change will change again. Therefore have **vairāgyam** towards trying to accomplish anything new.

And not accomplish — what is it? You think that by accomplishing this, I will become okay. By this relationship I will become okay. This will keep me secure. This idea is not true. Because again and again you notice in your own life — you examine your own life — again and again we see that we thought something would solve my problems, I will be okay after that. But it's like an ever‑moving target. And the world promotes this, because that's the way they make you do what they want you to do right now.

Okay, come on — you are enjoying life, playing all the time happily. Suddenly the 10th standard comes. And then you are told, come on, very important — study, study. One year you study, after that you will be good. Very untrue, you know. But we are all taken for a ride. Then we study hard. Then what happens now? 10th you did so well. Now you set a new standard for yourself. Now you have to keep up that standard. 12th also you will do equally well. And what your mother and father couldn't accomplish, they want you to accomplish — because, you know, they didn't do very well sometimes. So they say, you should become a physician. Why? Because I didn't become one. I want to enjoy myself by making you one. Now you are set up. You struggle hard. Just two more years, okay — after that you will have the whole life to enjoy. Two more years you study, then what happens? You are somehow made to a college — are you going to just walk out of the college without doing anything? No. Somehow you study for four years, you finish that. Then what happens? Are you done now? No, no. Now you have to get an MD seat. You study some more. Are you done with that? No, no. Now you have to do post‑MD, or get a job. Where does it end? We are on a permanent, eternal trip of becoming better. That is called **saṃsāra**.

Until I am happy to keep on seeing my growth and keep doing better, it works well. But at some point you hit a plateau. You recognise that whatever I do, it's like the horse running to get that grass which is tied in front of its face. Poor thing — I don't know if it's so foolish, but that's how human beings think the horse runs, because it wants to reach that grass in front, and the grass is always maintained at that distance, therefore it is always running. This is how life tends to work.

Therefore — anything that you depend upon for you to become okay, that dependency will not give you what you are really seeking. Therefore you don't love a wife for the wife's sake — you love her for your own sake. You don't love a husband for the husband's sake — you love him for your own sake. You don't love children for the children's sake — you love them for your own selfish sake. You don't love wealth for wealth's sake — you love wealth because you think that is what makes you happy. Finally, it is you. You don't love **loka**, experiences, for the experience's sake — you love them for your own sake. Even if you want to experience some feeling of high, whatever it might be. So if everything is for your own sake — who is this very demanding "you"? You try to know it, and you find it is non‑demanding. This is the thing. So this **vairāgyam** we have seen.

Now the next word that is going to be looked at is — **ātmā** **vā** **are** **draṣṭavyaḥ**. So what is this **ātmā**? Last time, towards the end, we saw — everybody knows **ātmā**. Who doesn't know oneself? Anybody here who doesn't know oneself, who has come to class? You are, and you want to do something — that's why you came. You come first. Before you do anything, before you know anything, you are. Is there a doubt? Nobody has a doubt. Therefore — why do I need the **veda** to come and tell me **ātmā** should be known? Give me a break, I know. Therefore, this **ātmā** should be known — what **ātmā** is to be known? First question.

I, the wanting fellow, have to know — yes, that I am wanting. I am a wanting fellow, that's what I need to know. That is the **draṣṭavya**? Can't be. Therefore, what did **sureśvara** say? It cannot be the known **ātmā** that you want to know — no **veda** is required to come and tell you that. It is the unknown **ātmā**. The unknown "I"? Yes. The unknown you. That is what has to be known.

So there is an unknown me? Yes. There is an unknown you. Known‑you and unknown‑you are fused. There is no two you's — there is only one fused you. A confused you. That is called **tādātmyam**. There is no second thing. There is only a wanting me. There is no "sometimes I become free from want" — generally I am wanting. This is the only "I" I know. So when I am wanting, I am wanting only; when I am not wanting, I am not wanting only. There is only one me, fused — and that fused is confused. Because, yes, there is only one thing — but the only one thing that is, is the free you. That's not how I see it.

So first we have to make — we have to put a wedge — to show there is "you" what you think you are, and there is something more about you. That is the first step of **viveka**. You have to separate what you think you are, this personality, from something which is more real than the personality. Then — what is more real than the personality? And finally you say, there is no personality, there is only that, that's all there is. This is the whole journey. But to make that split, and to be convinced that the personality is depending upon the real me — that the whole universe depends on me — is a big leap we made.

You can't — suppose you want to jump over to **laṅka** like **hanumān** **jī**. You better be sure that when you jump, you will land in **laṅka**. Otherwise you will fall in between. You can't say, half I will jump, I will jump in two steps. First step — where will the first step be? You will be in the ocean. Then you cannot have a second step. Therefore you must be able to jump. But here we cannot jump like that, because the leap is too big. There is a method to it — because you already are in **laṅka**. You just have to know "I am already in **laṅka**, I don't need to jump." That's the method.

So now what we have to see is — what is this **draṣṭavyaḥ**? The section we are going to look at now — it may not look immediately relevant sometimes, but we have to see how the discussion is relevant. Because the question is this: how do you accomplish this **draṣṭavyaḥ**? Is it a **vidhi**, an injunction? Injunction means a command — do it, do it, know it. The general thinking is, anything to be accomplished, you need to put an effort into it, you need to work for it. Work only produces what is not there. A pleased you is not there right now — if he were there, he wouldn't come to this class. Therefore the pleased you has to be gained. By what? By doing something.

Even now, look at our own mind: "Swami, what should I do for this **mokṣa**? What should I do?" No, you don't have to do anything, you are already free. "No, no, what should I do?" You have to know. "You told me I know it already." No, that is not knowing. So this "what should I do" business is a disease we all suffer from. It's a universal disease, because we only know one thing to get things done — do. What is already there, I don't need to do. I will not go and ask **bhagavān**, give me a hand with five fingers. If I want, I'll ask for a sixth finger — give me one extra thumb on this side also, then I have two thumbs, very nice, it'll be interesting. I can ask that. But I cannot go and tell **bhagavān**, give me a hand with five fingers — I already have one. I can say, give me an extra hand with five fingers. So you need to understand what you can ask for, what you can accomplish, and what is already accomplished. If something is already accomplished, it has to be known.

But the universal thinking is — anything in life, even small ends, you have to work to accomplish. **karma** is the means to accomplish. So if you want to have one house of your own, what do you need? You need to do, you need to accomplish it. Suppose you need a second house, a farmhouse — you need to work even harder. Suppose you need five houses — work even harder. Suppose you want to become a minister — work harder. Suppose you want to become the Prime Minister — it's not going to just fall in your lap, you need to work for it. So the more difficult the accomplishment, the higher the accomplishment, the more you need to work for it. Logically correct or not? Therefore, if you want to gain everything in the universe — how much work should you do? Infinite. Now, that infinite is too much. So give me an **upāya**, a trick by which I can reduce the work and get it done. So the **veda** will give you some trick — this is the way we look at it.

So these things are all true within a certain realm. Within the finite‑mathematics world, finite‑mathematics principles work — 1 plus 1 is 2, 2 plus 1 is 3, this is finite mathematics. What is infinite mathematics? Infinite plus 1 is infinite. No addition. You can't apply the rules that work in the realm of finite math to infinites — won't work. What works in Newtonian physics won't work in quantum physics — a paradigm shift. The paradigm shift here is — what you take yourself to be is not what you are. That's the paradigm shift. If you want to retain your identity and get better, that's within the paradigm. We don't know of another paradigm, and therefore there is no understanding of a paradigm shift. Therefore we try to fit in the paradigm shift into the current paradigm, and when you try to do that, you get frustrated, because it doesn't give you what the paradigm shift will give.

Understand the importance of **vidhi**. The idea is, the **veda** is a book of means and ends. It has to tell me new means and new ends, which I cannot know within my range of perception. And when it gives me a new end, I must apply the means — if unknown means are given, I must apply them to get that end. And if it's a known end but I have a new trick, a new means to get there, I should apply that. No effort, no result; no work, no pay. Therefore — what should be done? Tell me, tell me what should be done. That is the fellow. That's where we are, generally. See whether we fit there or not. What are we trying to do to gain this freedom — trying to do, or trying to just know? Where do we stand? That's going to be the discussion. I hope you will also enjoy this discussion.

So we saw this last time —

ātmā pratyakṣaprasiddheḥ syāt tatraiva ātmānubhūtitaḥ| iti prameyanirdeśo draṣṭavya iti tatpramā||

ātmabuddhiriyaṃ tāvat sarveṣāṃ jāyate svataḥ| aprāptā sarvātmatā tveti saivāto dhīrvidhīyate॥

So — **ātmabuddhiḥ** **iyam** **tavat**, this sense of "I", **sarveṣāṃ** **jāyate** **svataḥ**, for everybody it is there inherently, naturally. There is nothing to be known there. That is true, you know yourself. But you know yourself as an isolated individual. **aprāptā** **sarvātmatā** — "all that is here is me is **aprāptā**, **ajñātā**” therefore, **sā** **eva** **ataḥ** **dhīḥ** **vidhīyate** — that alone (that knowledge) is being enjoined here.

Now —

draṣṭṛdraṣṭavyayorbhede satyevaṃ dhīrvidhīyate| niyojyaviṣayābhede ghaṭate na vidhiryataḥ॥ 97 ॥

When can you give a command, that you need to change, that you need to accomplish something? **sureśvara** says: **draṣṭṛ**‑**draṣṭavyayoḥ** **bhede** **sati** — if there is a gap - if what is to be accomplished is totally different from the accomplisher **draṣṭa** the seer (**draṣṭṛ**) I am using the word "accomplish" because the question is in that frame: when can you mandate that something must be done by you? When the doer is separated from what he will accomplish by doing.

So now — what is the word? **draṣṭavyaḥ**. It means "should be seen." That's it. The **tavya**‑**pratyaya**, language‑wise, can mean a command, an injunction. And the **pūrvamīmāṃsaka** will argue that **tavya** means it's a **vidhi**, a command, an injunction — because it wants me to do something. He is very committed to saying the **veda** only tells me to do or not do; there is no business of just knowing. Therefore, this gap — **draṣṭṛ**‑**draṣṭavyayoḥ** **bhede** **sati** if there is a gap between the two **evaṃ** **dhī** **vidhīyate** — then you can have an injunction, "you should know.” **niyojya**‑**viṣaya**‑**abhede** **vidhiḥ** **na** **ghaṭate** **vidhiryataḥ** But where what is to be mandated and what is already there have no gap between them — no space, no differentiation between the knower and what is to be known — there cannot be a **vidhi**.

So what?

**uttarottarabhūyāṃsi** **nāmādīni** **yathākramam||** **upanyasyā''tmayāthātmyaṃ** **tattatvaṃ** **prābravīcchrutiḥ||** **98** **||** Now **sureśvara** is taking us to **chāndogya**, **saptama** **adhyāya** — the seventh chapter. There **sanatkumāra** is approached by **nārada**. **nārada** says what? Sir, I have heard that the knower of **ātmā** is free from sadness - **ātmavit** **śokaṃ** **tarati**, the knower of **ātmā** crosses sorrow; so please teach me that. **sanatkumāra** says — first tell me what you know, then I will teach you; because I need to know where you are to progress. Progress means - were we on **mantra** 2.4.5 or 2.4.6 I need to know before I progress. Let me know how much you have studied; what do you know? **nārada** said — you want to hear, okay — and he started, and he won't stop. He goes on and on — **ṛgvedaḥ**, **yajurvedaḥ**, **sāmavedaḥ**, **atharvavedaḥ**, **chandaḥ**, **śikṣā**, **kalpaḥ**, **niruktam**, **jyotiṣam**, **nakṣatravidyā**, **kṣatravidyā** — going on and on. After he finishes — five minutes he took — **sanatkumāra** said, is that all you know? "Yes sir, that's all I know." And that me, who knows all this — **so'haṃ** **bhagavo** **śocāmi** — that kind of me, O sir, is grieving, is unhappy, is wanting, still wanting. What should you do? **śokasya** **pāraṃ** **tārayatu** — help me cross over this ocean of sorrow.

**sanatkumāra** says — what you know is only **nāmamātram**. You only know names; you don't know the thing. So now he has to make a big leap, and that big leap, in that **upaniṣad**, is not done abruptly — it is done in a particular way. There he first gives an **upāsanā** based on **nāma**; from **nāma** he goes to the next step, and like this he keeps going — from 15, 20 steps he goes. Saying, what do you want to know next? You have known names — name after name after name. Now what do you want to know next? Because knowing a name won't help you. He doesn't ask him — he tells him. So he is taking him through a progression. Then he has to present the limitless:

uttarottarabhūyāṃsi nāmādīni yathākramam| upanyasyā''tmayāthātmyaṃ tattatvaṃ prābravīcchrutiḥ॥ 98 ॥

**uttarottara**‑**bhūyāṃsi** **nāmādīni** **yathākramam** **upanyasya** - **uttarottara** — in a sequence one after the other; **upanyasya** — having presented them; **nāmādīni** — beginning with **nāma**; **yathākramam** — in the right sequence; **ātma**‑**yāthātmyam** **tat**‑**tattvam** **prābravīt** **śrutiḥ** — then he described what you are really to **nārada** the **śruti** did that through **sanatkumāra**, the teacher.

bhūmnaśca lakṣaṇaṃ cakre yatra nānyaditi svayam| atha yatreti cālpasya prāṇāntasya ca lakṣaṇam॥ 99 ॥

**cakre** - “did” - **kṛ**-**dhātu** - **lṛṭ** **lakār** - **ātmanepada** . **bhūmna**: **lakṣaṇaṃ** **cakre** - Then what did **śruti** do? It gave **bhūmā** **lakṣaṇam** - **bhūmā** is what? **bhūmā** is **brahman** because that **imanic** **pratyaya** will come and then **bahu** will become **bhumā** - **bahuvat** **bhāva**: **bhuman**. Therefore, **bhūmā** means the limitlessly big, the limitless whole, **brahman**. **bhūmnaśca** **lakṣaṇaṃ** **cakre** as what? **nānyat** **iti** **svayam** - What is this **nānyat** business we will look at.

Okay, we are doing **bṛhadāraṇyaka**, but now we will do **chāndogya**.

yatra nānyatpaśyati nānyacchṛṇoti nānyadvijānāti sa bhūmātha yatrānyatpaśyatyanyacchṛṇotyanyadvijānāti tadalpaṃ yo vai bhūmā tadamṛtamatha yadalpaṃ tanmartyam̐ sa bhagavaḥ kasminpratiṣṭhita iti sve mahimni yadi vā na mahimnīti॥ 1 ॥ (chā 7.24.1)

Scene change. So now, what happens? **yatra** **nānyatpaśyati** **nānyacchṛṇoti** **nānyadvijānāti** **sa** **bhūmā** — what are we trying to show? Keep the context in view. The first statement is: a **vidhi** is only possible when what is to be accomplished is different from the accomplisher. Now, what do we want to show? What does **sureśvara** want to show?
