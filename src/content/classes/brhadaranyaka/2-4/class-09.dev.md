ॐ श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणानाम् आलयं करुणालयम्। नमामि भगवत्पादं शङ्करं लोकशङ्करम्॥ शङ्करं शङ्कराचार्यं केशवं बादरायणम्। सूत्रभाष्यकृतौ वन्दे भगवन्तौ पुनः पुनः॥ ईश्वरो गुरुरात्मेति मूर्तिभेदविभागिने। व्योमवद्व्याप्तदेहाय दक्षिणामूर्तये नमः॥ ॐ सह नाववतु। सह नौ भुनक्तु। सह वीर्यं करवावहै। तेजस्विनावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥ ॐ नमो ब्रह्मादिभ्यो ब्रह्मविद्यासंप्रदायकर्तृभ्यो वंशऋषिभ्यो नमो गुरुभ्यः॥
We are in the beginning of the **मैत्रेयी ब्राह्मण**, where **याज्ञवल्क्य** approached **मैत्रेयी** and said, "I want to take **संन्यास**, I want to move on from **गृहस्थाश्रम**, I will divide the wealth between you and **कात्यायनी** so you are in good shape." And she asked, "will wealth give me immortality, **मोक्ष** you are seeking?" "It won't." "Then why do you want to, you know, give it to me and go? Give me that after which you are going, give me that knowledge which you are after, that knowledge you share with me. Don't just give me some wealth which is not really wealth.” When she said this, **याज्ञवल्क्य** was very pleased with her, and he said, "if I didn't recognize this is what you wanted — I was unsure. Come, you please come, I will teach you."
Then the teaching begins. The teaching begins with what? Not by saying **तत् त्वम् असि**— where does it begin? **न वा अरे पत्युः कामाय पतिः प्रियो भवत्यात्मनस्तु कामाय पतिः प्रियो भवति** । So the whole series saying — a husband is not dear to the wife for the husband's sake. The husband is dear to the wife for her own sake. The wife is not dear to the husband for the wife's sake. The wife is dear to the husband for his own sake. Money is not dear before money. **पुत्र**। Children are not dear to the person for the children's sake. The children are dear to the person for his or her own sake. Wealth is not dear to a person for wealth's sake. Wealth is dear to the person for his or her own sake. Nothing is dear to a person for its own sake. Everything is dear to a person for his own sake. Therefore — you should know yourself. That's the beginning. The opening.
So if you see now — why should he say all this? Why don't you begin with what needs to be said? Why all this fluff? Even in **कठोपनिषद्**, you know, **यमराज** goes on and on — "**नचिकेता**, you are such a wonderful fellow, you are so brilliant, I was not like you. You know, I went after **कर्म**s. You are so brilliant. When I'm giving you everything on a platter, you say I don't want." **कर्मफल**। "How brilliant you are!" **नचिकेता** tells him, "enough, enough of all that. Tell me what really matters." Here also — is it all fluff? Why is the **उपनिषद्** talking about, you know, this — that the wife is not dear for the wife's sake, the husband is not dear for the husband's sake? Why all this is being said? Is there a reason for it?
So **भाष्यकार** says yes, there is a reason for it. What is the reason? Because this pursuit is a very jealous pursuit. This **ब्रह्मन्** pursuit is a pursuit that cannot handle anybody else you're going after. If you're going after anything else, then you go after that. If you want to come after me, become like **राम** —**एक्पत्नीव्रता** at the time when everybody had many wives, as a king he could have had many wives, like his father. But what did he say? "**सीता** will be my only wife." So also — if you are interested in many things, you go after them. If you are interested only in **ब्रह्मन्**, then you come to me. That's what **ब्रह्मन्** will say. Why is **ब्रह्मन्** insecure? That you are afraid. Why we don’t want somebody to relate with other people? It is because we are insecure. Like I told you earlier maybe in **पञ्चदशी** or somewhere. A person wants to get married. He wants to get married to a beautiful person or not a beautiful person? He has problem in both sides. If he marries a beautiful person, he is worried that other people will become interested in her. She will become interested in others. Others will become interested in her. And if he marries a not beautiful person, he is not interested in her. What to do now? So our pursuits are all dependent upon our insecurity.
So the question is — what is the purpose of saying this here? If indeed what you're going after is one thing, but what you're really after is another thing, then the difference between the two must be recognized. If you're really going after a career — or are you really going after whatever you think the career will give you? If you're going after a relationship for the sake of the relationship, or are you going after it as a means for something else? Do you do anything for the sake of itself? Or are you doing it for some other purpose? If you're doing something for some other purpose, you better recognize that. And once you recognize that what you're really after is something else, then why would you go after the things you're going after?
So I go after a hundred things. Because these hundred things capture my mind. You know — **व्यवसायात्मिका बुद्धि**: **एका**, **गीता**, second chapter. **व्यवसायात्मिका बुद्धिरेकेह कुरुनन्दन** —**व्यवसायात्मिका - निश्चयात्मिका बुद्धि**: - that I am only after one thing, **मोक्ष**। If that is not known, what happens? **बहुशाखा ह्यनन्ताश्च बुद्धयोऽव्यवसायिनाम्**, many branches. Many branches of what? Many branches of infinite things — **बहुशाखा** : **हि अनन्ता**: **बुद्धय**:। Where is all your interest in your **बुद्धि**? In your thinking? You are interested in a million things. Countless things. Because you don't know what's your next interest. **बहुशाखाः अनन्ताः**। For whom? **अव्यवसायिनाम्**। One who has not come to a clear determination as to what is it that they really want. If you know what you really want, then alone, the value for things which are after — not knowing what you really are after — will drop. Therefore, why should he say all this? So that you get some freedom, some space from all these relationships you have.
Relationship I'm using in a wider sense here. Your relationship with wealth also — the relationship, okay? Your relationship with the job is also a relationship. Your relationship with entrepreneurship is also a relationship. Your relationship with, you know, your club membership is also a relationship. Its all relationships - one-sided. Some relationships seem  two-sided and some relationships seem to be one-sided. Two-sided because the other fellow also responds to you. One-sided is where you tell, "oh this car really, it's my life" — the car doesn't say "you are my life", okay? It's happy to go with somebody else. Therefore, it's all relationships. And if you think this relationship with this is what makes me what I want be, then you will go after them. If you understand this relationship is only a means for me for something else, then you understand what you're really after is something else. And if you understand what you're really after is something else, then go for it directly.
You want to hold your nose — hold your nose, I say — and you say "I can't hold my nose.” (Trying to hold your nose round your neck). This is what we are trying to do all the time. "Hold your nose." "I can't." "Hey, you can hold like this, directly. You don't have to go around your head to hold your nose." "No, no, that's the only way I can hold my nose." Wrong. Going around the head is not important. It's called **द्राविड प्राणायाम**। It's not really Drāviḍa or anything. **द्राविड**, in South India they do **प्राणायाम** like that — not true, okay? But talked like that, apparently, in the north.
Okay, so now, why is all this being said? **वैराग्यार्थम्**। Wait — what is **वैराग्यम्**? Not turning your face away — that's not **वैराग्यम्**। I can turn my face away because some sight is very unseemly, you know — I see some scene which is unpleasant - so I am walking infant of a shop which is selling meat and I turn my face away.  I turn my face away because I cannot handle the stench or the looks. There is **द्वेष** for it in a way. **द्वेष** is not **वैराग्यम्**। Dislike for something is not **वैराग्यम्**. **वैराग्यम्** is dispassion. You see thing for what it is. You are objective. What is objectivity? Seeing a thing as it is. **यथाभूतदर्शनम्**। And that is the reason for **वैराग्य**। **यथाभूतदर्शनम्** is the cause for **वैराग्यम्**। It is the cause for objectivity. Otherwise it is one more dislike.
स होवाच — अमृतत्वसाधनं वैराग्यमुपदिदिक्षुः जायापतिपुत्रादिभ्यो विरागमुत्पादयति तत्सन्न्यासाय ।

So here, **शङ्कर** is saying **स होवाच याज्ञवल्क्य**: the teacher, the husband, he said — What did he say? It will come a little later. Why did he say? **शङ्कर** — **अमृतत्वसाधनं वैराग्यम् उपदिदिक्षुः**। **उपदिदिक्षुः**। What is **दिदिक्षुः**? What is **दिदिक्षुः**? I don't know — some shoe or something? No. **उपदेष्टुम् इच्छुः** — desiring to instruct. Desiring to teach. Wishing to teach. Wishing to teach what?
What did **मैत्रेयी** ask for? **मैत्रेयी** said that **येनाहं नामृता स्यां किमहं तेन कुर्यां** - if I am not going to get immortality with this  wealth you are going to leave behind for me **किमहं तेन कुर्यां** - what would I do with it? **यदेव भगवान्वेद तदेव मे ब्रूहि** -  What the respected, revered sir knows - as what - **तदेव-यदेव** - what is that? **यदेव केवलम् अमृतत्व-साधनं** That which will give me a total freedom from all sense of lack — **अमृतत्व-साधनम्**। That is called immortality here. Not living in this body forever. **तदेव**। Again, **भाष्यकार** doesn't leave it. **अमृतत्व-साधनम्**। That **अमृतत्व-साधनम्** — that alone, which you know. **तदेव**। That alone. Nothing else. Don't give me fluff. **तदेव मे ब्रूहि**। Teach me that."
And then what did he say? "Oh, you are so dear to me. **एहि** - Please come" — see how beautiful it is. "**एहि** - Please come. **आस्स्व** Please sit down. **व्याख्यास्यामि ते** I will teach you. Listen to me, who is going to talk about it, with attention. **व्याचक्षाणस्य तु मे निदिध्यासस्वेति** " What will he teach him? **व्याख्यास्यामि**। Again **भाष्यकार** doesn't leave. **ते तव इष्टम् अमृतत्व-साधनं ज्ञानं कथयिष्यामि**। "I'm only going to teach you **अमृतत्व-साधनम्**।" So it should be very clear by the time you arrive here — **याज्ञवल्क्य** is expected to teach only **अमृतत्व-साधनम्**, not anything else. **साधनम्** a means — **अमृतत्व** means freedom, immortality; **अमृतत्व-साधनम्** means means for immortality. That's what he has to teach. "Listen to me." And then he begins.
And when he begins, he says — **स होवाच** — **अमृतत्वसाधनं** before — what is that? Swamiji, last, when we just saw **ज्ञानम्**— he was going to teach knowledge. Why here **वैराग्यम्** ? Why changed ? In between mind changed or what? No, without **वैराग्यम्**, **अमृतत्व-साधनम्** will not enter the head. Therefore **अमृतत्व-साधनम्** — this is not direct **साधनम्**, this is indirect **साधनम्**, it is **साधनाय साधनम्**। **अमृतत्व-साधनाय यत् साधन**। So you want knowledge. For knowledge you need preparedness. Otherwise knowledge cannot happen. And therefore, when you are after something, if that needs another means, that becomes a **साधनम्**।
Now, for **वैराग्य** what do you need? Suppose you want to get objectivity. See, **वैराग्यम्** is needed for **ब्रह्मज्ञानम्**। For **वैराग्य** what do you need? You need **विवेक**। **विवेक वैराग्य शमादिषट्कसम्पत्ति**। For **विवेक** what do you need? What is **विवेक**? Proper understanding of what is what. That is what he is doing. First is **पुरुषार्थ विवेक**। You know what is **पुरुषार्थ**? **पुरुषैः सर्वैः अर्थ्यते इति पुरुषार्थः** — that which is universally desired, strived for by all of humanity. No exception. Every human being strives for which, generally, is called **पुरुषार्थ**। Exception will always be there, okay? So what does everybody strive for? Security. **अर्थ** is a **पुरुषार्थ**। Everybody strives to fulfil some desires. **काम** is a **पुरुषार्थ**। Desire is **काम**। If you are in a Vedic life, **धर्म** is a **पुरुषार्थ**। Otherwise it is not. But even unknowingly, humanity is also seeking **धर्म** — unknown to them. Because without **धर्म**, you will have internal conflict. You cannot enjoy anything you have if you have an internal conflict. Therefore, without that guilt, without a conscience biting you — if you want to enjoy something, **धर्म** has to be a **पुरुषार्थ**। You may lose sight of it for a while, but you can't lose sight of it forever. Then through and in and through all this, what you're really seeking is freedom from a sense of wanting, smallness. Therefore **मोक्ष** is definitely a **पुरुषार्थ** for everybody whether you recognize it or not. If you don't recognise it, you go around like this (reaching for the nose around the neck). You recognise it, you go direct. That's the difference between a **मुमुक्षु** and a non-**मुमुक्षु**। If you recognise "what I'm really after", you go after that alone. If you don't recognise, you go after **अर्थ-काम-धर्म**। **धर्म** you leave out, **अर्थ-काम** predominantly. **धर्म** is a bridge to **मोक्ष**, therefore that becomes a **साधनम्**।
So here — **अमृतत्व-साधनं वैराग्यम् उपदिदिक्षुः**। What did he tell now? **जाया-पति-पुत्रादिभ्यो वैराग्यम् उत्पादयति**। **उत्पादयति** — he produces, by his words, by his statement. He wants to produce. **उत्पादयति** here — present tense, in terms of what he is about to talk about. **उत्पादयति**। **विरागम् उत्पादयति**। For what? So you go and embrace them very happily — all the relationships, make them stronger? No. You become objective. And therefore you say "that I can be without. That is not what I am after. I wrongly thought that will deliver the goods that I wanted. Now I know better. What I am really after is not a relationship with an animate or inanimate thing." All relationships are for something else. What's the purpose of saying this? So that you know — why do you go after that which you don't want? Go after what you want. **पुरुषार्थ निश्चय**
That's why I said **पुरुषार्थ विवेक**, if this section is well understood, if you dwell on this, then it makes us wonder, what is it we are interested in life? Because everything a person values is talked about here. And in case you say "it didn't talk about" — there's one word **सर्वस्य कामाय सर्वं प्रियं भवति**, nothing is left out to imagination that way. **जायापतिपुत्रादिभ्यो विरागमुत्पादयति तत्सन्न्यासाय** । So what is the purpose? To make you see that be objective about what you are going after right now — so you recognise, "I am going after this, this is not what I am after. I am going after this, this is not what I am after.
