You will be asking for money for food also. Correct? So if you gave up something, where will you be? You will be without it. That's all. Not having money means poor. That's all it means. It doesn't mean **saṁnyāsa**. Suppose you gave up willingly also, you are still poor only. You don't have money. **nirdhana:** There is no money. So, okay, all this useless, useless, useless — you gave away. But do you have what it takes to what you really want? So, **hanumān** opened every pearl and threw it away, saying — no** rāma **in it. Did he get a pearl with **rāmāyaṇa** in it? He didn't.

Understand — **saṁnyāsa** by itself is not going to solve any problem. Then why do I take **saṁnyāsa**? Don't take. No, no — **śāstra** tells you **saṁnyāsa** is going to be very helpful to you. This **vairāgyam** — not **saṁnyāsa** — **saṁnyāsa** stands for **vairāgyam**. **vairāgyam** will be very helpful for you to understand what **śruti** has to tell you about you. And that **vairāgyam** is highlighted when a person takes **saṁnyāsa**, because understood and assimilated. But he is not taking up **saṁnyāsa** because he has a notion that **saṁnyāsa** is going to make me great. If you think **saṁnyāsa** will give you **mokṣa**, then you are on a trip. **sureśvara** makes it clear. **śāstra** is not doing **vidhi** of **saṁnyāsa**. What is it doing? It is saying **saṁnyāsasya  brahmayāthātmyavijñānasādhanatvaṁ - sanyāsa **is a means, helpful means, to gain this understanding. **saṁnyāsasya brahma yāthātmya vijñāna sādhanatvam vinā āgamena na vijñānam** — without **āgama**, is **veda** — without this **vedānta**, **pramāṇa** — you will not know the utility of **saṁnyāsa** as a **sādhana** for **jñānam**. **tat śāstreṇa iti bodhyate**. That is what is taught by the **śāstra**.
