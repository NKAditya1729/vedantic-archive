श्रुति स्मृति पुराणानाम् आलयम् करुणालयम्। नमामि भगवत्पादम् शंकरं लोक शंकरम्॥ शङ्करं शङ्कराचार्यं केशवं बादरायणम्। सूत्रभाष्यकृतौ वन्दे भगवन्तौ पुनः पुनः॥ ॐ सह नाववतु। सह नौ भुनक्तु। सह वीर्यं करवावहै। तेजस्विनावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥ ॐ नमो ब्रह्मादिभ्यो ब्रह्मविद्यासम्प्रदायकर्तृभ्यो वंशऋषिभ्यो नमो गुरुभ्यः॥

So, we entered the **मैत्रेयी ब्राह्मण** last week, and in that, we are looking at the introductory **भाष्यम्**, introducing the fourth **ब्राह्मण**. So every time we start a section, **भाष्यकार** will typically try to help us understand how this section is relevant in the whole — where does it fit in the whole, and what is its connection to the preceding one mainly. Why this section now?

So we have talked about **ब्रह्मविद्या**. The whole first chapter was about **कर्म**, and what **कर्म** can achieve — that was the primary focus of the first chapter. But within that, we saw **विद्यासूत्र** and **अविद्यासूत्र**. The **विद्यासूत्र** is **आत्मेत्येवोपासीत**. That **विद्यासूत्र** is the foundation, in a way, for the whole teaching. **सूत्र** means it is very briefly indicated, therefore the rest of the **उपनिषद्** is only going to expand on that **सूत्र**. For a **सूत्र**, you need a **व्याख्या**. We have this very common arrangement in the **शास्त्र**s where we present something very, very briefly, then we give a little explanation sometimes, and then we give it even more explanation, then even more, until it is fully explained. It is like a pyramid. So **वृत्ति** means — brief, easy to hold in the head. What do you hold in the head? **आत्मेत्येवोपासीत** — you be **आत्मा** Okay - you do enquiry into yourself, that’s all it is. Understand that. But then there was a lot said about it, and that was said over time. So it began there.

But then the first chapter is predominantly about showing what **कर्म** can give you, up to **ब्रह्मलोक प्राप्ति** — so that one gets **वैराग्यम्** from the fact that everything within that realm of **कर्म** is something just temporary. What is wrong if it is temporary? I am seeking, seeking, seeking, seeking — when will it end? It won't end. Therefore, my seeking is permanent, which means I am seeking to not seek permanently. I am seeking the permanent. That understanding has to be gained. Therefore, the entire presentation of **कर्म काण्ड** in **वेदान्त** is expected to help me get **वैराग्यम्** for **कर्म फल**, not to make me interested in **कर्म फल**. "Ah, can I become **ब्रह्माजी**? I should do **उपासना**." That can happen too, but that is not the intent. I mean, if that happens, when the person does it, then he will come back to **बृहदारण्यक** and say what? "Even though I became **ब्रह्माजी**, I became afraid." Then I have to remember, "Oh, why am I afraid?" So, the idea is finally **ज्ञानम्** alone will deliver; everything else is **वैराग्यम्**.

After that, the second chapter began. In the second chapter, the first three **ब्राह्मण**s, which we have seen, is **अजातशत्रु विद्या**. **ज्ञान काण्ड** has begun in a way — **ज्ञानम्** has begun there. So what is presented there is what is the **वस्तु**. **नेति नेति** is the teaching, culminated in that. Now when that is done, then why are you beginning **मैत्रेयी ब्राह्मण** again? And **भाष्यकार** said, anyway it is all the teaching continuing — but he said this **आख्यायिक**, the story of the **मैत्रेयी ब्राह्मण** - not even teaching; the story is meant to indicate **संन्यास** as an **अङ्ग** for **ज्ञानम्**. There is a lot more to be said about **संन्यास**. So we will see how much we will look into it later.

Since at the end of the last class we had a question — "Swamiji, now is **संन्यास** necessary?" — don't worry too much about that right now. I told you last time - when you have the understanding of **याज्ञवल्क्य**, then you think about **संन्यास** or no **संन्यास**. Until you get **याज्ञवल्क्य**'s understanding, don't worry about it right now. The topic evokes some controversy.

You look at the 18th chapter beginning in the **गीता**. After 17 chapters — let's leave the first chapter out, that is **अर्जुन**'s blabbering — 16 chapters later, 2 to 17 are done, what is **अर्जुन**'s question? "Please help me understand. What is **संन्यास**? What is **त्याग**? Tell me. **संन्यासस्य तत्त्वं वेदितुम् इच्छामि**।" Then what did **कृष्ण** tell him? **कृष्ण** said, okay okay. **संन्यास** and **त्याग** have one thing in common. What is that? Giving up. And that giving up is to be done but **यज्ञदानतपःकर्म न त्याज्यं कार्यमेव तत्** । Any question is answered depends on the questioner and on the context.

See, we are all trying to listen in to when somebody else is being taught. This is how we say it, right? We have a ringside view watching what **कृष्ण** says to **अर्जुन** — a ringside view. Here we will have a ringside view of what **याज्ञवल्क्य** is going to tell **मैत्रेयी**. We are not **मैत्रेयी**, neither are we **अर्जुन**s. We have elements of them. So it is extremely difficult to have one universal teaching for everybody, because each and every one of us is distinct. Therefore, if I am like **अर्जुन** and I am given a teaching that **श्वेतकेतु** or **नचिकेता** receives, it won't work for me. And if I am like **श्वेतकेतु**, and you try to tell me what **अर्जुन** is told, I will say, "I am getting bored with all this, potentially.

See **नचिकेता**'s impatience. **यम** is praising him so much, and he says, "Enough, enough about all this. You tell me what really counts. **अन्यत्र धर्मादन्यत्राधर्मादन्यत्रास्मात्कृताकृतात्** । **अन्यत्र भूताच्च भव्याच्च यत्तत्पश्यसि तद्वद** ॥” What is different from **धर्म**, what is different from **अधर्म**, what is different from **कृता** and what is different from **अकृता**. That you tell me. Enough of praise. He didn't say "enough of praise," but he just said this much. (Swamiji made a hand gesture how  **नचिकेता** might have told **यम** indicating enough of the praise) Means what? A shade of impatience, you know. So these things, if we interpret, how each one of us — we don't know.

Why am I saying all this? So that we don't get into this business of trying to debate **संन्यास** is necessary or not. First, you try to give up, try to do what has to be done, and try not to do what is not to be done. If I can't do that, where is the question of **संन्यास**? The question will come: "Swamiji, **संन्यास** is necessary." Suppose **संन्यास** is necessary — can you take it tomorrow? Are you ready? If you are, let's talk about it.

Is **संन्यास** by choice? **संन्यास** by choice is purely a lifestyle choice. It is a lifestyle choice. These are the two things which are said in the **गीता** — what is it? **ज्ञानयोगेन साङ्ख्यानाम्**, **कर्मयोगेन योगिनाम्**। Two lifestyles. One is exclusively the pursuit of knowledge. The other is pursuit of knowledge along with what has to be done. Two lifestyles.

Now the question is, is one lifestyle better than the other? That is the question of **अर्जुन** all the way. Is one lifestyle better? "You seem to say that knowledge alone will do. And therefore why should I not pursue knowledge to the exclusion of **कर्म**? Why not?" That is what seems correct — because knowledge alone is going to make me free. Why should I pursue anything but knowledge? True, it is true. But are you that person who can pursue knowledge to the exclusivity of **कर्म**, or are you not? Do you have that mind and preparedness to go pursue only knowledge? If you don't, then understand — these two are not two independent means. Though we talk of two **मार्ग**s, two lifestyles, these two lifestyles are not for you to pick like in a supermarket - pick A or pick B. For one fellow, the first one alone is a possibility. For another person, the second may also be a choice — again, not a necessity. But Pujya Swamiji would say, you know, if there is any question, go with **अर्जुन** — means, go with **कर्म योग**. Don't go along with **संन्यास** as a lifestyle, because that will create more trouble. It takes away the venues for growth. **कर्म** is a venue for growth, and until we use **कर्म** to grow, if we give up **कर्म** before we have grown enough, then **ज्ञानम्** cannot deliver the goods.

But where you draw the line is impossible to tell clearly. A person has to walk with a walker when they recover — for how long should they use the walker? "No, I will walk with the walker forever." "Hey, you can give it up." "I don't feel..." He can, but he doesn't give up. Then somebody has to nudge him and tell him, "Look, I am behind you. Let go of the walker. I will be the insurance behind you. Okay? Let go of the walker." When you let go of the walker and you can walk, then you know — "I don't need the walker." But there is a tough phase, I believe, which is you don't know whether you can let go or not let go. So there is a whole lot of grey; it is not black and white.

**शङ्कर** in general has a preference for the **संन्यास** lifestyle. That undertone you will see everywhere. And **शङ्कर** is taking the opportunity and saying, "Look, what does this story indicate? It indicates **संन्यास**." There is truth to it — not that it doesn't indicate it. Because the question is what? "**मैत्रेयी**, I want to take **संन्यास**. Do you give me permission?" That is the question. The whole story begins with that permission-seeking. So why **संन्यास**? Why do you want **संन्यास**? Because that is required for me to — you can look at it two ways — it is required for me to know whether I need a walker or not; or, you know, I need that because it is like the walker itself, which gives me stability. Both ways you can look at it.

Is it **विद्वत्संन्यास** or **विविदिषासंन्यास**? You cannot say for **याज्ञवल्क्य** it is **विविदिषासंन्यास**. **विविदिषा** means **संन्यास** in order to know. **विद्वत्संन्यास** is **संन्यास** because you know. See, if **संन्यास** is to be done — "to be done" means it is **कर्तृ तन्त्रम्**. If it is **कर्तृ तन्त्रम्**, then it is a **कर्म**. If it is a **कर्म** — it is not the same as **ज्ञान**, which is the source of **मोक्ष**. **संन्यास**, if it is a **विधि**, you have a problem with that too.

So I am saying it is too complicated, alright? I am just trying to show the possibilities. And you know what? **भाष्यकार** himself, at the end of the whole teaching of the **बृहदारण्यक**, at the end of 4-5, the last part of the **भाष्यम्**, he again has to discuss **संन्यास** and **कर्म**. Because the argument is, you know, **संन्यास** itself is invalid. You know what - **कर्म** is what has to be done. And there are plenty of sentences in the **वेद** which say **कर्म** should not be given up. **यावज्जीवम् अग्निहोत्रं जुहुयात्** — until you are alive. There is no other consideration. Until you are alive. You can't say "when I am old." No. Until you are alive. Until your body is cremated. Now where is the option for it?

So the **कर्मकाण्डी** will argue that until you are alive you cannot take **संन्यास**. After you have died, you can take it. Then what is **संन्यास विधि** for? You are also a **वैदिक** — you have to explain why there is **संन्यास विधि**. There must be a scope for it. See, whenever you have a rule and you have an exception, there must be a scope for the exception to apply. If there is no possibility for an exception, then you don't need an exception. Correct? Therefore, if **संन्यास विधि** is given — there is an injunction for **संन्यास** — there must be some place where it can apply.

"Yes, I will tell you where it can apply. You know what? People who do **कर्म** should be physically fit and mentally fit. Suppose you are blind or deaf — for such a person, you cannot do **कर्म**. For such people who are unfit for **कर्म**, **संन्यास** is ordained." This is the answer given by whom? The **कर्मकाण्डी**. See, now he has given you why — **यावज्जीवम्**, life never ends; until life ends, you don't take the **संन्यास**. After that, you do whatever you want. So what is the point in that anyway?

Then we ask him, "Hey, but we see sections which talk about **विद्या** alone gives **मोक्ष**, we see that, and we see this **नेति नेति**. So where do you place all this?" "Oh, that is all just for some **कर्म**. It is all **अर्थवाद**." "No, it is not **अर्थवाद**. We prove. We present it all” So then, if both **संन्यास** and **कर्म** are equally possible, which one should be taken when? There is a problem now. Is this like free for all? Anyone can choose anything? What does it mean? There will be **अव्यवस्था**. And secondly, if finally this is the position of the **वेद**, then **कर्म काण्ड** becomes **अप्रमाण**. We saw this all when we were discussing various sections in 2.1.20, I think. A lot of arguments were there.

So it will become meaningless? No, it doesn't become meaningless. Because until you know — this is the **व्यवस्था** generally — one is for **अविद्वत्**, one is for **विद्वत् पुरुष**. For ones who understood, and for ones who don't understand. For ones who don't understand, **कर्म** is valid — it is a means of growth. For ones who know, **संन्यास** is valid. Both are okay. And **शङ्कर** will interpolate and say even for those who want to know, **संन्यास** is valid.

There is again some difficulty justifying it. And see, everybody — the argument is all based on **श्रुति वाक्य**s. It is not based on anything else. Alright? So it is presented that way. Then the question is, when do you give up? I will tell you the most important thing — this is the statement: **यदहरेव विरजेत् तदहरेव प्रव्रजेत्**। There is a statement in the **जाबाल उपनिषद्**, I think. The day you have total **वैराग्यम्**, you want nothing. You don't want a roof on your head. You don't worry about your next meal. You don't worry about the clothes on your body. You don't worry about anything. You understand none of them count for you. **यदहरेव विरजेत्** - that day when you have this kind of **वैराग्यम्** ,**तदहरेव प्रव्रजेत्** -  On that day you can walk out. It doesn’t matter whether you are blind, your are deaf, whether you have studied **वेद** or didn’t study **वेद**, whether you are a man or a woman? It doesn't matter where you stand. But when you see the absolute futility of situations in your life for your freedom, and you understand there is freedom which is there in spite of all this — that day you walk out.

So finally, **संन्यास** is all about total **वैराग्यम्**. Now **शास्त्र** says for **ब्राह्मण**s alone, **संन्यास** is allowed. It is not allowed for **क्षत्रिय** and **वैश्य**. Therefore, **संन्यासविधि** is there— all these may be there extra, I am not denying any of them — but I am saying if you want to look at it logically only, then I am saying - And **शास्त्र** says that as much as that — **वैराग्यम्**, total **वैराग्यम्**, is the basis for the lifestyle. Which means that lifestyle is not by choice. That lifestyle is by knowledge, in a way. But **शास्त्र** kind of says, because you don't know, **संन्यास** is helpful for **ज्ञानम्**. **संन्यास** is helpful for **ज्ञानम्** because you are free from all responsibilities which society may expect — that is, in a society which accepts **संन्यास** as a lifestyle, not which calls you a lazy-bound. So **वैदिक** society accepted that, that for a person who has come to this point and that doesn't demand. He says **भवति भिक्षां देहि**। Whatever **भिक्षा** he gets, he takes. He doesn't say, "Give me this much, give me that, give me this." **साधु भिक्षा** — he doesn't ask. That kind of a person can take **संन्यास**, and since he has no other irons in the fire, his whole pursuit is only for **श्रवणम्**, **मननम्**, **निदिध्यासनम्**. Therefore, other than **गुरु**, he needs nothing else. That is how it is presented.

But until we are there, it is better to sit with **कर्म**, with **कर्म योग बुद्धि**. I am saying this because in this class here, we are not all **याज्ञवल्क्य**s. So it is better to know where we stand. **शास्त्र** will say what it has to say. I am going through the **शास्त्र**, I am not diluting it, but we should know where we stand. अस्या: ब्रह्मविद्याया: अङ्गत्वेन सन्न्यासो विधित्सितः।

For **ब्रह्मविद्या**, like an **अङ्ग**, like a limb, **संन्यास** is **विधित्सितः** — desired to be ordained. Then he said, **जाया-पुत्र-वित्तादि-लक्षणं पाङ्क्तं कर्म अविद्याविषयं यस्मात् न आत्म-प्राप्ति-साधनम्** ;

See, this is **वैदिक**. Why I am saying **वैदिक**? Suppose I ask you generally — "You do actions or not? What is action?" Whatever you decide to do, you choose to do, what is centered on you — **कर्तृ तन्त्रम्** — is **कर्म**. So you tell me, suppose I said you have to do **कर्म**, what do you expect for that? What do you need to do that? You would say, "I need myself — what else do I need? I need a few things around the world." But **वैदिक** means what? It will be presented as **पाङ्क्तं कर्म**. What is **पाङ्क्तम्**? Five factors. What are they? I: **यजमान** — the person who is doing the **यज्ञ**. Then **जाया** — wife. Modern fellow will say, "Why do I need a wife to do **कर्म**? I do it myself." For **वैदिक कर्म**s you require wife. Without wife you cannot do **अग्निहोत्र**.  **पुत्र** — for certain **कर्म**s you need to have a son also. Then you need **वित्तम्**. **वित्तम्** is of two kinds: **मानुषं वित्तम्**, **दैवं वित्तम्**. We have seen this before. In 1.4.17 probably we saw this. So **मानुषं वित्तम्** is money, resources. **दैवं वित्तम्** is **उपासना** — that knowledge. So these are considered the five factors. That is why the word **पाङ्क्तम्**.

So for a **वैदिक कर्म** alone, **पाङ्क्तम्**. For a normal **लौकिक कर्म**, I alone am enough. So we think. Alright. It is covering the whole gamut. So **पाङ्क्तं कर्म अविद्या विषयम् यस्मात् - भाष्यकार** is very clear — **कर्म** is what? **अविद्या विषयम्**. It is until you take yourself to be a doer, which is really not true. That can be shown right now. Do you always do? Do you always do something? In fact, right now, are you doing something? You are listening. You are not doing something. Listening is happening. So doing is only when you are a doer — rather, you are a doer when you are doing. Therefore, if you are not doing all the time, are you a doer all the time? Are you a doer by nature, **कर्ता**? You are not. Therefore, to think I am a **कर्ता** when I am not always a **कर्ता** is **अविद्या**. That is **अविद्या -मूल अविद्या** — thinking I am a doer, experiencer. I am neither a doer nor an experiencer. I am just a being, a conscious being and the very existence. This is what we need to understand.

So then what does **कर्म** become? **अविद्या विषयम्**. So he says, this **जाया-पुत्र-वित्तादि-लक्षणं पाङ्क्तं कर्म**. By that he covers all the five factors. This **कर्म** characterized by these is **अविद्या विषयम्**. Which **अविद्या**? **ब्रह्म-अविद्या** or **आत्म-अविद्या** — not knowing. The subject matter of **विद्या** is the limitless **ब्रह्मन्**. **यस्मात्** - Since it is **अविद्या विषयम्**, it is not **आत्म-प्राप्ति-साधनम्** or **ब्रह्म-प्राप्ति-साधनम्**. It is not a means to understand or to gain the limitless I. The I need not be gained. I am already there, trying to gain things, but that limitless, I want to gain. That limitless is me. I have to gain it only through **ज्ञानम्**, not through **कर्म**. Therefore, **न आत्म-प्राप्ति-साधनम्**. This is one statement.

Now he says, **अन्यसाधनं हि अन्यस्मै फल-साधनाय प्रयुज्यमानं प्रतिकूलं भवति**। You know, when there is a means for a given end, deploying it for another end is going to be counterproductive. It will be contrary. So you have a means for one end — suppose there is a means for stomachache, a medicine for stomachache. I take that. Suppose that — for fever, will it help? It doesn't. So what should be used there? You should use that. So **ज्ञानम्** is a means for **मोक्ष**. It is not a means for **कर्म**. **मोक्ष** does not come from **कर्म**. So to use **कर्म** to get **मोक्ष** is like what? Using the wrong means for the right end. Means and end must be connected. This **साध्य-साधन-विवेक** is very important.

**भाष्यकार** gives an example: **न हि बुभुक्षा-पिपासा-निवृत्त्यर्थं धावनं गमनं वा साधनम्**। Simple sentence in Sanskrit. You should understand this— **न हि - हि** = because **न बुभुक्षा-पिपासा-निवृत्त्यर्थम्**. **बुभुक्षा** is hunger — **भोक्तुम् इच्छा**. **पिपासा** is thirst - desire for drinking. For a person who is hungry or thirsty, who wants to eat or drink, what should he do? "He should go running and walking." Is that true? No. Therefore, he says, for a person who is interested in solving the problem of hunger or thirst, who wants to eat or drink, going, running and walking is not the means. It is not. Similarly, you cannot take **कर्म** as a means for **मोक्ष**.

See, **जाया-पुत्र-वित्त** those are the three mentioned in the first line **जाया-पुत्र-वित्तादि-लक्षणं**  —all of these are for whom - these are for **यजमान** - What is **जाया** for? **जाया** (wife) is for **पुत्र** (son) - **पुत्र** is for **पुत्रेण अयम् लोक**: **जैय्य**: - to win over **मनुष्य लोक**, you need to have a son. That is how **शास्त्र-वेद** presents it. And to gain **पितृ लोक** — they will translate **लोक** as a Mane, some sphere of experience — for that what do you need? You need **कर्म**, **कर्मणा पितृलोकः**. And for **देवलोक**, you need **उपासना**. Therefore, **मनुष्य लोक**, **पितृ लोक**, **देवलोक साधन** has become what? **पुत्र**, **कर्म**, and **उपासना**. These are the **साधन**s. This is the **विनियोग** — this is how the means and ends are assigned.

**साधनत्वेन हि पुत्रादि साधनानि श्रुतानि**, **न आत्म-प्रतिपत्ति-साधनत्वेन**। Not for knowing yourself, not for knowing the self — which self? The limitless self, the unbound self. **विशेषितत्वात् च** — because it has been clearly specified which is for which. The rule in **वेद** is: if you mention one **साधन** for one end, you tie it to it. Don't try to fix it elsewhere. One for one. It is very clear. **विशेषितत्वात् च**। So **पुत्र** is for **मनुष्य लोक**, **कर्म** is for **पितृ लोक**, **उपासना** is for **देवलोक**. This is mentioned. So you don't try to stick it into **मोक्ष**. It doesn't work that way.

**न च ब्रह्म-विदो विहितानि**। This **कर्म - पाङ्क्तम् कर्मनब्रह्म-विद**: **विहितानि** — **विहितानि** -  they are not ordained. They are not ordained for whom? For a **ब्रह्मवित्**, for one who knows **ब्रह्मन्**. Which means, what is **भाष्यकार** saying here? **याज्ञवल्क्य** is **ब्रह्मवित्**? That is why I said, if it is considered as **विद्वत् संन्यास** or **विविदिषा**, take it as **विद्वत् संन्यास**. I am not talking about an ordinary person.

**काम्यत्व-श्रवणात्** — **कर्म** is known to be to fulfill a desire. Why do you say that? **एतावान् वै कामः** — we will look into this. We have seen it before, 1.4.17. But good to remember it again. **एतावान् वै कामः इति**। See, **कर्म** is when you have something to achieve, something you don't have, you want to achieve. You have a desire for something. You have a desired end which is not with you. And to achieve it, you have to undertake an action, a **कर्म**. So, unless you have a desired end other than you, **कर्म** has no role. There must be a desired end other than you, separate from you.

And what about **ब्रह्मवित्**? **ब्रह्मविदश्च आप्त-कामत्वात् आप्तकामस्य कामानुपपत्तेः**| Who is the **ब्रह्मवित्**? **ब्रह्मवित्** means **ब्रह्म-आत्म-वित्** — one who knows **आत्मा** is **ब्रह्मन्**. And that **ब्रह्मवित्**, what is his characteristic? He knows the **ब्रह्मन्** which is limitless — **अनन्तं ब्रह्म**, **आनन्दम् ब्रह्म**. When he knows the limitless, he cannot know the limitless by standing apart from it. Therefore, a **ब्रह्मवित् ब्रह्मैव भवति**, **श्रुति** says this. The knower of **ब्रह्मन्** is **ब्रह्मन्**, because the knower of limitless cannot be a fellow who is standing apart from **ब्रह्मन्**, because otherwise he will limit **ब्रह्मन्**. Standing apart from **ब्रह्मन्**, poor **ब्रह्मन्** gets limited because he stands apart from **ब्रह्मन्**. Therefore, **ब्रह्मवित् ब्रह्मैव भवति**. It is the mathematics of infinity. Therefore, once you say your dealing is infinite, there is only infinite. You can't bring finite numbers there.

So **ब्रह्मवित् ब्रह्मैव भवति**. That being so, what desire will he have? What does limitless mean? Unbound means there is no time other than **ब्रह्मन्**, there is no place other than **ब्रह्मन्**, there is no object, experience, anything other than **ब्रह्मन्**. **ब्रह्मन्** is all there is. That is what it means to be whole, infinite, boundless. We use words to indicate that. Therefore, if there is a person who is a **ब्रह्मवित्**, what desire will he have? What binding desire will he have? **ब्रह्मवित्** means everything is his gain — **ब्रह्मविदाप्नोति परम्** । Therefore, what desire will he have? None. Therefore, in terms of desire, what will you call him? **आप्त-कामः**।**आप्नोति सर्वम् - सर्वान् कामान् -  सर्वान्श्च लोकान्** — everything is attained at once, **युगपत्**, because he is all of it at once. It is like the pot space suddenly gaining the whole space just by knowing, "I am not confined to a pot." That much alone is the knowledge. Therefore, this being the case, **ब्रह्मवित्** is what? **आप्त-कामः**। There is nothing to fulfill in his mind. There is nothing to be done. **आप्तकामस्य कामानुपपत्तेः** - And for a person who has no desires, there is no question of "I have one desire — **कर्म** I have to do for that."

And he gives a quotation: **येषां नोऽयम् आत्मायं लोकः**।**लोक** is a field of experience, generally. But for some — for **मुमुक्षु**s or for **ज्ञानि**s — what is their **लोक**? **आत्मा** is the **लोक**. "I am my **लोक**. There is no other **लोक**." The unknown me for a **मुमुक्षु** and the unknown me for a **ज्ञानि** — that is my **लोक**. For a **ज्ञानि**, the experiencer, experience and the experienced is all oneself. Therefore, what **लोक** do we have? **आत्मा** is the **लोक**. If you want to talk in terms of experience, I will talk like that. What is your trip? **स्वर्ग** is my trip. What is your trip? Switzerland is my trip. What is your trip? **आत्मा** is my trip. I am my own trip. That is called **आत्म-काम**. That is what it means by saying **येषां न**. Don't take it as **नो - न उ**— it is **नः**, "for us, for us who are in interested in **ज्ञानम्**, **आत्मा अयम्लोक**: - this **आत्मा** is our **लोक**" For such a person, where is the question of **काम**? If there is no **काम**, what is **कर्म**? **कामअवाप्ति साधनम्**— it is a means to fulfill a desire which is unfulfilled. A desire, a **काम**, an object which is desired, stands out apart from me, to gain it, I have to do **कर्म**.

Therefore, look, this is not one more like **पितृ लोक**, **देवलोक**, etc. This **ब्रह्मन्** we are talking about is not like that. It is yourself. And therefore, to understand that, there is no place for what? **कर्म**. If there is no place for **कर्म**, why? Because I have no desires to be fulfilled. If I have no desires to be fulfilled, that is the fulfillment of **वैराग्य**. 100% **वैराग्य** is what? There is nothing other than me. There is no question of **राग** for something else. **राग** is when there is something other than me. And when there is total **वैराग्यम्**, what **कर्म** will you do for what sake?

**किम् इच्छन् कस्य कामाय शरीरम् अनुसंज्वरेत्- आत्मानं चेत् विजानीयात्** — if you know yourself as **ब्रह्मन्**, limitless, **किम् इच्छन्** — desiring what? **कस्य कामाय** — for fulfilling which desire? **शरीरम् अनुसंज्वरेत्** — will you torture this body of yours? Means, you won't. It is **आक्षेप**. When that is so, you tell me, what choice does the **ज्ञानि** have? Whether he takes **संन्यास** or not, he is what **कुर्वन् अपि न लिप्यते**. Why is he doing and he says he is not doing, that should be the **ज्ञानि** — that is **ज्ञानि**. But still, one person may say, "I need to be off for a while."

As I told you, this is all a question of — now you get the great ones — when you have the knowledge, it has to be shaken and seen whether it is knowledge or not. So it is said sometimes you take some time away, because unless you see, "I can say, you know what, I don't need a roof over my head, I am okay without it" — when will I know whether I need it or not till I have a roof over my head? It is very nice to say, "I don't think it matters, any food is okay, I will eat anything." But every day I get good food, no trouble, right? "I think we can manage without all this petrol and gas and all that, it's okay.” Try - Let the war go on for some more time, then we will know whether we can manage or we can't manage. So the point is, anything, whether you can do without it, the real test of that **वैराग्यम्** is when situation comes up. So it is better to sometimes test yourself rather than wait for a situation to find out, "No, I didn't get it yet." Okay? So you can look at — I am just showing you how **संन्यास** can be looked at here in the context. But the fact is, really, **ज्ञानम्** — see, here in this matter, you will see the **गीता** so clearly in the 18th chapter. Both **कर्म फल त्याग**, which means not doing **कर्म** purely for the sake of **फल**, being bound by **कर्म फल** — that is **कर्म योग** — or **संन्यास**, which is giving up **कर्म** itself. So **कर्म त्याग** or **कर्म फल त्याग** are both **अविद्यावत् विषय**. Both are meant for the **अविद्वान्**. Because for the **विद्वान्**, **सर्व कर्म फल त्यागम्** is by **ज्ञानम्** alone. It is not by **संन्यास**. It is not by the lifestyle of **संन्यास**.

So the lifestyle of **संन्यास** is a consequence, or the lifestyle of **संन्यास** could be an aid. And here **शङ्कर** is saying it is an aid. It is an **अङ्ग**. But again, as I said elsewhere, you see **वामदेव** comes — he is an exception to all rules. **वामदेव** is born and he says, "I am everybody, I am everything." What **संन्यास** does he mean now?

So when it comes to this **ब्रह्म-ज्ञानम्**, there cannot be a **विधि**. The argument is presented in the **वार्तिक**. **संन्यास** — does this happen because of **विधि**, or will it happen because of **वैराग्यम्**? I can tell you, okay, from tomorrow onwards, give up the mobile phone. Can you give it up? Not easy, right? So I am saying, give up everything. Can you give up? **येन त्यजसि तत् त्यज**। **त्यज धर्ममधर्मं च**। Give up **धर्म**, give up **अधर्म**. Then what should I give up after that? I have given up **धर्म** and **अधर्म**. I give up. That “I” you give up. **येन त्यजसि तत् त्यज**। — that **अहङ्कार त्याग**. How are you going to do it? How are you going to give up that I? After giving up, you will say what? "I gave up I." "You" will remain there. Okay? How are you going to give up I? I cannot be given up. It can only be understood as what? Just an appearance. It cannot be given up. It is an enlightened **अहङ्कार**. **अहङ्कार** is never given up. **अहङ्कार** joins the **ज्ञानम्** — **अहं ब्रह्मास्मि**. It doesn't say **अहं कर्ता भोक्तास्मि**. That is the difference.

Therefore, this giving up is not by choice. So **संन्यास** by choice is only a means. **संन्यास** by consequence is **सर्व कर्म संन्यास**, **ज्ञानेनएव**, there is no other. And if that **ज्ञानम्** is there, whether you do or not do, whether you are **जनक** who is ruling the kingdom, or you are a **मौनी बाबा** sitting in a cave, both are equally free. There is no question about that. So **शुक** is sent to **जनक** by **व्यास** to gain confirmation. He goes to **जनक**. **जनक** gives him a good palace, all people serving him, so many women serving him. **शुक** is not interested in any of that. Then **जनक** says, "You already have it. You are free enough." But he doesn't want to hang around. He wants to go somewhere. **जनक** is ruling. What difference is there? But there is a difference in lifestyle, in **प्रारब्ध** perhaps.

So why am I saying all this? **शङ्कर** is not saying all that. He is only saying **संन्यास** is a limb, and he is proving here that **संन्यास** is necessary, in a way — in a way — because **याज्ञवल्क्य** who knew so much took **संन्यास**. What does it indicate? It indicates something. But I have said, if you give another example — suppose you take the example of **नचिकेता** and you say he didn't take **संन्यास**, then what? An example cannot be a means of knowing. You should know this — an example is not a **प्रमाण**. An example only supports a given point. This is very important for us to understand. Because people give an example and try to show you something is not right. Because in general, the **न्याय** rule is what? For anything, you have to give a logic and you should give an example. That is how **व्यवहार** works. Right?

So he says, **इति श्रुतेः** — therefore, because of all these reasons, what is it? **ब्रह्म-विद्याया**: **अङ्गत्वेन संन्यासो विधित्सितः**। Understand why all this is being said? To show that when you have no **काम**, **संन्यास** is the consequence anyway. So **संन्यास**, when you understood that **कर्म फल** is not what you are interested in — even for a **मुमुक्षु**, **संन्यास** is a choice as a lifestyle. **शमः कारणम् उच्यते** — **आरुरुक्षोर्मुनेर्योगं कर्म कारणमुच्यते** ।**योगारूढस्य तस्यैव शमः कारणमुच्यते** | - [Bhagavad Gita 6.3](https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/display/bhashya/Gita/devanagari?chapter=6&verse=3). Only when you are able to be by yourself, then **शम** is the **कारण** for you, not when you depend on things.

Right? So what is this **एतावान् वै कामः** — I said we will look into it, just so we remember. So this is that section, **एतावान् वै कामः**, 1.4.17. What does **एतावान्** mean? “This much alone”. You know, so if I say, "Okay, I am going to buy this house," how big is this house? I show — “this much alone”. Not the next house, not the road, not the part behind it — this much alone - **एतावान्** so this **तावान्**, **यावान्** — those words in Sanskrit are doing a **परिच्छेदः**. This much alone. **एतावान् वै कामः** — this much alone is **कामः**. That is why he says, **एतावद् विषय परिच्छिन्न**:

So he says, what are they? Look, all that you have, you know, you are looking for fields of experience. You are looking for **लोक**:, fields of experience. Alright? And for these **लोक**s, there are means - to gain these experiences there are means, like — the son is a means for **मनुष्य लोक**, **कर्म** is a means for **पितृ लोक**, **उपासना** is a means for **देवलोक**. These are the means with which you will gain some end. So for the sake of those ends, what do you want? You need means. So if I want **देवलोक**, my means is **उपासना**. If I want **मनुष्य लोक**, my means is **पुत्र**, etc. This is the way it is being presented.

So he says, all these —if you really look at it — there is really only one desire. What is the desire? The desire is for an end or for ends. And for the ends, you need means. Therefore, in life, what is the only thing that you are interested in? Ends, and for ends, means. Okay? So we say **पुत्रैषणा**, **वित्तैषणा**, **लोकैषणा**. These are means— **पुत्र**, **वित्त** are means; **लोक** is end. So these are the only **एषणा**s. **एषणा** means desires. Only three desires in any human being — what are they? Desire for ends and desire for means. Reduce it to one — desire for ends. Desire for an end is what? **काम**. A desire, an end which I don't have, is **काम**. Therefore, the desire for an end means **काम** is there. So now, if you have no **काम**, what do you need now? There is no end. If you are free from the desire for an end, there is no end that you desire. What means do you need? You need nothing. Whereas **कर्म** is what? **कर्म** is always using means to gain an end — except when we do **कर्म** for **अन्तःकरण शुद्धि**, where it is not considered an end exactly. Right?

Therefore this **काम** — when this **एतावान् वै कामः** means "this much alone" — **काम** is all about - means, this **कर्म** will give you, this **काम**. There is nothing more to it, even if you wish. See, he says **एषणे साध्यसाधनलक्षणे कामः** — the desire is only for ends and means. There is no other desire. Whatever desire it is, it is for a means. And, you know, **येन प्रयुक्तः अविद्वान् अवश एव** - being compelled by them — I am trying to reduce Sanskrit — being compelled by them like a **कोशकारवत्**, silkworm, like a silkworm, **आत्मानं वेष्टयति** one covers oneself, one builds a cocoon around oneself in the forms of one's own desires, and then **कर्ममार्ग एवात्मानं प्रणिदधत्** he has no other way except to take to action, because **बहिर्मुखीभूतः** he is looking outward. He doesn't know himself as a free limitless being. - **न स्वं लोकं प्रतिजानाति**. Therefore, he is always looking out, and there are endless means. So since there are endless means, how can you say "this much alone is a desire"? **एतावान्** — how can you limit it? It is being said even though they are endless - **अनन्ता हि कामाः**, finally, what is it? It is only for an end. The ends may be endless, but that there is an end, is an end. That is the limitation there. You are looking for something other than you. Therefore - **यस्मात्** other than even wishing **न - इच्छन् - चन** — **इच्छन्नपि**, **अतः अस्मात्फलसाधनलक्षणात्** , **भूयः अधिकतरम्** , **न विन्देत् न लभेत** other than a means and an end there is nothing that can be gained. **लब्धव्यविषयो हि कामः** A desire is something other than you which needs to be gained. Therefore, **कर्म** is only when you have some end to be gained. **एतावान् वै कामः** — and **काम** is only for an end, and unless you have an end, there is no need for a **कर्म**. So if you are free from the need for anything, there is no question of you being to do **कर्म**, because **कर्म** is only for **काम्यम्**.

What is being shown? That for a person who understands the limitlessness of oneself, **कर्म** has no scope in his life, has no relevance for him. If he does it, it is purely **लोकसङ्ग्रहणार्थम्**, not for his own need. So this is all to show what? **भाष्यकार** said all this for what? See, again, I can go and look into **येषां नः अयम् आत्मा अयं लोकः**। For everybody else, **लोक**, their field of experience, is either a place or some property, something they want, or experience, finally. But for one for whom I am everything, oneself is everything, there is no need for **कर्म**. Therefore, **संन्यास** is a natural thing, and that **संन्यास** is what is being told here by this whole story.

**केचित् तु ब्रह्म-विदोऽपि एषणा-सम्बन्धं वर्णयन्ति**। But there are some people. **भाष्यकार** — he doesn't name who, but there are some people in his time also. **केचित्** — indefinite. **ब्रह्म-विद**: **अपि**, even for a knower of **ब्रह्मन्**, **एषणा-सम्बन्धं वर्णयन्ति** — they say they also have some desires. **भाष्यकार** very nicely says, **तैः बृहदारण्यकं न श्रुतम्** — it looks like they have not really studied or heard **बृहदारण्यक उपनिषद्**. If they had, they wouldn't say that.

**पुत्रादि-एषणानाम् अविद्वद्-विषयत्वम्**, **विद्या-विषये च येषां नः अयम् आत्मा अयं लोकः इति** — so these people didn't hear. Why? Because what does **बृहदारण्यक** say? It says, for some of us, for **मुमुक्षु**s and **ज्ञानि**s, **आत्मा** is the **लोक**. There is no other **लोक**. And **एषणा** is all about a **लोक**. It says **पुत्रैषणा**, **वित्तैषणा**, **लोकैषणा** — this is **एषणा**, **एषणा त्रय** it is called. But really, **पुत्रैषणा**, **वित्तैषणा** is for the sake of **लोकैषणा**. The means are for the sake of the end. Therefore, remove the other two — what is remaining is only one, **लोकैषणा**. And for us, we have only one **लोक**, which is myself. If I am the **लोक**, what should I need to do? To be yourself, what do you need to do? What do you need to do to be yourself? Dance! If you dance, also you are you. If you sing, also you are you. If you eat, also you are you. If you sleep, also you are you. If you sit idly, also you are you. If you talk, also you are you. What do you have to do with yourself? Which you? The fulfilled you. Therefore, you know, for such a person, **किं प्रजया करिष्यामः** — **बृहदारण्यक** says this, **किं प्रजया करिष्यामः**।

Arey, by **पुत्रेन अयम् लोक**: **जैय्य**: — for this world to be gained, you must have a son. Haven't you seen the **श्रुति**? **कृष्ण-केश**: **जात-पुत्रः यजेत** — one who has got dark hair and one who has got a child, that fellow only can do a certain **कर्म**. Without that, I don't have **कर्म अधिकारित्वम्**. When my **लोक** is only **आत्मा**, **किं प्रजया करिष्यामः** what should I do with progeny? I don't need progeny, because progeny is a means for an end, and I have no end in view, because I am the end.

**इति एष विभागः तैः न श्रुतः** — this also has not been heard by them. And who gave the **श्रुति**? **श्रुत्या कृतः** — the **वेद** has said that.

**सर्वक्रिया-कारक-फलोपमर्द-स्वरूपायां च विद्यायां सत्याम्** , **सह कार्येण अविद्याया अनुपपत्ति-लक्षणश्च विरोधः तैर्न विज्ञातःसर्वक्रिया-कारक-फलोपमर्द-स्वरूपायां च विद्यायां सत्याम्** One phrase. **सति सप्तमि**. That is happening when — when what is so? When there is knowledge, **विद्या**, **ब्रह्मविद्या**. And what is that knowledge characterized by? **सर्व-क्रिया-कारक-फल-उपमर्द-स्वरूपायाम्**।**उपमर्द** means **उपमर्दनम्** — "Make chutney out of it. Grind it to nothing." That chutney — **उपमर्द**. What do you make chutney out of? **क्रिया-कारक-फल** - Action, means of action, outcome of action — all of these. What do you do? You make chutney out of it. How do you make chutney out of it? You show that they have no being of their own. They all depend on one being, one existence, one reality. And that reality, I am. So I don't depend on **कर्म** — **कर्म** depends on me. That is the understanding. **सर्व-क्रिया** — this is a very common way of presenting **संसार** being removed. That is why, **ब्रह्मार्पणं ब्रह्म हविः ब्रह्माग्नौ ब्रह्मणा हुतम्** — everything is **ब्रह्मन्**. The doer is **ब्रह्मन्**, what is to be done is **ब्रह्मन्**, the means of action is **ब्रह्मन्**, everything is **ब्रह्मन्**. Therefore, what is **ब्रह्मन्**? It is like this — you are saying the bangle is **ब्रह्मन्**, the necklace is **ब्रह्मन्**, the ring is **ब्रह्मन्** — therefore, what is not **ब्रह्मन्**? Gold. It went to the same flow. Bangle is gold, ring is gold, chain is gold, earring is gold. Then what is that they are all gold? If you look at gold, gold alone is gold. Forms alone — all of these are gold. Same way, everything — all **कारक**s, all factors of action, the outcome of action, the action itself — everything is what? One. One reality.

And therefore, that is the **विद्या**. When that **विद्या** is there, there is no **भेदम्**. There is no **क्रिया-कारक भेदम्**. Therefore, this kind of knowledge, when it is there — **सह कार्येण अविद्याया अनुपपत्ति-लक्षणश्च विरोधः** -  that **विद्या** and saying "I am a doer" — those two cannot be together. "I am a doer" and "there is no doer" cannot go together. This is **अनुपपत्ति** — the untenability of this **ब्रह्मविद्या** and **कर्म** going together — **तैर्न विज्ञातः** - they did not understand that, they didn’t understand clearly for sure. **विशेषया न ज्ञातम्** — they didn't understand it clearly for sure. That is why they are saying what? They are saying **ब्रह्मविदोऽपि एषणा-सम्बन्धम् अस्ति**। They think there is a connection between a **ब्रह्मवित्** and a desire. He also has something to accomplish. No, not true.

Further, not just **श्रुति**, they did not even read **स्मृति**. **व्यास-वाक्यं च तैः न श्रुतम्** — they did not even listen to or study **महाभारत**. Forget **वेद**. **कर्म-विद्या-स्वरूपयोः विद्याविद्यात्मकयोः प्रतिकूलवर्तनं विरोधः**।**भाष्यकार** used the word **विरोधः** — suddenly, I think he looked at some face in the class and said, "Let me explain what **विरोधः** means." So he is explaining. See, this is the way **भाष्यम्** works. You use a word, and if you think that word could be misunderstood, you have to explain that word. You have to explain it till the understanding has reached the person, you try, because your understanding must become the understanding of the person in front of you. It is not a commentary. That is what I was saying in the **शङ्कर जयन्ती** class. It is not just a mere commentary. The word "commentary" has a very broad meaning. For us, commentaries — you have to explain each word, its connection to other words. And if you use a word to explain one word, and if that word is not understood, you explain another word. If that is not understood, you explain another word until it is understood.

So here he says, what is this **विरोध**? **सह कार्येण अविद्यायाः अनुपपत्ति-लक्षण विरोधः**। He said, then what is **विरोध**? **कर्म-विद्या-स्वरूपयोः विद्याविद्यात्मकयोः प्रतिकूलवर्तनं विरोधः**। **प्रतिकूल-वर्तनम्** — they are in opposition to each other. What? **कर्म** and **विद्या**. Action requires **कारक**. Action is done for the sake of **फल**. Therefore, what does action depend upon? A **कर्ता** being separate from the **कर्म**. Whereas what is **विद्या**? **कर्तृ-कर्म-भेद** is not there. Chutney has been made. Therefore, how will the two coexist? You know what, let us take some light and darkness and mix them together. What will you get? Can you mix light and darkness? That is what you are trying to do. **विद्या** and **अविद्या** are like light and darkness. One is opposed to the other. **अविद्या - विरोधार्थे नञ्**.

So, **कर्म-विद्या-स्वरूपयोः विद्याविद्यात्मकयोः प्रतिकूलवर्तनं विरोधः**। He explained what is **विरोधः**. Then he continues. See, otherwise, if you just look at the sentence by itself, you will wonder what is happening again in between. He is explaining **विरोध**.

यदिदं वेदवचनं कुरु कर्म त्यजेति च ।कां गतिं विद्यया यान्ति कां च गच्छन्ति कर्मणा’ (मो. ध. २४१ । १ । २) ॥

This is in **मोक्ष धर्म**. **मोक्ष धर्म** means what? In **महाभारत**, in **महाभारत** 12th section, **मोक्ष पर्व**, in that 241st chapter, the verses are there. So what are the verses there? There, the question is — **शुक** is asking his father **व्यास**. **शुक महर्षि**, who is credited with the authorship of **भागवतम्**, teaching **भागवतम्** — that **शुक महर्षि** has gone to **वेदव्यास**, his father. Father means — father will not tell wrong things to his son. This is the idea in the **शास्त्र**. That a father will never give anything but the best to his children. Children are always dear to the father. Whether children will understand or not, there is no question of a father doing wrong for the child. Therefore, if the father knows, he will give the best to the son. Alright? So the father will be approached by the son. What does he ask?

यदिदं वेदवचनं कुरु कर्म त्यजेति च। कां गतिं विद्यया यान्ति, कां च गच्छन्ति कर्मणा॥

"I see two types of statements in the whole **वेद - कुरु कर्म** and **त्यज कर्म इति च** i.e do **कर्म** and give up **कर्म**. I see both kinds of statements in the **वेद**. **कां गतिं विद्यया यान्ति**, **कां च गच्छन्ति कर्मणा - कां गतिं**  Which — what will be the lot, or what will be the end, to be gained by those who go with **कर्म**? And what will be the end to be gained by **विद्या**?" Looks like there are two, because **वेद** seems to say do **कर्म**, give up **कर्म**. So, look, quietly, what did he do? "Give up **कर्म**" means what? "**त्यज कर्म**" means what? He took it as **विद्या**. Very quietly. Means he understands there is one which is the path of **कर्म**, one which is the path of **विद्या** — not **कर्म** and **अकर्म**. Because **अकर्म** is also **कर्म** — **कर्मणो ह्यपि बोद्धव्यं बोद्धव्यं च विकर्मणः** ।**अकर्मणश्च बोद्धव्यं गहना कर्मणो गतिः** || B.G. 4.17action, prohibited action, **विकर्म**, not doing action by choice, **अकर्म** — all of these are what? **कर्म**. Because there is choice involved. So, what is not **कर्म**? It is only **विद्या**. No will. Why **विद्या**? Because **विद्या** is **ज्ञानतन्त्रम्**, not **कर्तृतन्त्रम्**, **वस्तुतन्त्रम्**. It depends only on the object, on the reality.

So he is saying, "What end will one reach only by knowledge?" Which knowledge? **ब्रह्म विद्या**. Not anything else. "And where will you go with **कर्म**? **एतद् वै श्रोतुम् इच्छामि** — this is what I would like to understand, sir. **शुक** is asking his father **व्यास**. **तत् भवान् प्रब्रवीतु मे** — Sir, father. **एताव् अन्योन्य वैरूप्ये वर्तेते प्रतिकूलतः** — so they seem to be opposed to each other. Do **कर्म**, give up **कर्म**. I want to know what it is." Which is what?

What is the answer of **व्यास**? **कर्मणा बध्यते जन्तुः**, **विद्यया च विमुच्यते**। **तस्मात् कर्म न कुर्वन्ति यतयः पारदर्शिनः**॥ "**जन्तुः**, a living being, **कर्मणा बध्यते** — he gets bound, because **कर्म** produces more **कर्म**, and **कर्म फल** is what gives you your body and life. **विद्यया च विमुच्यते** | By **विद्या**, he is freed. **तस्मात्कर्म न कुर्वन्ति** Therefore **शुक**, **पारदर्शिनः - ब्रह्मवित्** those who know **ब्रह्मन्**, **कर्म न कुर्वन्ति** don't do **कर्म**." It is not by choice — they can't do **कर्म - कुर्वन् अपि न करोति सह** |

So, **इत्येवमादि** — **विरोधः प्रदर्शितः**। Look Sir, **कर्म** and **विद्या** don't go together.

Now, you should keep one thing in mind. This statement is what? **कर्मणा बध्यते जन्तु**: |Alright. What does **भगवान्** say in the **गीता**? **न हि कश्चित्क्षणमपि जातु तिष्ठत्यकर्मकृत्** । **कार्यते ह्यवशः कर्म सर्वः प्रकृतिजैर्गुणैः** ॥ "Once you are a human being, born, you cannot even live without **कर्म**”. **न हि कश्चित्क्षणमपि** Even for a moment, it is not that action is not happening. **कार्यते**, you will do. **अवशः**, without any say over it. **कार्यते ह्यवशः कर्म सर्वः प्रकृति-जैर् गुणैः**।" Therefore, then how do you do **कर्म** which won't bind? That is not the subject matter here. This **कर्म** is used in a general way. Generally, **कर्म** binds. But this statement is taken as "**कर्म** always binds." But what is **भगवान्** saying in the **गीता**? **यज्ञार्थात् कर्मणोऽन्यत्र लोकोऽयं कर्म-बन्धनः**। A **कर्म** done other than for the sake of **ईश्वर** alone is binding, not the **कर्म** which is done for the sake of **ईश्वर**. **कुर्वन्न् एवेह कर्माणि जिजीविषेत् - उपनिषद्**, **ईशावास्य**, cannot say you must live only doing **कर्म** if **कर्म** is going to be **बन्ध**.

So this is **लौकिक दृष्ट्या** — **कर्मणा बध्यते जन्तुः**। **रमण** also says what? **कर्तु राज्ञया प्राप्य तेफलम्** । **कर्म किंपरं** - is it the highest ? No - **कर्म तज्जडम्** ॥—**कृतिमहोदधौ पतनकारणम्** ।**फलमशाश्वतं गतिनिरोधकम्** ॥ it is going to push you back. But still what, **ईश्वरार्पितं नेच्छया कृतम्** ।**चित्तशोधकं मुक्तिसाधकम्** ॥ don't go only to **व्यास वक्य** and say, "**कर्म** should be given up." Because you know what? Every time we look at something, what we gain from it is what we already have concluded. This is our capacity, you know. We will read into everything what we want to read into it. This is the phenomenal capacity we all have — lack of objectivity. The human mind is like that.

And what do we understand? **कर्म** and **ज्ञान** don't go together. But that is after understanding yourself correctly. Until then, **कर्म** has to be done until you gain what it needs — the prepared mind to be free from **कर्म**. Therefore, I am deliberately going both ways. Because I am not talking to **मैत्रेयी** here. They are all not **मैत्रेयी**s necessarily. **मैत्रेयी** is very evolved. That is why he can say all this to her.
