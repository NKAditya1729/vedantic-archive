Even **vittasādhye**। What is **vittasādhya**? **sādhya** means that which can be gained. Sometimes it can mean that which should be gained. Not by **yājñavalkya** — by **maitreyī** — when it was done, by saying "who wants that? Give me what really values." Then she even did emotional blackmail. "You're giving me what is useless to you? So **tuṣṭaḥ āha**। Why was he pleased? **sva-abhiprāya sampattau**। **sva-abhiprāya - svasya abhiprāya** - One’s own thinking. **abhiprāya** is one's view, one's thinking, one's understanding. So his understanding is what? This **karma**, this wealth etc. are not going to deliver the goods. One will keep on doing **karma**, that will bring you to more opportunities to **karma** — more bodies, more lives, more **karma** — but the cycle will never end. Therefore wealth is not a means for freedom. It's not. **karma** is not a means for freedom. This is his clear and ascertained view. Otherwise he will not say "I want to take **saṃnyāsa**।" Because **saṃnyāsa** means — not going away. **saṃnyāsa** means, you know, signing off your rights to do **karma**। That's what — if you understand **saṃnyāsa** — you no longer can do **karma**। It's like saying — you know, suppose you want to go all around the world on a car, okay? Then you won't go and surrender your driving licence. Your driving licence you will hold on to with your dear life, because that is what is going to allow you to take this RV, nice recreational vehicle, and you drive all around. It has a bed, it has a kitchen, it has everything in there. And all you do is get on, hop on the RV and drive. And you can drive from one end of the country to another in your own style. Air-conditioned RV, with bunk beds. What a wonderful life it will be, you know. Whole life you work, so you have enough money and time to travel the world as you like. Now what do you do? And every day he will ask, "how is the horse?" First thing in the morning, he will come and ask, "how is the horse?" One day, they tried. Finally, the horse couldn't recover. Now among the veterinary physicians, the question is — who is going to get his neck cut off? Who wants to do it? Nobody wants their neck to be cut. And the king is an irrational fellow. He will do this. How are you going to save your neck now? One vet said, "okay, I will not." Everybody said, "thank you, sir. Thank you for leaving us a burden." So he would — king came. "Okay, what about the horse?" "Sir, the horse doesn't get up. The horse doesn't move. The horse doesn't eat. The horse doesn't neigh. The horse doesn't walk. The horse doesn't twitch." Keeps on saying. The king says, "so the horse is dead?" "Yes, sir." All this — you say "horse doesn't move, horse doesn't neigh, horse doesn't tremble" — all this for what? All this to understand one thing — what? The horse is no longer alive. Okay. So in all ways, you have understood the horse is no longer alive. There is no more a doubt about the horse not being alive. Like somebody very dear to you passed away. And one person comes and touches and says, "no, the body is still warm. Are you sure he's dead?" Very valid question at that point. Because the person — perhaps there is life. Maybe you didn't look. "No, I checked the pulse, but the temperature is there." So you have to nail it. There should be no doubt that the person is dead. Medically dead.
So too — when should **ātmavidyā** be taught? When you are very sure that what you really care for, what you really love, is only yourself. Everything else you love for your own sake. "I want to feel good." Let's modify the word a little. "I want to be the pleased me. I want to be the pleased me. When I say "I don't want this car, I bought it only one month ago, and I sold it" — when you sell a car, even if you sell it the next day, you take a deep discount. You have to give a discount. And it has to be conveyed in a way which is understandable by everybody. That's the opening. "The wife is not dear for the wife's sake. The wife is dear for the husband's sake?" No. "The wife is dear for her own sake. Husband is not dear to the wife for the husband's sake.
