ॐ श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणानाम् आलयं करुणालयम्। नमामि भगवत्पादं शङ्करं लोकशङ्करम्॥ शङ्करं शङ्कराचार्यं केशवं बादरायणम्। सूत्रभाष्यकृतौ वन्दे भगवन्तौ पुनः पुनः॥ ईश्वरो गुरुरात्मेति मूर्तिभेदविभागिने। व्योमवद्व्याप्तदेहाय दक्षिणामूर्तये नमः॥ ॐ सह नाववतु। सह नौ भुनक्तु। सह वीर्यं करवावहै। तेजस्विनावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥ ॐ नमो ब्रह्मादिभ्यो ब्रह्मविद्यासंप्रदायकर्तृभ्यो वंशऋषिभ्यो नमो गुरुभ्यः॥

We just entered the sixth **मन्त्र**, and the fourth **ब्राह्मण** of the second **अध्याय** — **मैत्रेयी** **ब्राह्मण**. So the fifth **मन्त्र** was a **मन्त्र** which said — what we are really seeking, what is really dear to us is what we are seeking. And what's really dear to us is ourselves. So everything else is loved is a derived love. It's not a love for the thing per se, but because somehow it makes me feel good. So all that I really love is the pleased me — the me without any limitations, any agenda. And that me seems to come out in the presence of, or in the absence of, something. And I have attributed my being comfortable to those situations or objects or relationships, and I'm insecure without them. And therefore I have to discern the fact that what I really love myself is a secure, comfortable me which does not depend on anything else. And that me is to be discovered, is to be known.

**आत्मा** **वा** **अरे** **द्रष्टव्यः** — we say it has to be known, not because it is something which is to be created, but because it's not recognized. It's missed. Despite it being the only reality, it's missed. Then how would I know it? **श्रोतव्यः** — that's a **विधि**, you have to understand. **द्रष्टव्यः** — if you consider **विधि**, it's a **विधि** to know. Knowing cannot really be a **विधि**, because you cannot ordain "I should know", because it's not by command that I know. I know — somehow I know — but I don't know it by a command, an injunction. So **श्रोतव्यः** — this you do. This is the way to go about it. Do it. Not that it will give you — **श्रोतव्यः** doesn't mean you will become **ज्ञानी** — but that is the way. Listen. Enquire. **श्रोतव्यः**.

And when you listen, when you find something that doesn't seem true, doesn't gel in your head, find out why you think it's not right. And if you see a reason for it, see what is the reason for which you think that **श्रुति**'s vision is not your vision. If the **श्रुति** says there's only one reality, why do I feel there are many realities? Why do I think there are many realities? And I have to make peace with myself that the knowledge that **श्रुति** gives is indeed right. The benefit of doubt goes to **श्रुति**. That is called **श्रवणम्**.

And I have to see how my logic is flawed for me to appreciate **श्रुति**'s vision as the only possibility. That is called **मननम्** — **मन्तव्यः**. Technically we say **लिङ्ग** — there are, there are these various things which have to be looked into to do that **तात्पर्यनिश्चय** of **श्रुतिवाक्य** — **मीमांसा**. But really, what's required is — nothing in my understanding stands against what the **श्रुति** has to convey to me. That's very important. Otherwise it's just — it's like, you know, it's a philosophy. It sits in the book. I can talk of different philosophies — which is your philosophy? None of them. I don't know which one is right — that will be my difficulty. Therefore **मननम्** — what should be the end of **मननम्**? **श्रुति**'s vision alone is true.

Then **निदिध्यासितव्यः**. Orientation issues are there — work them out. That can take time. You cannot put a line on it, because emotions can also disturb you. Emotional you overwhelms you in some way. So then — that, till when? When is it really disturbed? When you don't need to do **निदिध्यासन** — it's all over. That is, basically — with the same conviction that I take myself to be an individual, with that same conviction I see there is no individual, there is only one reality. That should be — that's called **दृढज्ञानम्** — **निष्ठा** means it doesn't require a second thought. It's not — see, I don't think and say, "I'm a man." I don't think. I just say. I don't think and answer, "my name is this." I just say. If you ask me what did I eat for breakfast yesterday morning, I may have to think. But my identity, whatever I consider it, I don't think. It's always been **अविचारसिद्ध** anyway. Without thinking only, I've been saying what I think I am. But after thinking, when you arrive at reality, that should become my natural way of looking upon myself.

So this was what? **श्रोतव्यो** **मन्तव्यो** **निदिध्यासितव्यः**. What will happen if you do this? Then you will recognize everything is **आत्मा**. Otherwise this knowledge is not complete. It is not the knowledge of a distinct me other than everything else which completes the matter. This is why we saw also at some extent that, you know, the **साङ्ख्य** also says there is only, you know, everything else — **असङ्गः** **पुरुषः**. But for him there are many **आत्मा**s. There is something called "I" which is not available for your, you know, eyes, etc. Not available. That part he accepts. So, you know, there is something which you are, which is not what you see — that is not enough. That is not **ज्ञानम्**. The **ज्ञानम्** is **सर्वात्मता** — there is nothing but me. That's a different dimension. So if you only recognize "I am not recognizable by sight etc.", that is **त्वम्पद**-**विचार**. It is not **ज्ञानम्**. Sometimes it is confused for **ज्ञानम्**. And **साक्षी** and **साक्षी** — that is not **ज्ञानम्**. I don't see — I don't recognize the second thing alone as **ज्ञानम्**. There is no possibility of a second thing. So that **ज्ञानम्** has to be taught.

And therefore we entered into 2.4.6. That 2.4.6 — what did it begin with? **ब्रह्म** **तं** **परादात्**. So there, what did we see? Let's go to the text now. **ब्रह्म** **तं** **परादाद्योऽन्यत्रात्मनो** **ब्रह्म** **वेद**. And before that — very important, we don't leave this here — **कथम्** **अन्यस्मिन्विदिते** **अन्यद्विदितं** **भवति** ? How, by knowing one thing, another thing can become known? By knowing one thing, you know only one thing. You cannot know a second thing by knowing one thing. And the answer was what? That's not a problem. Why? Because **आत्मव्यतिरेकेण** **अन्यत्** **किञ्चित्** **नास्ति** — because other than **आत्मा**, there is nothing else. You are right — if it was there, if there was something — if there is something, **यदि** **अस्ति** — not "if it was there" — **यदि** **अस्ति** — if it is there, if there is something other than **आत्मा**, you are absolutely right, sir. By knowing **आत्मा** you cannot know that. True. **न** **तु** **अन्यद्** **अस्ति** But there is no other thing other than **आत्मा**. **आत्मैव** **तु** **सर्वम्** alone is everything. Therefore, if **आत्मा** is known, everything is as well known. **कथं** **पुनः** **आत्मा** **एव** **सर्वम्**? How then? How? How is **आत्मा** everything? **इति** **एतत्** **श्रावयति** — this, the **उपनिषद्**, makes you hear. **श्रावयति** **श्रुतिः**. This is how **भाष्यकार** connected.

All right. So in this — in this context, the **वार्तिककार** brings out — **वार्तिक**, **वार्तिकसार**, both will bring out the idea — that there is this thinking that there is **परमात्मा**, there is **आत्मा** — there are two. There is **कारणम्**, there is **कार्यम्** — there is a cause, there is an effect. There is **व्यष्टि**, there is **समष्टि** — individual, total. You can't deny this. That's my experience. So how can you — how can you say this, that everything is only one, there is only one thing? It's not a possibility. Because in my experience, everything I experience is — first I am, and there is another. That's my experience. And I see that I do not call all the shots. There is a lot of uncertainty in life. There is something else which determines or limits me also. Therefore **परमात्मा** **अन्यः**.

And what we have to show — that is not the vision of the **श्रुति**. First, see — the first step is what? That is not the vision of the **श्रुति** — that is **श्रवणम्**. Then, how it cannot be — that is **मननम्**. Not watertight — they go hand in hand sometimes, but still there is a line. When **श्रद्धा** is — the **शास्त्र** is **प्रमाणम्** — I want to understand what **शास्त्र** has to say. Then my question — I don't agree, I agree. First, understand what it is saying.

So it is saying **ब्रह्म** **तं** **परादात्**. **शास्त्र** is saying **ब्रह्म** **तं** **परादात्** — **ब्राह्मणजाति** **तं** **परादात्** — would deny you. Whom? **यः** **अन्यत्रात्मनः** **ब्रह्म** **वेद** — who takes **आत्मा** to be different from **ब्राह्मणजाति**. You know, this **ब्राह्मणजाति** would negate or deny one who says **ब्राह्मणजाति** is different from **आत्मा**. Similarly, **क्षत्त्रं** **तं** **परादात्** — **क्षत्रियजाति** would deny or negate a person, push him away in a way, who takes **आत्मा** to be different from **क्षत्रियजाति**.

So then — so that's what **भाष्यकार** said. **ब्रह्म** — what is **ब्रह्म**? **ब्राह्मणजातिः**. **तं** **पुरुषं** **परादात्** — that person — **परादात्**, **परादध्यात्**, **पराकुर्यात्** — will dissolve. **कम्** ? Whom? **यः** **अन्यत्रात्मनः** — **आत्मस्वरूपव्यतिरेकेण**. What is **भाष्यकार** explaining? **अन्यत्रात्मनः** — what is **अन्यत्र**? **आत्मस्वरूपव्यतिरेकेण** — other than **आत्मस्वरूप**. Not what you take as **आत्मा** — **आत्मस्वरूप**. So the word **आत्मा** here is the **वस्तु** — **आत्मवस्तु** as **उपनिषद्** wants me to understand, not as I know myself. Because **आत्मा** is also — see, the person will say **आत्मा** **कः** — I'd say, who am I? That's correct. It's not wrong. **आत्मा** is I. But here the word **आत्मा** is used not in terms of what I know of myself, but what is **श्रुति**'s view of me.

So, **आत्मस्वरूपव्यतिरेकेण**. And what is their thinking? What is this person's view? **आत्मैव** **न** **भवतीयं** **ब्राह्मणजातिः** — this **ब्राह्मणजाति** is not **आत्मा**, it is different from **आत्मा**. Now **आत्मा** — another way of looking at is “reality”. The absolute reality. Therefore this **ब्राह्मणजाति** is a different reality. There is one reality — there are many realities. See the view — world view. There are many realities. Is that our world view or not? I am there, you are there, we are different. My thoughts are different from me. A pot is different from a book. This is all — this is all our world view. Therefore **जाति** — **ब्राह्मणजाति** is not me, I am different from that. To say — I am different from the — **ब्राह्मणजाति** is different from me, I am different from **ब्राह्मणजाति** — "I" here, understand — where do I stand? I stand here (in the body). If you say I am different from **ब्राह्मणजाति**, it means you are looking at yourself in a particular way. You are not looking at yourself as **सत्यं** **ज्ञानम्** **अनन्तं** **ब्रह्म** — **आत्मा**. You are looking at it differently. Okay?

For such a person, **तं** **परादध्यात्** — the **ब्राह्मणजाति**, which is actually **आत्मा**. So I can use the word **परमात्मा**. See — **परमात्मा**, **जीवात्मा** — there is no difference, okay? But if it makes it easier, I can say **ब्राह्मणजाति** is not different from **परमात्मा**. **परम** will go later. Only **आत्मा** is. There is no **परमात्मा** and **छोटा** **आत्मा**, **बड़ा** **आत्मा** — there is no such thing. But to start with, we can — we can say **परमात्मा**, perhaps. Because — why I am saying that? Because **भाष्यकार** says quietly here, you know, it's **परमात्मा**.

So — **अनात्मस्वरूपेण** **मां** **पश्यति** — **मां** **ब्राह्मणजातिम्** **अनात्मस्वरूपेण** **पश्यति** — this fellow thinks **ब्राह्मणजाति** is different from me — from, you know, from the reality. But **परमात्मा** **हि** **सर्वेषामात्मा**. Okay? So now he is going on and on. We will not just go on — we will spend some time here.

Now the question is — we are looking at a **जाति**. What is a **जाति**? What is a **जाति**? If you look at the world — you're trying to know the world. You are trying to understand the reality of the world by observing the world. You're a scientist, okay? You're observing the world. How do you look at the world? This is the **नैयायिक**s and **वैशेषिक**s — you have to give them marks for doing this well. They have tried very hard. So they will say — you cannot say — when you look at a person, you look at **यज्ञदत्त** or **देवदत्त**. **देवदत्त** means the name, okay — general names, like John. You look at **देवदत्त**. Now, is **देवदत्त** and human being — are they two equal? Are the two words same? No. What is the relationship between **देवदत्त** and human being? **देवदत्त** is human being. Human being is not **देवदत्त** alone. Correct? John also comes. No trouble. Therefore there is a **व्यक्ति**, an individual, and there is a **जाति** — a species, a grouping, a category — which is different from the individual. Yes? When you know what's a tree — any tree you recognize. Mango tree, apple tree, pear tree — you recognize. Therefore this tree is different from individual trees. All right?

And further — so the point is, I am looking at the world, and in my world view there is — what is world view? I should be able to see it, I should be able to objectify. That alone is. Correct? And within that I can do further, you know, subclassifications. I can work with those. Right? So these people said — the word interestingly used — **पदार्थ**. There are only seven **पदार्थ**s. Anything you know must have a word to communicate — there must be a word for it. **पद** and **अर्थ**. **पद** means a word, **अर्थ** means its meaning. All right.

Now among their **पदार्थ**s they talk of something called **सामान्य**. They have seven categories. They are trying to understand the world. You cannot understand the world by looking at one, one, one, one, one — how many will you count? So the method is what? You group. You classify. So in the classification, one of the classifications is **सामान्य**. What is a **सामान्य**? That which inheres in other many. It has to inhere in many. So tree-ness inheres in many trees. Human-ness inheres in many human beings. Yeah? **सामान्य**.

Now, it is this **सामान्य**, he says, they look upon as saying there is something called **परसामान्य**, **अपरसामान्य**, **परापरसामान्य**. There is a **सामान्य** which is **परसामान्य**.
