oṃ śrutismṛtipurāṇānām ālayaṃ karuṇālayam| namāmi bhagavatpādaṃ śaṅkaraṃ lokaśaṅkaram|| śaṅkaraṃ śaṅkarācāryaṃ keśavaṃ bādarāyaṇam| sūtrabhāṣyakṛtau vande bhagavantau punaḥ punaḥ|| īśvaro gururātmeti mūrtibhedavibhāgine| vyomavadvāptadehāya dakṣiṇāmūrtaye namaḥ|| oṃ saha nāvavatu| saha nau bhunaktu| saha vīryaṃ karavāvahai| tejasvināvad hītamastu mā vidviṣāvahai|| oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ|| oṃ namo brahmādibhyo brahmavidyāsaṃpradāyakṛtbhyo vaṃśaṛṣibhyo namo gurubhyaḥ||

We are looking at the **bhāṣyam** of the fifth **kaṇḍikā**, the fourth **brāhmaṇa** — the **maitreyī** **brāhmaṇa** of **bṛhadāraṇyaka**| So what was presented is — **maitreyī** said, don't give me wealth, give me real wealth. The wealth you are after, the freedom you are after — what will you give it to me? Then he invited her to come and listen — I will teach you, you listen. He started with what? — A wife is not loved, you know, for the wife's sake; she is loved for one's own sake. That's from the husband's side. A husband is not loved for the husband's sake; he is loved for one's own sake, for the wife's own sake. A child, a progeny, is not loved for the progeny's sake; the progeny is loved for one's own sake. Wealth is not loved for the sake of wealth; wealth is loved for one's own sake. **lokāḥ**, **vedāḥ** — anything you love is not for that. You see it as a means for you to be pleased. Therefore, what you are really interested in, what you really love, is the pleased you.

Okay? So what? Therefore, that's what is worth understanding. What is the nature of the pleased you? I know the nature. If you knew, I wouldn't tell you — you have to know it. If you already know, **śruti** has no job. **śruti** is a **pramāṇam**| It has a job only for **ajñātajñāpanam** — **pramāṇa** **lakṣaṇam** - **ajñātajñāpitam** what is not known is made known by which, that is called a **pramāṇam**| And **śruti** is a deliberate **pramāṇam**| Deliberate means — it's not something you just get; it's deliberate in the sense that every word must have a meaning. What do I mean by that? My ears hear a lot of things; many of them I ignore, they're useless to me. Most of them are useless, very few things are really useful. Same thing — you see a lot of things, very few things are really required by you. But when it comes to **śāstra**, you cannot say many of them are useless. Every word in the **śāstra** must be meaningful — **prayojanavattvam**| That **prayojanavattvam** is the main important factor. It should serve a purpose. Otherwise it becomes purposeless — **pramāṇāntara**-**anadhigatam** and should be **prayojanavat**. Therefore, sometimes we say **arthavadbodhakatvam** — we shorten it, **arthavat**; **arthaḥ** is **prayojanam**, not "meaning" necessarily. So it should serve a purpose.

Therefore, if **śruti** says **ātmā** **vā** **are** **draṣṭavyaḥ**, what does it indicate? What you think you know about yourself is still not really known — not adequately known. Yes, body is you — true, we are not questioning that — not enough, not adequately known. **asamagrā** **vidyā** is **avidyā**| Therefore, **ātmā** **vā** **are** **draṣṭavyaḥ** — **ātmā** should be seen. How? How to see? Because I am, I know I am — "I am a useless guy, I know." No, that's not true. How will you know? **śrotavyaḥ** — it has to be listened to. What has to be listened to? What is you — the determination has to be listened to. How is the listening to be done? Listening is to be done with a view to understand what is the **śāstra** trying to convey to me about myself. What is this **tātparyam** of the **śāstra**? **śruti**-**tātparyam** **kim**? It wants to convey something, and what it wants to convey I want to grasp. I am not interested in going with a conclusion and finding things to support my conclusion. Very, very big difference it makes. If I go into the **śāstra** looking for something to support my own conclusion, then **śāstra** is not a **pramāṇam**| **tarka**, my own logic, becomes **pradhāna**, and the **śāstra** becomes secondary. You want the **śāstra** right, Ok, I will give you one - support what I have to say. I have already decided.

It's like the modern business houses. If they want to do something, it's not uncommon that you hire a high-priced consulting firm to study the problem and come up with recommendations. And a smart consulting firm knows that you should never give an advice which is not sought. So what should you do? Find out what does the fellow really want. He wants to lay off 10,000 people. Okay. So you spend one month studying — don't give it just like that. And then you come up with your research conclusion — recommendation is 10,000 people should be laid off. Now the management will say, "this is what Anderson Consulting has to tell us, and therefore, you know, we have no choice but to lay off people — we are very objective." The fact is they already know what they want to do, and they find a consultant whom they'll pay and he will scratch their back for them. This is not uncommon. This is how the world works.

Same way, if you approach the **śruti** already saying, "I know what I want to conclude" — now I will say **annamayaḥ** **ātmā**, I told you body is **ātmā**, look, **śruti** also says **ātmā** is **annamayaḥ**| Hey — what's the next line after that? Did you read? **anyo'ntara** **ātmā** **prāṇamayaḥ**| **śruti** is saying **annamayaḥ** **ātmā** — I don't want to go further, because that's where I want to stop. This is not **śravaṇam**| What is **śravaṇam**? **śravaṇam** is listening to the **śāstra**, keeping the **śāstra**-**artha**-**nirṇaya** basis in view. It is the **ṣaḍliṅga** — **upakrama**, **upasaṃhāra**, **abhyāsa**, **prayojana** — all these have to be kept in mind. **upapatti** also. But keeping everything in mind, what is **śāstra**-**tātparyam** has to be ascertained. And what is **śravaṇam** here further? It is that **tātparyam** which leads you to see that **ātmā** is **jagatkāraṇaṃ** **brahma** **eva**| That kind of a listening is called **śravaṇam**|

All right — I listened, the **śāstra** told. I understand the **śāstra** is saying everything is **brahman** and **tattvamasi**| I understand. But how can it be? How can it be? **śaṅkara** himself says — even if ten **śāstra**s, a thousand **śāstra**s come and say "**agni** is cold to touch," what should you do? You cannot accept the **śāstra**; you have to find another meaning for the **śāstra**| You cannot say that this part of the **śāstra** will be rejected, the rest of it you keep — that is called **ardhajaratīya** **nyāya**| You cannot have half an egg for hatching and half an egg for eating. Doesn't work that way. Either the whole **śāstra** is real or the whole **śāstra** is not. You cannot take a piecemeal approach. This is what a lot of times we do — you quote the **śāstra** where it suits you, and you forget the **śāstra** where it doesn't suit you, which means you become an opportunist even with the **śāstra**| No — the whole **śāstra** is **pramāṇam**| Therefore you understood.

Now the problem is what? I understand what the **śāstra** says, I can't agree. If that is so, then what is the next part? **mantavyaḥ**| Then **mantavyaḥ** — so **mananam** **kartavya**:| That is meant to do what? Remove my doubts, my conclusions which stand against what the **śāstra** is revealing. That's the purpose of **mananam**| And even after I do that — okay, I have no reason by which I can deny what the **śāstra** is saying, but I still can't go with it; my orientation is extremely strong, "this much alone I am" — then what? Then **nididhyāsitavyaḥ**| So it is one teaching. **ātmā** **vā** **are** **draṣṭavyaḥ** is one teaching, and for that the method of understanding is threefold — not three different means, threefold. **śravaṇam** alone is the **aṅgī**| **manana**-**nididhyāsana** are the **aṅgāḥ** — which means **manana** and **nididhyāsana** supplement **śravaṇam**, they don't stand independently. **tātparya**-**niścaya** is the goal. All right — this is what is **ātmā** **vā** **are** **śrotavyaḥ**, **mantavyaḥ**, **nididhyāsitavyaḥ**|

Let's go back to the next. **tasmāt** **ātmā** **vā** **are** **draṣṭavyaḥ** **darśanārhaḥ**, **darśanaviṣayam** **āpādayitavyaḥ**; **śrotavyaḥ** **pūrvam** **ācāryataḥ** - from **ācārya**; and **āgamataśca** - in keeping with the **śāstra** ; **paścāt** - after that **mantavyaḥ** **tarkataḥ** - by logic; **tato** - after that **nididhyāsitavyaḥ** **niścayena** **dhyātavyaḥ** - with clear understanding you dwell, you abide in it; **evaṃ** **hi** **asau** **dṛṣṭo** **bhavati** - in this way alone it comes to be known; **śravaṇa**-**manana**-**nididhyāsana**-**sādhanaiḥ** **nirvartitaiḥ** - all these must be used as needed not only one; **yadā** **ekatvam** **etāni** **upagatāni** - when all these three becomes one means of knowledge to you and understand that they are not three different things, **tadā** **samyagdarśanaṃ** **brahmaikatva**-**viṣayaṃ** **prasīdati** — only then, basically when you have understood **śāstra**-**tātparyam**, removed the objections you have in your mind against it, and you have removed the habitual wrong thinking from your head, then alone there is abidance — not just by listening - **iti** "the **śāstra** said.” Then

yat brahmakṣatrādi karmanimittaṃ varṇāśramādilakṣaṇam ātmanyavidyādhyāropitapratyayaviṣayaṃ kriyākārakaphalātmakam avidyāpratyayaviṣayam — rajjvāmiva sarpapratyayaḥ, tadupamardanārthamāha;

Now he is introducing the next sentence. What is the next sentence in the **śruti**? It is, after **nididhyāsitavyaḥ** — **maitreyi** **ātmano** **vā** **are** **darśanena** **śravaṇena** **matyā** **vijñānena** **idaṃ** **sarvaṃ** **viditam**| What does it say? Hey **maitreyi**, **ātmano** **vā** **are** **darśanena** — only by seeing clearly what **ātmā** is. **draṣṭavyaḥ** was the **vidhi**, was the teaching — it should be seen. Now what has happened after learning? **darśanam** has happened. You are seeing it clearly. You can't miss it, because all you see is that. Therefore, **ātmano** **vai** **are** **darśanena**| And how do you get that **darśanam**? **śravaṇena** — by listening. **matyā** — by doing **mananam**| **vijñānena** — by doing **nididhyāsana**| **sarvaṃ** **viditam**

“Hey I came to know that I want to be the pleased self — why are you bringing **sarvaṃ** **viditam** here? What is the correlation? I let the world go to dogs, I don't care, I want to be happy." It doesn't work that way, because you see, all these seem to somehow connect to you. And even if you say "no, no, nothing bothers me," they will bother you — if not now, they'll bother you after five minutes, just wait. So the question is — if there are other things as true as I am, as real as I am, there is no way that I can abide in freedom. If other things are there **dvaitād** **bhayaṃ** **bhavati|** If a second thing is there, it means you are going to be limited — in space you will be limited, in capacity you will be limited; somewhere that fellow will have overriding authority. You cannot have total freedom. Therefore, what is required for you to be always happy — to be the pleased self? Nothing should come between you and the pleased self. If nothing should come, everything should be under my control — that's what we normally think. Nothing is in my control. Therefore then, what should be done? Suppose there is nothing at all that goes against you — if nothing goes against you, then it's there but it's aligned with your likes and dislikes. But what is the fact? They are not aligned. In fact, even if somebody tries to align with my likes and dislikes, my likes and dislikes themselves are constantly changing. What I wanted one year ago is not what I want today. In fact, what I ate two days ago is not what I want today — I'm bored with it, I want something different. You saw a movie, you liked it very much — come on, let's go for it every day! For how many days? You say "enough." Anything you like, you like it only till you like it; then you say enough. And you said "I don't like this at all, I don't like this" — then after some days you developed a liking for it, now you like it. How can anybody satisfy you? Therefore, if there is a second thing, it's impossible to be pleased, to abide in that pleased self. Therefore, what's required?

And moreover, the question is this — even after **mananam**, a question will come; typically it will be: "But what about all this? But what about **karma**?" So many questions come in. What is the place of everything? If everything is myself, why is **jagat** created? The mind never rests. Therefore he is saying — look, by this what will happen? **sarvaṃ** **viditaṃ** **syāt**| Hey — I thought you said **ātmā** **vā** **are** **draṣṭavya**: - **ātmā** should be known? By knowing **ātmā**, suppose you know — I ask you, what is this? This is a mouse. Good. Now, by that, do you know what this is (now Swamiji is showing a pen)? Not because of that (because of mouse). You may know what this is (pen), but not because you know what that is (mouse). By knowing one thing, how can other things be known? By knowing one thing you know one thing. Therefore, if you know **ātmā**, what should you know? You should know **ātmā**| Therefore, if by knowing **ātmā** everything has to become known, what does it mean? **ātmā** is everything. See how it is being said subtly — the **mahāvākya** is sitting there. **ātmano** **darśanena** **sarvaṃ** **viditaṃ** **syāt** — **katham**? Because **sarvaṃ** **khalu** **ātmā** **eva**| That is what is being said.

So he is saying — look, now the **bhāṣyakāra** wants to introduce this sentence. Before the sentence comes, what does he do? He says:

yat brahma-kṣatrādi-karma-nimittaṃ varṇāśramādi-lakṣaṇam ātmani avidyā-adhyāropita-pratyaya-viṣayaṃ kriyā-kāraka-phalātmakam avidyā-pratyaya-viṣayam — rajjvām iva sarpa-pratyayaḥ, tadupamardanārtham āha|

See, what are all these now? Finally, what do we want? **kriyā**-**kāraka**-**phalātmakam** — my life is about **phalam**| Something which is not there today, I want to gain. **phalam** means what is gained. And what do you need for **phalam**? You need **kriyā**| **kriyā** is action — because what is not there, I want to gain; to gain what is not, I have to do something. **karmaṇā** **aprāptasya** **prāptiḥ** **karmaṇā**| Therefore, for **karma**-**kriyā**, what do you need? **kārakāḥ** — factors of action. You need **karaṇa**, you need **adhikaraṇa**, you need all the **kārakāḥ**| Therefore, my life is all about what? **kriyā**, **kāraka**, **phalam**| That is my life.

And who can do what **kriyā**? Depends on who I am, where do I stand in society, what is my role, what is my position. That, in the **veda**, was presented as what? Your **varṇa** and **āśrama**| **brahma**-**kṣatrādi** — **brahma** is **brāhmaṇa**, **kṣatra** is **kṣatriya** — bring everything else, you bring **varṇa**, you bring **āśrama**, you bring **deśa**, you bring **kāla** — everything is **ādipadāt** to always determine what should be done by you. "Swamiji, no **varṇa**-**āśrama** today." Fine — you still have an identification of your role, your place in the scheme of things; you take yourself to be somebody. Suppose you take yourself to be a very important director of Microsoft — then you will work for Microsoft's gain, not for Apple's gain. You will have a **svakarma** even there, you understand? Any role you take, you will know what is good for you in your own way. Therefore, depending on your own identification, that is **brahma**-**kṣatrādi**-**karma**-**nimittam** — they have duties to be done and that is what - that **karma**-**nimittam**; So to do a **karma**, to do something I need to know where do I stand? That is **varṇāśramādi**-**lakṣaṇam**, characterized by what is my **varṇa**, what is my **āśrama**| **kriyā**-**kāraka**-**phalātmakam**

What is all this? It is **ātmani** **avidyā**-**adhyāropita**-**pratyaya**-**viṣayam**|**śaṅkara** says - **pratyaya** is a thought; **viṣaya** is the subject matter — it is the subject matter of a thought. How did that thought come? **adhyāropitam** —you imagined something there, you projected something there. How? **avidyayā** **adhyāropitam**| **avidyā** about what? You say there is **avidyā** — **avidyā** is about what? You do not know what? What is covered? **ātmā**| Therefore, **ātmani** **avidyā**-**adhyāropita**-**pratyaya**-**viṣayam**| And what is that? **kriyā**-**kāraka**-**phalātmakam** — **ahaṃ** **kartā**, **ahaṃ** **bhoktā** — I am a doer, I am an experiencer, I am going to gain the result by doing this **karma**, I am going to become more significant in life, more significant in the world. This is what? **adhyāropitam** — because of what? not knowing yourself, **ātmani** **adhyāropitam**| Therefore the whole thing is what? **avidyā**-**pratyaya**-**viṣayam** — it is all not knowledge, it is ignorance-based; it's not knowing the truth, because of which I see things as they are. Like what? **rajjvām** **iva** **sarpa**-**pratyayaḥ** ; The whole **jagat** that you see is different from you — it's like seeing a snake where there is only a rope. How do you see a snake? Can you explain how you see a snake? Can you explain anytime how you made an error? You can try to explain, but you cannot explain an error — because the error was knowledge for you. An error can only be corrected; if the error is known as error, that is correction — after the correction. So here, **rajjvām** **iva** **sarpa**-**pratyayaḥ**|

So the whole problem is **avidyā** — **avidyā** of what? **ātma**-**avidyā**| What is **ātma**-**avidyā**? That you think you stand apart from the whole world as a separate, isolated entity — that is **avidyā**| Therefore that's what makes me feel small and insignificant. Because if I am this much, what I am not is far, far, far more than what I am. So anybody who is sane, who sees this much as "me," cannot but be threatened, cannot but feel small — logical. Therefore the only solution — if this is real, your total freedom from a sense of smallness is a pipe dream, an impossibility. Therefore what should I do — if this knowledge has to sink home what is the only way? It cannot be wishful thinking; it must be the truth — **yathārtha**-**jñānam**| Therefore I should show you that **ātmā** **eva** **idaṃ** **sarvam**| Then you don't get into this mode of **phalam** — you want something to become different, you want something to become more significant. There is nothing else, everything is you. Then what will you look for? **kimicchankasya** **kāmāya** **śarīramanusañcaret** | One for whom everything is oneself — what would you do, wishing for what? There is nothing else, no **phala**, no **kāraka** — everything is made chutney, **upamardita**| So for that reason **tadupamardanārtham** **āha** in order to completely dismantle this thinking that "I am different from things in the world, and I will become more significant by aligning the world, by gaining something, by getting something" — to dismantle this completely, what is said? **ātmani** **khalu** **are** **maitreyi** **dṛṣṭe** **śrute** **mate** **vijñāte** **idaṃ** **sarvaṃ** **viditaṃ** **vijñātaṃ** **bhavati**|

If you know yourself — all **sati** **saptamī|** **ātmani** **dṛṣṭe** **śrute** **mate** **vijñāte** **sati|** **śrute** **sati** — when it's heard, then there are doubts. **mate** **sati** — remove doubts, logical doubts. Then still habitual errors are there — that also removed - **vijñāte** **sati** **idaṃ** **sarvam** — the whole thing which you call **idaṃ**, **idaṃ**, **idam**, "this." svato'khilo'priyo'nātmā pratyaṅmohaikahetutaḥ| pratyagāhlādakāritvādapriyo'pi priyo mataḥ॥ 79 ॥

**svataḥ** **akhilaḥ** **apriyaḥ** **anātmā** — by itself, without you backing it with your wrong thinking — **svataḥ**, by itself, anything — **akhilaḥ**, without any exception; **akhila** is "remaining," **akhila** is without exception — **svataḥ** **akhilaḥ** **apriyaḥ**; **kim** **apriyaḥ**? **anātmā** **apriyaḥ**. **svataḥ** **akhilaḥ** **anātmā** **apriyaḥ** — anything other than you, by itself, has no way to become dear to you. It is **apriya**. Okay? **pratyaṅmohaikahetutaḥ**. Let's put it this way — any **anātmā** which is not dear to you, why is it not dear to you? **pratyaṅmohaikahetutaḥ**. **pratyak** means inner. Inner — you keep going inner. What does inner mean? Not outer. What is outer? What can be objectified. Anything that can be objectified is outer. What is innermost? What can never be objectified. See, "inner" can give you a spatial feeling — you will try to find **ātmā** inside now. "So there is this body; in the body there is one heart; in that heart there is a little space; in that space is **ātmā**." Sounds very nice. **hṛdaya**-**deha**-**madhye**, **hṛdaya**-**kohara**-**madhye**, **kevalam** **aham** **aham** **iti** — this is **upāsanā**-**arthe**, **hṛdayaṃ** **sthānam** — that is not being questioned; but if you think **ātmā** is confined to a space in your heart, you are off. So he says, **pratyak** means **ātmā**. **pratyaṅmohaikahetutaḥ** — on account of not knowing who you are, something is not dear to you, something is dear to you. **pratyagāhlādakāritvāt** — and this **ātmā**, something makes it happy **āhlādakāri**. **āhlāda** is happiness — **āhlāda** all the way, **āhlādakārī**. **pratyagāhlādakārī** means what pleases me; on account of pleasing me — **pratyagāhlādakāritvāt** **apriyo'pi** **priyo** **mataḥ**. By itself, nothing is really **priya**; nothing which is **anātmā** is **priya**, because it is not.

See, what is **anātmā**? Another thing you can think of, right? **anātmā** is "not us," basically. But what is it that you care for? Do you care for something which is totally unconnected to you? A thing which is totally unconnected to you can neither be a source of pleasure nor a source of pain — nothing, it's zero. Therefore, what bothers you has become — what? — first connected to **ātmā** in some way, what you take yourself to be. That is called **ātma**-**śeṣa**. **śeṣa** means connected. How? **śeṣe** **ṣaṣṭhī**, not **kāraka**-**sambandha**. See grammar, how it connects — it's called **śeṣa**. So **ātma**-**śeṣa** means what? Not something other than **ātmā**. **ātma**-**śeṣa** means what is connected to you. What is unconnected to you cannot be pleasing or displeasing. What's connected to you — if you say it makes me pleased, it becomes dear to you; and if you say it doesn't please me, it becomes undear to you. Therefore, what is the **hetu**? Who is saying it's dear or undear? That is **rakta**-**dviṣṭa**-**manaḥ**. Therefore, this is what is driving your **priya**-**apriya**. **ātmā** in itself is neither **priya** nor **apriya**. See, I am changing it slightly — I am saying **ātmā** itself is neither **priya** nor **apriya**. I can say that way also. Or I can say **ātmā** alone is **priya** — not **priya** because of **rāga**; **priya** irrespective of **rāga**. That's important here. **pratyagāhlādakāritvāt** **apriyo'pi** **priyo** **mataḥ** - **apriyo'pi** **priyāyate** .

So, what is not dear can become dear for your own sake. Till now I didn't like **jāmuna** at all. **jāmuna** — what **jāmuna**? Not **gulāba** **jāmuna**, okay — the **jāmuna** which grows on trees, that **jāmuna**, the fruit. It was never dear. Then suddenly you discover you have sugar. And then someone tells you, "**jāmuna** is very good for sugar." Up till now, it's got a kind of astringent taste, that **jāmuna**. Now suddenly you find it so lovable. **karelā** you never liked. Now you have to eat **karelā** — **karelā** juice is going to give you some health. And over time you develop a liking for it, slight liking. And then you discover — my God, just by eating **jāmuna**, and taking that seed of that **jāmuna**, making it powder and consuming it, my diabetes is improving. "I love it” **apriyo'pi** **priyāyate**. It becomes **priya**. Why? Because you want to be free from diabetes. What is dear to you? Freedom from diabetes. Therefore, when am I pleased? When I — who is free from diabetes — am a pleased I. When I see HbA1c, it says five, instead of nine, I feel very good. Six is the norm, and it's down to five from nine. Wow — **priya**. I have become **priya** to myself now. Now I am a healthy person. So what made the **apriya** into **priya**? **pratyagāhlādakāritvāt**. What pleases you is dear to you.

jāyādirapriyaḥ sarvaḥ svata ādyantaduḥkhakṛt| taddhetorātmanaḥ prīterjāyādiḥ priya ucyate॥ 80 ॥

So he says — **jāyādiḥ** - **jāyā** is wife; **jāyādiḥ** - **jāyā**, **pati**, **santāna**, **vitta** whatever you want to bring in, everything, is **priya**. **sarvaḥ**. Whatever is dear to — really what? **svataḥ** **ādyantaduḥkhakṛt**. Everything is having a beginning and an end. Something is yet to begin — okay? What is yet to begin? Monsoon is yet to begin. Heat — hot, hot, hot. Monsoon is yet to begin, so wait for it to begin; till it begins, you are **duḥkhī**. Why is it delayed this time? Monsoon — they said it will come earlier, 26th May, it hasn't yet come. Why is it delayed? So hot. So it is **ādyanta** — it had a beginning. And monsoon came; it seemed like it came, five days it rained in Goa heavily, then it went away — that is called, it went away because some systems weakened, apparently. But what happened? **duḥkha** again — we are sweating. **duḥkhakṛt**, **ādyantaduḥkhakṛt**. Anything which is loved by you, like a wife, a husband, wealth, anything, is all in time — beginning and end. **taddhetoḥ** **ātmanaḥ** **prīteḥ** **jāyādiḥ** **priya** **ucyate** — therefore, understand, what you really love is the pleased self; all these somehow seem to make the pleased self come out — otherwise they are **duḥkhakṛt**.
